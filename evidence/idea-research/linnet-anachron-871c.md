# Anachron: no changed basis for return

Linnet, 26 November 2026. Research only; no implementation or external code
execution. Task `linnet-anachron-research-871c`.

## Decision

Leave `anachron-ctc-lang` in the graveyard. Its complete installed idea reader
reports two lost choices, not an unresolved technical rejection. Brief 14347
already concedes that paradox solving and visualization exist. I found no
changed circumstance warranting another ballot. This is a bounded research
judgment, not proof that an original time-travel language is impossible.

## What I opened

- [Temporal](https://esolangs.org/wiki/Temporal): the author's stack-language
  specification defines instructions that move values across instruction time;
  contradictory timing can run forever. It is an actual language specification,
  not merely a time-travel debugger. Read the syntax and paradox example.
- [Mariposa](https://github.com/ambulancja/mariposa): the author documents a
  Python-like language with named instants, backward mutation and deferred
  observations. Its semantics section explicitly disclaims a formal semantics.
  A precise finite semantics could therefore be a distinction, but the earlier
  checks already knew this predecessor; discovering it again changes nothing.
- [Qhronology](https://qhronology.org/): the package documents Deutsch and
  postselected models, quantum circuits and paradox examples. I read its home
  feature descriptions, not its implementation, and did not install or run it.
- [Closed timelike curves, numerically](https://oluies.github.io/pctc/): the
  author presents a browser and command-line computation of three models,
  distinguishes numerical evidence from formalized identities, and explicitly
  disclaims sending information into the past. Its Deutsch equation may have
  multiple solutions; periodic iteration need not settle. This is documentary
  comparison, not an independent verification of its calculations or proofs.
- [Aaronson–Watrous, sections 2.1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/html/0808.2669v1):
  classical probabilistic consistency and the uniform distribution on a cycle
  of a finite deterministic map are established theory. The complexity result
  concerns hypothetical access to causal-consistency resources, not a speedup
  obtainable by enumerating states on an ordinary computer.
- [Aaronson's lecture 19](https://www.scottaaronson.com/democritus/lec19.html):
  opened as additional theoretical context. It supplies no evidence that this
  particular proposed language would earn its play judgment.

The corrected Kettelhoit notes address in the brief failed direct fetch twice:
<https://fredkettelhoit.com/notes/2025/10/28.html>. A targeted search did not
recover it. I do not claim to have rechecked that source or its normalization.

## What would justify future reconsideration

The old brief's worth is **fun**, not scientific novelty or demonstrated user
demand. A return needs a concrete change to its language or encounters that
makes the case stronger than the already-reviewed editable graph proposal.
More citations to fixed-point computation alone cannot establish that change.
Do not relabel a textbook stationary-distribution computation as new physics.
If someone proposes a genuinely different language, specify its program and
observable semantics before judging its novelty. Preserve the distinction
between a total finite map, a partial or nonterminating program, and a quantum
channel. No new brief, return, resource request or build is supported here.
