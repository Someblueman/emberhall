## Comparison against the exact frozen 5180 milestones

- **Shadow Theatre — chosen.** One finite-screen model: a point light, at most
  three opaque vertical segments strictly between source and screen, and
  interval-union projection. Three original solvable challenges plus an
  editable sandbox; keyboard controls and text interval output; no automatic
  motion. The numeric core is analytically testable without a browser:
  endpoint projections, overlapping, touching and disjoint intervals, clipping,
  forbidden source and prop positions, repeated results, and lossless challenge
  import/export are all checkable against hand-derived expectations. The
  milestone also keeps the human claim honest by requiring a non-author to
  predict an unseen case before reveal and to solve and explain a challenge,
  with that observation retained separately from numerical correctness. I
  choose it because it gives visitors something to walk up to and try — the
  kind of earned, visible house growth the owner has repeatedly asked for —
  while its correctness does not depend on visual taste. It also moves the
  witnessed prediction-before-reveal practice from Signal Garden to a different
  spatial model. The milestone explicitly excludes refraction, mirrors,
  penumbra and 3D, so the first step is genuinely bounded.
- **Pocket Press — second.** A real give-away artifact: eight text-and-simple-
  shape pages, reading order beside an imposed A4 or Letter sheet, a reversible
  JSON project, a labelled fold/cut guide, and atomic rejection of malformed
  imports. Its page/rotation mapping is independently testable, and a short
  story or workshop booklet is good outside value. I place it second because
  its acceptance leans on browser print preview, and the milestone itself says
  a physical fold remains explicitly unwitnessed; the visible result could
  therefore stay partly unproven. That is a risk judgment, not a claim that the
  editor is weak.
- **Tool Bench — third.** A concrete small-group lending desk: catalogue,
  check-out/return, due and overdue history, printable tag sheet, and a JSON
  loan log a group can audit and sync by file. The loan transitions,
  double-lend rejection and malformed import are cleanly testable. It is third
  because the shape is a familiar kiosk and its acceptance is a broad browser
  trial (create, return, reimport, reject an invalid import, inspect the tag
  sheet) rather than one narrow walk-up puzzle.
- **Cue Rehearsal — fourth.** The causal comparison is useful and distinct from
  the completed handover-record checker: precedence-only earliest times, delay
  propagation, cycles, missing IDs, resource blocks and overlapping resource
  intervals, with baseline and one cloned scenario preserved side by side. It
  is fourth because maintaining a dependency graph may cost more than it saves
  for a small event, and its output overlaps existing rundown and scheduling
  tools; the milestone's honest "do not auto-schedule" boundary is right, but
  the first-step value is less visible to a visitor.
- **Hearth Focus — fifth.** A configurable focus timer with an exported-prefix
  view of awake residents and live claims, overlap warnings, localStorage and
  JSON export is a reasonable tool. It is fifth because it overlaps existing
  claim, digest and status views, and the milestone is explicit that a stale
  export warns rather than protects; it would add a convenience, not a new
  kind of house capability.
