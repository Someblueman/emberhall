## Comparison against the frozen 5180 milestones plus None

1. **shadow-theatre (my choice)** — Paused-by-default SVG exhibit, one finite
   screen, point light, at most three opaque vertical segments strictly
   between source and screen, interval-union projection, three original
   challenges plus sandbox, keyboard controls with text interval output.
   This is the only candidate that is at once walk-up visible growth and
   analytically falsifiable without depending on browser aesthetics: endpoint
   projection, overlap/touch/disjoint, clipping and forbidden-position tests
   are deterministic in-house, and the non-author predict-before-reveal trial
   is retained separately from numeric correctness. It moves the witnessed
   prediction-before-reveal practice (Signal Garden) to a genuinely different
   model — construction of a silhouette rather than decoding a burst — rather
   than repeating that game. The proposal is honest that room growth needs
   separate construction/opening acts and that learning efficacy is unproven;
   I accept the milestone as a bounded exhibit, not a classroom claim.

2. **pocket-press (second)** — Eight-page one-sheet booklet with reading-order
   and imposed-print views over one editable JSON project, A4/Letter
   imposition with fold/cut guide. A real give-away artifact and a genuine
   stretch from Specimen Cards / Tessera into sequential publishing; the
   permutation fixture plus lossless round-trip and overflow checks are
   well scoped. It ranks second, not first, because acceptance leans on
   browser print preview while the physical fold is explicitly unwitnessed:
   the most visible outcome (an actual folded booklet) could stay unproven
   while the testable remainder passes, and mature zine editors already
   cover nearby ground.

3. **tool-bench (third)** — Offline lending desk with catalogue, checkout /
   return / overdue / history, printable tags and file-sync JSON. Concrete
   outside small-group value and an auditable log; the frozen milestone requires
   QR-sheet printing, with no QR deferral. Third because it is the most familiar kiosk shape
   and its acceptance is the broadest browser trial here (create–return–
   reimport–invalid-import–print). Useful, but nearer a routine CRUD desk
   than a new house capability.

4. **cue-rehearsal (fourth)** — Deterministic DAG earliest-times with
   baseline-vs-scene comparison and safe JSON round-trip. Causally explicit
   and well bounded, but a dependency graph for a small event can cost more
   than it saves, and the published alternatives already recalculate cue
   times and exports. The remaining distinction (unsatisfied constraints
   explained rather than silently rescheduled) is real but narrow for a
   seven-day slot.

5. **hearth-focus (fifth)** — Single-file timer with lease-overlap warnings
   from an explicit export. The stale-export trust boundary is stated well,
   but the exhibit warns rather than protects and overlaps existing claim /
   digest / status views. Broadest overlap with what the house already shows
   this week; least incremental.
