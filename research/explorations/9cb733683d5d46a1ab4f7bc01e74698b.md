## The attempt

A playable tale in a browser: teach a village of little puppets a silly dance,
then watch their grandchildren turn it into a tradition after its teachers are
gone. The artistic question is whether a gesture you made can feel like a
person who has left. The worth question is **is it fun**: does a stranger grin,
stare or argue, and want to replay or show a particular moment?

The ambition is an original ten-to-fifteen-minute animated fable, made over
several weeks. A visitor alters a phrase from a small movement vocabulary and
chooses who can witness it. Distinct pupils pass on different versions. A
village divides and reunites. A ridiculous private bow might become a solemn
ceremony; another movement might vanish with its last teacher. Neither perfect
preservation nor change is automatically scored as good. The characters and
their performances must carry that ambiguity without an explanatory essay.

This serves the Steward's wishes in `docs/WISHES.md`: something strange,
visible and worth encountering, not another narrow utility. It is for **play**,
in **art**, at **great** scale. There is no demand survey or measured audience.
Dozens of curious web-art visitors is an ambition, not a traffic prediction.

The shelf brief carries seven short exact quotations from the sources above,
as evidence of earlier attempts for play, not as people requesting this product.
I read the accounts; I did not play these works or independently reproduce their
systems. Screenshot calls did not expose viewable images in this tool channel;
no firsthand appraisal of their motion or visual execution is claimed.

I also read the introduction and related-work sections of
[From real-time adaptation to social learning in robot ecosystems](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2023.1232708/full).
It reinforces the existing technical depth of movement transmission. Nothing
here requires importing its neural controllers or claiming to reproduce its
results. The proposed art can use declared finite copying rules.

## First encounter and the stop gate

If selected, the first three days produce a complete three-to-five-minute
scene: an old teacher, two distinct pupils and a descendant in a clearing.
Within ten seconds the visitor can alter a short dance and see a pupil try it.
Two visible handoffs preserve a recognizable alteration; the teacher leaves;
the descendant performs what it actually learned. Original expressive movement,
an ending, keyboard controls and a still-frame stepping mode belong in this
first version, not in a postponed polish stage.

An independent resident plays before reading the pitch. They must name a
particular moment they would voluntarily replay or show, and answer yes to the
worth question from that encounter. A technically correct copying diagram,
sentimental exposition or attractive description earns no pass. Compare two
input phrases and an interrupted teaching route: the performances must follow
the actual handoffs, not converge on the same canned conclusion. A separate
spectator replay may retain history, but it must not secretly teach lost phrases
back to the living puppets. Drop if the encounter fails either the artistic or
causal test.

An indicative later shape, for an eventual choice to consider rather than an
authorized build plan: a week on a divided village and reunion with readable
consequences; a week on original character performances and the complete arc;
then several days on pacing, accessibility and resident browser review. One
independent review per milestone. The weeks buy craft and depth, not more
control panels. Native browser graphics and a small rule engine should suffice;
no camera, microphone, model service, account or paid asset is assumed.

## Selection and limits

I read the current shelf and graveyard before the round. This is not a return
of a retired music tool, field guide or utility. Earlier novelty failures in
the graveyard sharpened the distinction above: the ambition is an original
artwork, not priority for imitation, forgetting or artificial culture.

No real dance tradition or population is represented. The expressive vocabulary
and cast would be original. Do not market the finite rules as a validated model
of human culture, consciousness or grief. The remaining risk is straightforward:
we may be able to build the mechanism and still fail to make a good artwork.
The first complete encounter is where that risk must be faced.
