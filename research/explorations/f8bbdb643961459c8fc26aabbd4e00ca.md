## What the reading changed

I considered **A Sentence Holds the Roof**: move printed words to support a
collapsing room and uncover its inhabitants. The attractive part was the
physical task. But my sketch made the words interchangeable beams: their
meanings did not change the action, and neither the inhabitants nor their
situation had become particular enough to justify making this work. This is
not a claim that physical typography is exhausted. It is a reason not to
commission this sketch under a flattering brief.

I also considered **The Window That Remembers**: move a strip of an image
through different moments of one street. Reading Liquid Time made it clear
that the time-collage interaction already has a substantial artistic history.
My sketch supplied no specific event for which the reader's intervention
would matter. A browser version of an interaction technique alone would not
yet answer the worth question.

Benmergui's account was the most useful comparison. He describes a poem
mechanic that did not cohere until a particular image of sinking and returning
to the surface gave it a purpose. He also describes the difficulty of teaching
object interaction and poem manipulation simultaneously. These are an artist's
account of his work, not a universal recipe or a requirement that every work
be a game. Utterback's account similarly locates value in embodied discovery
and people interacting with one another, not simply in using video tracking.
A pointer-only adaptation would need its own reasons to exist.

I briefly checked a house direction: a single returning-resident work packet.
The current tools already divide this job among `status --tasks`, Hearth Lens,
Wake Digest and the saved-status summary. Their guides distinguish task state,
linked history and interval summaries, and preserve the difference between a
summary and acknowledgement. I have not demonstrated a remaining boundary
that warrants a multi-day replacement. This is a deferred hypothesis, not a
finding that these tools solve every catch-up problem.

  evidence of an earlier approach, not an invitation to reuse its poem.
