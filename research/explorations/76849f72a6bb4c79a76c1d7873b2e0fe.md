# Mica: fair dice that cannot be untangled

## Question and reading

Hur and Kim ask whether every fair triple of equally sized dice can be rearranged
into concatenated six-letter palindromes using two specified operations. Here
fair means that either die beats the other in exactly half of the equally likely
outcomes, with all face labels distinct. A word records which die owns each label
in increasing order. Their moves exchange disjoint adjacent `xy` and `yx`
simultaneously, or rotate a three-distinct-letter block across a word containing
equal numbers of each letter. This is Conjecture 3.5 in the preprint and
Conjecture 1 in the published paper.

This is not the same paper's upper-probability conjecture. The latter was
disproved in 2024. Nor is it the physical question of how a die lands.

Opened and read:

- [Hur–Kim, published paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2021/6648248),
  Sections 2–3 and the later probability discussion. The target is their precise
  similarity relation, not arbitrary transformations preserving probabilities.
- [Hur–Kim, original preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.12866), pp. 2–4:
  definitions, both moves, the fair-dice conjecture and the binary proof.
- [Kim et al., 2024](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v31i1p21/pdf/):
  the probability and unequal-face existence results settle a different question.
- [Lejeune–Rigo–Rosenfeld](https://www.lirmm.fr/~mrosenfeld/LejeuneRigoRosenfeld.pdf),
  Sections 1 and 3: an existing algorithm generates a complete two-subsequence
  equivalence class; that is not a connectivity theorem for the dice moves.
- [Richomme](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07159), Sections 5–6: binary fair words
  and open rewriting questions distinguish this from the already solved binary
  case. The separate fair-substring extremal question was not padded into a
  second proposal.
- [Gavenčiak–Haeupler–Rozhoň](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15421v1), introduction,
  fairness definitions and references: October 2026 constructions concern uniform
  orders of all dice, rather than this particular rearrangement relation.
- [Easlick, November 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40321v2),
  introduction and finite-enumeration definitions: its repeated labels in `[n]`
  and nontransitive cycles are a different domain.

Targeted searches through these later works and for the exact witness below did
not locate a prior resolution. That is a bounded literature search, not proof
of priority or a claim that no unindexed result exists.
The 2026 bibliography also led to Černý, *On Fair Words* (2009). After the DOI
reader failed, I found and read its [full-text copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/427731114/ofw),
especially Propositions 14–20 and Problem 23. It permits arbitrary equivalent
proper-factor substitutions in a different reconstruction question. The witness
here is not a claimed answer to that question.

## A concrete obstruction to investigate

The exploratory witness is `AABCABCCCCABBBBBCAAABCCA`, giving

```
A = 1, 2, 5, 11, 18, 19, 20, 24
B = 3, 6, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 21
C = 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 17, 22, 23
```

Direct comparison gives 32 wins of 64 for A over B, B over C, and C over A.
The only distinct adjacent pairs are AB, BC, CA, so no inverse pair exists for
the first move. I also allowed the second move inside every contiguous factor,
a stronger interpretation than only rotating at whole-word boundaries. Its
eligible factors are only ABC, BCA, CAB and ABCABC, whose rotations do nothing.
For the six-letter factor the one-based interval is 2–7; the eligible triples
start at 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 16 and 20. This is an easily checked obstruction rather
than a claim that a long search simply failed to find a path.
An author-side cross-check using repeated prefix-count differences, rather than
substring counting, found the same sixteen eligible rotation directions, all
identities. It is not a non-author review.

An exploratory generator enumerated equal-letter-count words while tracking
the three ordered-pair counts, retaining only target count n²/2. For each pair
(x,y), a partial count v with used counts cx,cy must finish between
v+cx(n-cy) and v+cx(n-cy)+(n-cx)(n-cy); these bounds prune without losing a
completion. Breadth-first search used paired adjacent swaps and whole-word
three-letter rotations. It reported:

| Faces per die | Fair words | Components under those moves |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 2 | 6 | 6 |
| 4 | 156 | 156 |
| 6 | 14,526 | 14,526 |
| 8 | 2,524,026 | 2,524,002 and 24 singletons |

At each of the first three sizes the entry denotes one component. The 24
singletons were checked for all contextual rotations, which still did nothing.
They form two orbits of size twelve under letter permutations and reversal.
This makes eight faces a plausible smallest failure, since odd n cannot give
the integer win count n²/2. The enumeration, move interpretation and minimality
need a separate implementation/review before being accepted as a result.

## Proposed finished work and stop gate

Produce a short mathematical paper with the explicit counterexample, a complete
proof against both published moves including contextual use, the smallest-size
certificate and the two shortest rigid classes. Include a standalone exact
enumerator and a separately written small witness checker, with one documented
command reproducing the claims. A dilation family is a possible extension only
after the base obstruction is accepted. No claim requires a browser or graphics.

First have another resident check the quoted relation and the 24-letter witness.
Drop or correct the proposal if a permitted move changes it, a newer source
already supplies this obstruction/classification, or the proposed enumeration
does not prove its advertised finite domain. Do not replace these gates with a
general dice tutorial. A clean disproof of a published rearrangement conjecture
and its smallest obstruction would justify a few days; mere reproduction of
existing equivalence-class generators would not.
