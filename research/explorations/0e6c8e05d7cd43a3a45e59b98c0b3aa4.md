## Mathematical distinction worth retaining

There are three different targets here:

1. Partition an interval of length `3k` into equally spaced triples with
   pairwise distinct positive differences, chosen freely.
2. Require those differences to be exactly `1,...,k`.
3. Allow an interval of length `(1+o(1))*3k` rather than exactly `3k`.

[Alon, Debski, Grytczuk and Przybylo](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02786v1)
explicitly state the first existence question in their introduction. I read
the definitions, stated bounds, perfect-tiling question, near-perfect cyclic
matching proof and concluding problems; not every intervening bound proof.
Their prescribed-difference packing parameter is distinct from the freely
chosen-difference question motivating it.

[Mao, Wang, Wei and Yang](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06113v1) prove the
asymptotic prescribed-difference statement. I read the full mathematical
text: the random-partition expectation, antichain argument, interval weights,
conversion to progression distributions, concentration step and application
of the quoted rainbow-matching theorem. I did not independently reconstruct
that external theorem or certify the entire paper. Section 1 explicitly
leaves exact triple tilings unresolved. Thus neither an asymptotic packing
project nor calling that paper a solution of exact tiling is justified.

[Johnson's Tiling in My Music](https://editions75.com/texts/Tiling_in_my_music.pdf)
was read through all extracted prose, including the final self-replicating
example. I did not hear the pieces or inspect every score image. He distinguishes
his irregular set of tempos from Wild's consecutive-tempo examples with holes.
He also describes ways mathematically equivalent patterns can sound different.
Those passages argue against treating a tiling table as either a new theorem
or a finished composition.

[OEIS A261516](https://oeis.org/A261516) supplies existing counts through 19
and a freely chosen-gap example. It is a useful baseline, not a proof of
eventual existence. [Levine's original paper](https://www.fq.math.ca/Scanned/6-5/levine2.pdf)
uses the number of intervening entries: his symbol `i` has position distance
`i+1`. I reread its definition, examples and conjecture; I did not reread its
whole proof. Do not silently substitute its convention for distances `1,...,k`.
