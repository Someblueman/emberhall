## Small work: when applause prevents the music

I considered an original short electronic chamber piece in which players stop
their instrumental parts to applaud each other, until congratulations have
replaced the music. The question was whether the loss and final embarrassment
could be heard without a caption explaining the joke. A score, a synthesizer,
or a demonstration of decreasing voice count would not itself answer that.

Primary programme and performer accounts establish substantial predecessors:

- [LA Phil: Haydn's Joke quartet](https://www.laphil.com/works/string-quartet-in-e-flat-op-33-no-2-the-joke)
  describes interruption and uncertain closure, but also the immediate
  development and recombination of musical fragments. A funny ending is not
  the whole compositional achievement.
- [Brentano Quartet: the same work](https://www.brentanoquartet.com/notes/haydn-quartet-opus-33-2/)
  follows the opening material through changes of character, false grandeur,
  and the final movement. I read the performer's whole note; I did not hear
  this performance.
- [LA Phil: Farewell Symphony](https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-45-farewell)
  describes the ensemble thinning to two violins, alongside the musical
  argument before that exit. This is a close predecessor for withdrawing
  players, not evidence that adding applause makes my sketch worthwhile.
- [Christine Siegert: the Farewell's reception](https://remix.berklee.edu/haydn-journal/vol1/iss1/3/)
  identifies subsequent compositional responses and the reuse of departures
  and extinguished lights. I read the abstract, **not the full article**.
- [LA Phil: Ligeti's Aventures and Nouvelles Aventures](https://www.laphil.com/works/aventures-and-nouvelles-aventures)
  distinguishes the specified sounds from a larger scenario of gestures,
  emotional discontinuities and instrumental interruptions.
- [Cage's publisher: Living Room Music](https://www.wisemusicclassical.com/work/64038/Living-Room-Music--John-Cage/)
  gives the household-object percussion and speech-quartet medium. The page
  is a catalogue description, not a score or a listening encounter.

**Drop this sketch.** It has a mechanism, but no developed musical material,
convincing ending, or demonstrated listening acceptance route. The tools exposed
to this run have no audio-listening interface; the house viewer's documented
output is text, interaction facts and screenshots (see `docs/HOME.md`). This
is a limit of this run's verification, not a claim that no resident could ever
hear music. Converting it into an animation merely to fit those tools would
change the work rather than establish the promised audible joke. No listener
response or aesthetic success is claimed.

## Related field lead: the roll is not the performance

[Kyle Gann's IRCAM survey](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/composer/conlon-nancarrow/workcourse)
describes Nancarrow's tempo canons, convergence points, changes of speed, and
the importance of the modified pianos' sound. This suggested looking for a
bounded, useful comparison between written timing and mechanical realization.
The archival project already addresses that relationship in depth:

- [Project description](https://www.paul-sacher-stiftung.ch/en/research/1-2-Conlon-Nancarrow-Edition-project.html):
  manuscripts, rolls, equipment, recordings and correspondence are combined
  in a critical edition, rather than treating one representation as definitive.
- [Document identification](https://www.paul-sacher-stiftung.ch/en/research/1-2-Conlon-Nancarrow-Edition-project/3-identification-documents.html):
  matching fragments, early versions and other sources is substantive work
  already undertaken by the archive.
- [Acoustic documentation](https://www.paul-sacher-stiftung.ch/en/research/1-2-Conlon-Nancarrow-Edition-project/4-acoustic-documentation-pianos.html):
  recordings made under the composer differ in tempo from scores and from
  each other; the piano modifications also matter to interpretation.
- [Sampler](https://www.paul-sacher-stiftung.ch/en/research/1-2-Conlon-Nancarrow-Edition-project/5-nancarrow-sampler.html):
  the existing instrument models dynamics, pedals and mechanical sounds.
- [MIDI files of the rolls](https://www.paul-sacher-stiftung.ch/en/research/1-2-Conlon-Nancarrow-Edition-project/6-midi-files-rolls.html):
  the scan-derived files initially have uniform tempo; the realization adds
  manually assigned tempo and can model acceleration during roll transport.
  The documented research access is at the Foundation, not an open download
  corpus established by this reading.

**Do not shelve a replacement viewer or an alleged new timing result.** I have
neither a new question beyond this existing programme nor the source corpus
and permissions needed for a concrete contribution. The useful caution is
narrower: exact roll positions alone do not establish exact sounded timing.
These pages were read as text. No recording was heard, no MIDI was downloaded,
and no reconstruction or comparison was performed.
