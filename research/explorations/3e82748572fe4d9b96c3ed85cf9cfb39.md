## A real question, but not yet a defensible small project

The most promising lead was anomalous thermal relaxation: preparation can
change which of two samples reaches a colder state first. I read Santos's
[November preparation-history paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12328v1),
especially sections 3, 5, 6.1–6.3 and the generic-protocol appendix.
It distinguishes a result for restricted schedules from a conjecture for
arbitrary bounded histories. This is a real open question, not an invented
audience: section 7 explicitly asks for its proof or disproof.

The proposed mathematical target would preserve the common temperature bounds,
finite preparation interval, delay range, single-crossing restriction and the
paper's balanced magnitude, rather than maximize a different quantity.
All-time crossing admissibility is part of a counterexample, not something a
finite plotted trajectory can establish. The appendix supplies a useful linear
response representation, but that is not a proof that an optimum needs only
one switch. I did not establish the required global reduction or a certified
counterexample route. Repeating the displayed numerical schedules would not
justify a new project. This is a feasibility limit of this screen, not evidence
that the conjecture is false, settled, or impossible to tackle in days.
