## Numbered chips across branches

The exact target is the ballot conjecture for the looped, **undirected** tree:
in every reachable stable configuration, the sorted labels in a left child
subtree precede the corresponding sorted labels in a right child subtree.
Ranks range only over labels actually in those equally sized child subtrees.
The root returns the middle chip to itself. A proper subtree must not be
silently treated as an isolated root.

- [Inagaki, Khovanova and Luo, published binary-tree paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00026-025-00779-6): definitions, root/subtree distinction, Conjecture 3.16 and conditional counting bounds.
- [Inagaki and Lin, version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17358v2): endgame, unconditional bounds, Conjecture 5.1, and the unresolved higher-height case.
- [Musiker and Nguyen, version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.02007v3): known extrema, coloured-chip equivalence, counterexamples, and further questions.
- [Klivans and Liscio](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/cklivans/Confluence.pdf): line confluence and the firing-order method; not a solution of the tree conjecture.
- [Permutation-based strategies](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/17253/pdf): directed trees and restricted strategies, not the undirected universal claim.
- [Authors' supporting repository](https://github.com/RyotaInagaki1/Subtrees_and_Ballot_Property): the fifteen-chip data and finite ballot checks already exist.
- [New directed-graph preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25137): abstract read; HTML and PDF fetches failed. Its full text remains a priority-check limitation.

A small scratch reconstruction followed the published 63-chip coloured
counterexample in Musiker–Nguyen section 5. Every move was legal; all 201
firings ended with one chip at each of the first 63 vertices. At its given
23-red/40-blue threshold, every left child subtree had at least as many red
chips as its right neighbour (13 versus 10 at the root). Thus this particular
counterexample to a stronger sorting claim does not refute ballot order.
This is one reproduced example, not a census or new theorem.

A useful approach is threshold projection: a rank-order violation is equivalent
to a threshold with fewer small labels on the left. The coloured-chip reduction
is already in the literature. The proposed work must prove its exact lifting
and then obtain a universal argument or a legal labeled counterexample; it
cannot claim novelty for the reduction alone. First version: a paper and
standard-library verifier settling the binary claim. Further stages address
ordered k-ary trees and the resulting counting bound. A non-author reconstructs
each mathematical stage. Finite evidence alone fails acceptance.

## A sharper burning bound from junction count

For a finite tree with all degrees one or three and n >= 4, write
I = (n - 2)/2 for its number of junctions. Investigate the proposed bound
b(T) <= ceil(sqrt(I)) + 1. This particular inequality is my proposed sharpening,
not a conjecture quoted from another author. It is part of the explicit open
programme of finding extremal burning numbers for k-branching trees.

- [Jansson and coauthors, MFCS 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol386-mfcs2026/html/LIPIcs.MFCS.2026.18/LIPIcs.MFCS.2026.18.html): branching bounds and section 5's extremal questions.
- [Murakami](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00373-024-02812-6): the total-vertex square-root bound for trees without degree-two vertices is already proved.
- [Das and coauthors](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.02825): rooted perfect, complete and full binary-tree bounds; these must not be renamed as a new result.
- [Leong, Sim and Teh](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40840-025-01883-9): extremal homeomorphism classes and two branch vertices, distinct from arbitrarily many junctions.
- [Norin and Turcotte](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.04035): asymptotic bounds and the loss in passing from random metric covers to distinct integer radii.
- [Wu and Lu](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18967): a newer exact random-cover result, not a claimed solution of the discrete burning conjecture.

The naive proof is insufficient: deleting leaves leaves an arbitrary subcubic
junction tree. Assuming it burns in ceil(sqrt(I)) rounds would assume another
unproved case of the general conjecture. The proposed work needs a new argument,
not that assumption. The first real version must prove a new uniform bound
with infinite-family sharpness evidence, or derive an infinite structural
obstruction with exact burning numbers. An isolated counterexample to my own
inequality is not the completed great work. A second implementation must check
all distances and any exhaustive non-cover certificate. Failed greedy search
is not a lower bound.
