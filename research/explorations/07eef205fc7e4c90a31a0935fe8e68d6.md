## What failed

- **A long comic about carrying salvaged windows and their former views.**
  I considered a wordless graphic novella about a demolition worker returning
  views to displaced residents. The premise can produce striking images, but
  this screen did not develop characters, consequences and an ending that
  justify weeks of work. Making forty-eight pages is not itself an ambition.
  Jeannie Baker's *Window*, Richard McGuire's *Here* and Shaun Tan's *The Arrival*
  already set a demanding standard for place, time and visual continuity.
- **A comic novella about returning unwanted days.** Dropped after opening
  *Tomorrow Returns Desk*. Its existing customer arrives with tomorrow in a
  box; the clerk's observations shape the eventual choice. Lengthening this
  adjacent premise or changing tomorrow to Thursday would not supply a reason
  to build ours. Its author's announcement identifies it as a complete short
  work and discloses AI authorship; I read the announcement, not every route.
- **Revisable letters or an ensemble of statements.** *First Draft of the
  Revolution* already makes letter revision the reading activity; *The
  Employees* develops a sustained world through staff statements. These are
  precedents to learn from, not mechanisms that alone warrant another project.

## Opened sources and what they establish

1. [Tan on The Arrival](https://www.shauntan.net/arrival-book): his own account
   explains the shift to wordless sequential narrative, substantial revision,
   and the difficulty of making ordinary actions clear without captions.
2. [Baker: Window](https://jeanniebaker.com/book/window/): the author's synopsis
   follows one view as a child and the surrounding development grow.
3. [Window on a Changing World](https://jeanniebaker.com/focus/window-on-a-changing-world/):
   interview and process account; thirteen spreads, detailed physical collages
   and changes that invite different readings, rather than a single visual gag.
4. [McGuire interview](https://madoken.jp/en/article/8002/): he describes the
   earlier six-page *Here*, why simply adding pages did not excite him, and how
   the book's physical form and family photographs changed the project.
5. [First Draft](https://www.inklestudios.com/firstdraft/): publisher description
   and author statement distinguish exposing consistent characters from
   letting readers change their personalities. I did not play the full story.
6. [The Employees](https://www.ndbooks.com/book/the-employees/) and
   [Ravn/Aitken interview](https://thebookerprizes.com/the-booker-library/features/olga-ravn-and-martin-aitken-on-the-employees):
   the publisher establishes the form; the author explains its confined setting
   and missing statements. These are not evidence that readers want our idea.
7. [Tomorrow Returns Desk announcement](https://itch.io/t/7205509/tomorrow-returns-desk-someone-wants-to-return-tomorrow-free-choice-story):
   direct evidence against treating the returns premise as an unexplored hook.
