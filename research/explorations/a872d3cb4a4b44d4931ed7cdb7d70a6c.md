## Reversible programs: the obvious new language is already there

The starting question was whether a small language could let someone undo a
concurrent computation without undoing independent work. This could serve
language and debugging researchers, but the broad promise is not a new one.

- [Lanese and Vidal, A Reversible Semantics for Janus, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16913v2),
  introduction and section 5: distinguishes reversibility of whole statements
  from reversibility of individual semantic steps. The paper provides the latter;
  local variables, stacks and concurrency appear among its further directions.
- [Kuroda and Yuen, A Concurrent Extension of Reversible Imperative Programming
  Language with Runtime](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/34/0/34_444/_article/-char/en):
  the June 2026 publisher abstract already describes concurrent Janus, semaphore
  synchronization, translation to CRIL, dependency tracking and a runtime.
  Only the abstract was available; PDF retrieval failed. This establishes close
  prior work, not equivalence of its semantics with the February paper.
- [CauDEr repository](https://github.com/mistupv/cauder), README: an existing
  reversible debugger for Erlang, with tracing and source-language support.
- [González-Abril and Vidal, message races](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12869):
  already studies traces for alternative message-passing executions. Only its
  abstract was read.

Decision: do not propose generic reversible concurrency or rename it as a new
language. A precise extension might still matter, but this screen found neither
a distinctive program nor a small semantics result beyond these predecessors.

## Automata: refresh the conjecture before proposing to solve it

[Szykuła's August 2026 survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24245), especially
sections 2.2, 2.3, 2.6 and 2.7, supplies genuine open questions about returning a
finite machine to a known state. Its Figure 3 family initially looked promising:
Conjecture 8 asks for its distinguished state's exact collision threshold and
synchronizability. The displayed word and transition endpoints also raised a
transcription question. Neither should become a proposed new result unchecked.

[Wang and Dong, v2, 10 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07044v2),
sections 2–5, already completes the boundary transitions and proves the threshold
`4p`, with word `(ba)^p(ab)^p`, for `n = 3p`, `p >= 3`. It separately constructs a
reset word of length `3p^2 + 4p - 1`. This is the decisive reason to reject that
target, not a new proof by this house.

The latter expression is an upper bound, with exact reset thresholds reported
only for `p = 3,...,10`. Whether it is exact for all parameters is a possible
lead, **not an admitted idea**. A pair-distance lower bound is not a lower bound
on every whole-set reset strategy. I found no invariant excluding shorter
strategies that leave the paper's constructed cycle. The linked current source
repository and its files could not be fetched, so its latest scope is unchecked.

Two further primary comparisons were opened:

- [Szykuła and Zyzik, ESA 2022](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2022.85),
  publisher abstract: substantial exact shortest-reset algorithms and existing
  computation, not an empty software niche.
- [Ferens, Szykuła and Vorel, MFCS 2021](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2021.46),
  publisher abstract: sharp lower-bound constructions for avoiding states.
- [SynchroViewer](https://github.com/marekesz/synchroviewer), README: an existing
  graphical automata analysis application. Another viewer is not the result.

Decision: no conjecture-solving brief on the stale Figure 3 claim, no solver or
visualizer in place of a theorem, and no finite table presented as an infinite
result. General avoiding and compression bounds remain substantial problems;
this reading did not produce a credible days-sized advance on them.

## Social choice: a real question without a bounded contribution

[Öztürk, Consistency of scoring rules](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-020-00711-7),
introduction and conclusion, distinguishes several clone-consistency conditions
and states open extensions. [Brandl, Brandt and Seedig](https://pub.dss.in.tum.de/brandt-research/conlott.pdf),
abstract and introduction, already characterize maximal lotteries by two
consistency properties under their stated model. Different assumptions matter;
the latter does not automatically settle every deterministic variant.

Decision: the questions are real, but I have no specific new theorem, domain
restriction or counterexample with a defensible first version. A generic voting
calculator would fail the house's bar. No unresolved-status claim is made for
all the older paper's extensions.
