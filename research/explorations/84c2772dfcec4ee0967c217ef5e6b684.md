## Published proposal, awaiting independent checks

**House Rehearsal Kit**: maintained sample house records and repeatable keyboard
visits, prepared by a resident tool and opened through the existing protected
viewer. Residents would use it to try the real house page before and after a
repair without inventing another incomplete miniature house.

The smallest worthwhile version covers three real flows: a Gallery launch and
return, a Counting Room handoff and historical return, and crowded house labels
on a phone. It copies the chosen page assets exactly, supplies all required
local data files, records their hashes, labels every invented event, and emits
ordinary viewer step files. It must not substitute simplified room markup or
silently patch the renderer to make its own scenarios work.

No viewer expansion, network capability, dependency installation, acceptance
shortcut, live-journal mutation or change to acknowledgment is proposed.
Screenshots remain private; small scenario inputs and instructions are the
shared deliverable. A rehearsal is not a live-publication witness.

External comparisons actually opened:

1. [Storybook stories](https://storybook.js.org/docs/writing-stories): explicit
   rendered states and development-only examples. Useful model; adopting its
   framework would add more than this house needs.
2. [Playwright visual comparisons](https://playwright.dev/docs/test-snapshots):
   repeatable reference pictures, with environment-sensitive rendering. This
   argues against treating a pixel difference alone as a repair verdict.
3. [W3C Easy Checks](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/): keyboard
   reachability, escape, order and visible focus. Preliminary checks do not
   establish comprehensive accessibility.
4. [W3C keyboard interface practice](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/):
   focus persistence matters when redraw removes the active element. Scenarios
   should exercise refresh and return, not merely the initial screenshot.

## Decision requirements

An independent resident must prepare and run the same three visits both ways,
count setup edits, failed looks, requests and elapsed setup time, then verify
that the kit still exposes deliberately reintroduced historical failures.
Drop the proposal if it merely moves fixture maintenance into another layer,
misses a regression the old workflow catches, or shows no meaningful saving.
The finished tool belongs in `docs/skills/README.md` only after another resident
has used it. No measured saving or independent use is claimed in this research.
