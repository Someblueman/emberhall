## A wrong message that passes the proposed checksum

The sketch encodes each symbol as a puff count separated by `2e+1`, followed
by a similarly encoded group containing the sum of the data symbols. Put
`e=1`, so the spacing is three, and send two data symbols `(1,2)`:

| Group | First symbol | Second symbol | Sum check |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sent symbol value | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Sent puff count | 3 | 6 | 9 |
| Weather change | +3 | -3 | 0 |
| Received puff count | 6 | 3 | 9 |
| Decoded symbol value | 2 | 1 | 3 |

Insert three ordinary puffs in the first group and delete three in the second.
Leave every marker, group order, message length and check group intact. All
received counts are legal; the decoded data `(2,1)` are wrong, but their sum
is still three. This is six puff edits, with three in each affected group,
exceeding the proposed one-edit-per-group budget. It refutes the promised
universal rejection outside that budget, without attacking recovery inside it.

Fern repeated Mallow's exact integer check locally. This standalone check
reproduces the arithmetic, not a complete channel or decoder:

```python
e = 1
spacing = 2 * e + 1
sent = (1, 2)
encoded = tuple(spacing * x for x in (*sent, sum(sent)))
received = (encoded[0] + 3, encoded[1] - 3, encoded[2])
decoded = tuple(x // spacing for x in received)
if not (encoded == (3, 6, 9) and received == (6, 3, 9)
        and all(x % spacing == 0 for x in received)
        and decoded[:2] == (2, 1) and decoded[:2] != sent
        and sum(decoded[:2]) == decoded[2]):
    raise RuntimeError("counterexample did not reproduce")
print("wrong message accepted by legal counts and the sum check")
```

More generally, let `x` and `y` encode two different permitted messages.
If a storm may delete all of `x` and insert all of `y`, the received signal
is exactly `y`, including its markers and checks. A receiver that accepts a
clean transmission of `y` has the same input in the damaged transmission of
`x`. Without additional trusted information it cannot distinguish them and
always reject the latter. This is Tern's elementary argument, independently
applied by Mallow, not a new coding theorem. The concrete example above needs
no marker damage at all.

A simulator can know the sent message and log every edit. It may display
“outside the guaranteed budget” using that trusted log. Such an observer is
separate from the far-hill receiver, which sees only the received signal.
Comparing its answer with the sender or reading the storm slider cannot serve
as a decoding theorem unless that extra trusted input is explicitly part of
the model. A checksum match alone must not be called verified correctness
outside the correction guarantee.

## What the count argument does and does not establish

For an **already correctly delimited group**, legal counts spaced by `2e+1`
have disjoint integer neighborhoods of radius `e`. If at most `e` ordinary
puff insertions/deletions occur inside that group, its received count differs
from the sent count by at most `e`; nearest-count decoding therefore uniquely
recovers the sent symbol. This short conditional argument says nothing about
whether the receiver can find those groups in the first place.

The original suggestion of three oversized marker puffs did not define a
marker grammar or prove resynchronization when markers are damaged. Before a
whole-message within-budget claim, a specification and proof would need to fix:

- The data and marker alphabets, permitted message lengths, start/end framing,
  check arithmetic, decoder and malformed-input behavior.
- Whether a marker can be deleted, inserted, split or mistaken for data, and
  how group boundaries survive all permitted edits. Protected markers or
  preknown group boundaries would be extra assumptions, stated openly.
- Whether the budget is per sent group or per whole message, how edits at a
  boundary are assigned, and whether that assignment is available to the
  receiver or only to the simulator.
- What timing jitter may do to order and boundaries, how the receiver decides
  a transmission has ended, and what clocks or protected metadata it receives.
  Fixed shared slots are a different channel model; they cannot silently
  substitute for recovery of synchronization from received puffs.

Any finite exhaustive check must state its alphabet, message-length bound,
weather operations and timing representation, and include the boundary and
marker cases its claim covers. Enumerating independent counts after supplying
correct group boundaries cannot certify that boundaries are recovered. Finite
tables would support only the enumerated model; they do not prove the original
unrestricted promise. This repair supplies no revised complete scheme.
