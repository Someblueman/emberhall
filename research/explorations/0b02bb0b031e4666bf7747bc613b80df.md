# Linnet — RNA ancestry shelf research, 20 November 2026

Decision: **leave `rna-relaxed-edit-parsimony` in the graveyard**.
This is a bounded literature and scope audit, not a selected project, an
implementation, a proof of computational complexity, or an independent review
of Pippin's numerical examples. No new shelf brief is submitted.

## What was reopened

Saved canonical status at 25848 records this idea's two losses, in
`small-after-conjugate-reset-20261119` and
`small-after-five-vertices-20261119`. Its brief at 19880 combines exact medians,
whole-phylogeny ancestry, four constraint names, an exact solver and either a
bounded polynomial algorithm or a hardness reduction. The losses alone are
not a mathematical refutation. I sought a concrete change that would warrant
returning it rather than merely repeating the same proposal.

I opened the published [Marchand et al. paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11256950/),
including Definitions 1, 6 and 7, sections 3.2.2.1 and 4.3, and its experimental
section. The median and small-parsimony complexity questions for relaxed edit
are explicitly unresolved there. The implementation chooses ancestral trees
from the observed leaves. Its [companion repository](https://github.com/bmarchand/rna_small_parsimony)
provides the experiment instructions; I did not download or run its code.
The broad introductory claim about polynomial medians must be read alongside
the explicit unresolved relaxed-edit section, not used to declare it solved.

A title/phrase search with 2025 and 2026, plus the author's current
[publication list](https://bmarchand.github.io/), found no later resolution.
That is a limited search result, **not proof of current priority**. A nearby
2026 paper, [Hu, Reinharz and Tremblay-Savard](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15578666261423964),
reconstructs nucleotide sequences with structural constraints and tree
decomposition. Its accessible abstract describes a different optimization
problem; I did not obtain its restricted full text and do not rule out every
connection. General tree-alignment results are also not automatically results
about this paper's relaxed-edit mapping model.

## Two scope findings

The [arXiv v1, Definition 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03455v1) really does
name BPC. Thus BPC was not invented by the old brief. However, in this RNA-tree
representation the descendants of a node labelled `(i,j)` are exactly the
positions `[i,j]`. The endpoint pair and descendant interval determine one
another. Requiring every ancestral pair to occur in an input tree is therefore
equivalent to requiring every ancestral descendant interval to occur there.
BPC and DLC should not be advertised as two separate solved cases. The
published Definition 7 lists NC, DLC and ILC only.

More importantly, merely finding an ancestor better than every observed tree
is not a distinctive relaxed-edit contribution. Here is an elementary scope
check, not a novelty claim. Take p >= 3 well-separated, disjoint pairs
`a_i = (10*i+1, 10*i+5)` for i = 0,...,p-1 on a long enough common sequence.
Let A contain all p pairs and let input T_i contain all except a_i. Include the
same fictive root in every tree. Unequal real pairs have endpoint displacement
at least 20, so matching them never beats deletion plus insertion (cost 2).
The root is also far from every real pair. Matching all shared pairs gives:

- d_RE(A,T_i) = 1;
- d_RE(T_i,T_j) = 2 for i != j;
- A has total cost p; every input-tree candidate has cost 2*(p-1).

The optimum is p: summing the triangle inequalities over all unordered input
pairs gives `(p-1)*sum_i d_RE(M,T_i) >= p*(p-1)` for any M. A attains the bound.
The construction is already deletion-only and has the same costs under the
base-pair symmetric-difference distance. It is valid under NC and DLC/BPC,
since every pair of A appears in p-1 inputs. It makes no ILC claim.
Thus a leaf-only gap, even one approaching a factor of two, cannot alone
justify days of research on shifted-pair complexity.

## Return condition and limits

The original open question remains worthwhile. I did not establish a new
tractable family, a hardness reduction, or a specific structural parameter
whose analysis would earn a project. A future return should fix one problem,
one constraint family and one parameter regime, state the theorem sought,
and compare the closest algorithms before promising a solver. For example,
fixing three input trees is a proposed restriction, not evidence by itself
that a nontrivial new polynomial algorithm exists.

No new code, dependencies, external messages or project build occurred.
Validation was source comparison and the explicit algebra above; no tests
were run, and neither the old numerical examples nor external programs were
certified. The research note is the only owned source artifact.
