## Candidate 1: rules that explain what would change an answer

The possible great work was a language in which a programmer declares recursive
rules and asks for missing conclusions, necessary changes, and small diverse
explanations. A dependency or access-policy program would be a natural first
example. Its intended benefit was making the reason for an answer, including
absence, part of the program's meaning rather than an afterthought.

The underlying difficulty is real. Krötzsch's *Modern Datalog*, section 6,
describes the loss of simple proof-tree explanations with negation [1]. But
Soufflé already supplies minimal-height derivations and guided explanations of
missing tuples [2]. Bogaerts et al. define why/why-not provenance for negation
under well-founded and stable semantics and establish its complexity [3].
Calautti et al. study diverse explanation subsets, with algorithms and a public
implementation [4,5]. Thapa's November preprint addresses minimum interventions
among true, false and undefined answers [6]. These are distinct notions;
provenance support, a counterfactual repair and a diverse sample must not be
silently treated as the same object.

There are still explicit questions: diverse explanations with negation [4], and
syntactic restrictions guaranteeing coverage for tractable interventions [6].
However, this screen did not establish a new semantic construction, tractable
fragment, or convincing program improved by the proposed language. Adding syntax
around established algorithms does not yet justify weeks. **Do not shelve this
candidate on the evidence gathered.** A later proposal must select one of those
precise questions and establish its algorithmic and language contribution.

## Candidate 2: differentiation that accounts for moving boundaries

The second possible great work was a numerical language that treats integration
and discontinuity boundaries explicitly. The motivating failure is concrete:
differentiating a discretized step-function integral can miss its boundary term.

Teg already makes integration a language primitive and supports higher derivatives
[7]. Potto adds compositional evaluation and separate compilation, with semantics,
a renderer and an executable illustrative example [8,9]. Its earlier paper also
shows the boundary/interior decomposition [10]. Aδ handles more general nested
discontinuities with a different sampling approach; the author's comparison
explains both the coordinate-transform burden and its own language limits [11].
The 2025 automatic-sampling work further broadens shader support [12]. SoftJAX
and SoftTorch instead supply smooth relaxations; that is a different target from
sound differentiation of the original discontinuous model [13].

Automatic coordinate decomposition or certified handling of singular boundaries
might offer a stronger project. This reading did not establish a feasible new
algorithm or a sharp advantage over these predecessors. A familiar half-plane or
circle demonstration would not answer that question. **Do not shelve this
candidate either.**
