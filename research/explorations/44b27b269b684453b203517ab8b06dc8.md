## Question under investigation

What can an original designed work let readers see or compare that existing
responsive charts and transit maps hide? Two directions are being tested against
actual earlier work, rather than treating an attractive format as a need.

### Responsive visual arguments

[Kim, Moritz and Hullman, 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.07724), especially
sections 4.3–4.4, already distinguish information, interaction, discoverability,
concurrency and perception losses. A readable narrow chart can preserve values
while separating the marks needed for a comparison. Conversely, deleting data
can strengthen a deliberately chosen message. A blanket rule that mobile views
must preserve everything would misread the work.

[Cicero, 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.08314), sections 6–8, already supplies
thirteen reproductions, reusable transformations, associated-element handling and
a prototype recommender. It even describes preserving more takeaways by retaining
data and externalizing annotations. A new responsive grammar or gallery of
resize tricks is not a sufficient proposal.

The [companion gallery](https://mucollective.github.io/responsive-vis-gallery/)
contains a problem/strategy/loss/compensation structure. The fetch exposed its
interface and unresolved template text, not its running examples. I cannot claim
to have tested the gallery or that an example is missing from it.

[W3C Meaningful Sequence](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/meaningful-sequence.html)
requires a programmatically determinable correct order when order affects
meaning; it explicitly permits several correct orders. That is a design
constraint, not a proof that a visual comparison remains perceptually available.

[Rahman et al., 2026](https://publications.sci.utah.edu/publications/Rah2026a/2604.07691v1.pdf),
particularly pages 6–10, describes proximity, keyed notes, hierarchy and
responsive annotation density. The authors distinguish practitioner heuristics
from tested audience outcomes. Their concrete warning about data linkage lost
in cross-tool editing further weakens a proposal that merely draws prettier
callouts. A candidate must state precisely what relationship survives each
transformation, then show the actual alternative—not call an automated check a
comprehension study.

Working possibility: an original executable design book of explicit comparison
questions, paired failures and alternative compositions, with freely reusable
source figures. Its worth depends on adding something beyond the existing
378-pair study, thirteen Cicero examples and annotation gallery. A new name for
those collections would fail.

### Transit maps and time

[Wu et al., 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/cgf.14030),
sections 5 and 7, separates visual quality, route choice and network learning.
The paper explicitly warns against deriving general design rules from a
particular curvilinear map's faster route-planning result. Its open challenges
include topographical distortion and professional composition, but an untested
map cannot claim to solve those empirical questions.

[Forsch et al., 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tgis.12821)
already computes schematic reachable zones with a separation property, handles
timetables and displays different times of day. Its extensions discuss nested
zones, river clipping, closing small holes and combinations of outline methods.
A night-time reachability picture alone is not a new capability.

The original survey PDFs exceeded the web tool's size bound; the Wiley full
texts were available. Requested screenshots of responsive papers did not return
visible images through this tool route. No visual trial is claimed.

## Later reading changed the shortlist

[Task-oriented insight preservation](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.08141), including
sections 3 and 6, already measures identification, comparison and trend losses.
[AdaMV](https://content.ieeevis.org/year/2023/paper_v-tvcg-10029921.html) already
adapts multiple-view layouts. [Proteus](https://yong-wang.org/publication/26-DIS26-Proteus.pdf)
adds current multi-level and interaction adaptation, though its authors explicitly
leave task-equivalence evaluation open. A narrower comparison-contract library
remained conceivable, but was not sufficiently distinguished from that work and
constraint-based breakpoints to earn a brief this round. It was not published.

The same check rejected a general transit-map pipeline: both
[Legible Cities](https://github.com/richc117/legible-cities) and
[Portolan](https://github.com/alexwohlbruck/portolan) describe current schedule-to-map
pipelines. [Forrest's bus-map abstract](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/6/64/2023/ica-abs-6-64-2023.pdf)
and [Walker's frequency-mapping argument](https://humantransit.org/basics/the-case-for-frequency-mapping)
show genuine design problems, not evidence that the proposed generic tool is
missing. These sources were read, not installed or run.

## Two design proposals

**Please Take This Street With You** is an original puzzle adventure. A walking
carrier borrows one adjacent uncommitted road, walks along what remains, and lays
it elsewhere. Delivering a letter commits a clearly marked street to its
neighbourhood. The player chooses the delivery order and must retain a way home.
This commitment was added during research because unrestricted road reuse could
otherwise reduce the game to repetitive hopping. Whether it is actually fun is
unproved; the first gate is one playable board with two letters, reliable undo,
and an uncoached non-author's account of a satisfying consequence.

The closest reading was the creators' [Carto interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-board-games-helped-inspire-tile-hopping-puzzler-i-carto-i-),
[DEVICE 6 development account](https://simogo.com/work/device-6/),
[The Pedestrian interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-skookum-arts-built-i-the-pedestrian-i-puzzles-out-of-urban-signage),
and [Railbound interview](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=0x08hncy).
[Paper Trail](https://www.papertrailgame.com/) and
[Road Not Taken](https://blog.playstation.com/2013/12/10/the-unique-puzzles-of-road-not-taken-on-ps4/)
also have close mechanics or imagery. Moving worlds, finite track and carrying
objects are not new. The proposal stands on a particular authored play experience,
not a claim to have invented them. The complete ambition is three compact town
chapters, roughly six deliveries each, with original postal-stamp silhouettes,
blue-black street drawing and character writing.

**A Mariner in Two Voices** is a complete illustrated digital edition of
Coleridge, not a newly authored poem. Its visual argument holds the sailor's
verse and the later marginal voice together: open space, repeated eyes, rigging
that hardens into a grid, then opens without completely disappearing. The first
version is all of Part IV with four original plates and a faithful linked gloss;
the complete work develops twenty-four plates across seven parts. The independent
question is whether it rewards a second look, while remaining readable with
motion off. No new scholarly interpretation or missing-edition claim is needed.

[The Digital Rime rationale](https://www.huronresearch.ca/thedigitalrime/rationale/)
already explains its optional gloss and parallel text versions. I read its
[poem](https://www.huronresearch.ca/thedigitalrime/the-poem/), including the Part IV
water-snakes and transition to sleep, alongside the separate
[1817 gloss transcription](https://www.huronresearch.ca/eng2/texts/1817-ed-rime-of-the-ancient-mariner-w-glosses/gloss-and-further-changes-to-the-1817-sibylline-leaves-edition/).
Its text must still be collated against a declared edition before building;
the project must not silently mix versions. Gutenberg's requested pages returned
503 errors and are not cited as opened sources.

[Intercity's account](https://intercitystudio.com/work/ambr) of the Ancient Mariner
Big Read already describes changing water-state imagery, and
[Plymouth's completion report](https://www.plymouth.ac.uk/news/unique-releases-mark-conclusion-of-coleridge-classic-for-the-21st-century)
records its finished audio-visual work. The
[British Library account](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/en/articles/an-introduction-to-the-rime-of-the-ancient-mariner/)
discusses the differing narrators and earlier illustration traditions. The new
edition must earn its place through its own sustained graphic interpretation;
none of those modern images, recordings or commentary would be copied.

Both proposals are great-scale candidates with distinct first gates, linked
precedents and quoted creator/editor evidence. Neither has been built, selected,
independently kept or demonstrated to meet its worth test. No small or house
candidate was added merely to fill a bench category.
