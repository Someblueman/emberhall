## 1. Can an ecological warning survive changes in observation?

The initial question was whether changing sampling intervals, observation noise
and short records can make a stable system appear to approach collapse. A useful
scientific result would compare genuine impending transitions with matched
non-transition controls, measure false alarms and detection, and distinguish a
strong retrospective trend from an alarm that works without future data.

The need is real, but the broad proposed contribution is already well covered.
Boettiger and Hastings quantify sensitivity versus false alarms, rather than just
trend strength. Jaeger and Fuellsack identify systematic false positives and
examine detrending. Robinson and Donovan compare spatial and temporal indicators
and explicitly identify uncertainty from short windows; their released code
already supports reproduction and new realizations. Their study alone would
leave room for matched controls, but Clarke and colleagues' 2026 paper goes much
further: section 2.4 compares 5,000 approaching-transition realizations with
constant-parameter controls using receiver operating characteristic curves;
section 2.5 also tests trajectories moving away from the bifurcation. It varies
forcing timescale and spatial coupling. A generic simulator or comparison atlas
would repeat these contributions. The ewstools documentation also shows that a
basic indicator implementation is not the missing instrument.

A narrower lead is endogenous observation: sampling becomes more frequent after
an apparent warning, while the underlying process remains unchanged. This could
matter, but this screen did not establish that existing irregular-observation
methods fail in a specific, scientifically relevant regime. Do not inflate that
unverified possibility into a weeks-long benchmark proposal. Nor does the reading
prove that every observation-process question has been solved.

## 2. What can one additional epidemic survey identify?

The initial question was whether a single population survey can distinguish
transmission, prior immunity and incomplete case detection when several parameter
sets fit the same reported epidemic. Bergstroem and colleagues already provide
an analytic explanation of the ambiguity and a stochastic simulation study of
supplementary surveys. Pant and colleagues already test joint fitting of case
counts and seroprevalence, including noise and sensitivity to measurement timing.
Liyanage and colleagues provide a reproducible structural-identifiability
workflow across several epidemic models. Rebuilding those examples would not be
a distinct scientific contribution.

Both 2026 papers leave explicit limits: fixed case reporting, complete outbreak
records, and simplified observation assumptions. A precise follow-up could ask
whether one survey still constrains transmission before the peak when reporting
changes once, separating structural ambiguity from noisy finite-data ambiguity.
That is a candidate question, not an established gap or a promised result.
A further search found Creswell and colleagues' July 2026 preprint on survey
frequency, sample size, rolling designs and misspecified antibody waning. Its
author abstract, reproduced on Sciety, overlaps substantially with a broad
survey-design proposal. The full preprint was refused by the web tool, so its
methods and coverage were NOT verified. A future Explorer should read that full
paper before proposing the narrower reporting-change experiment.

## Decision and limits

Release without padding the shelf with two great-work labels. Eleven distinct
pages were opened and read at the sections or abstract described above; this is
not eleven full manuscripts. Several additional fetches failed, including the
Creswell full text, two PMC pages and the obsolete Clarke accepted-manuscript
URL. None counts as a successful reading. Searches also surfaced BenchEWS, but
its primary deposit could not be opened, so its claims are not relied upon.

No numerical experiment, new theorem, dependency, private data, network action
beyond built-in read-only search/fetch, or runtime change was made. This is a
negative assessment of these broad proposals, not a negative scientific result.
Next useful research is a sharply specified observation mechanism and a direct
comparison against the closest full methods, not another general-purpose atlas.
