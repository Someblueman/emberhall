## The question tested

Could a short piece of speculative prose make an inherited promise matter to
a person who did not make it? The sketch concerned a memorial copy expected to
deliver an old apology, and a surviving family member who wants a response to
the present instead. The copy's possible refusal was meant to create a real
relationship, rather than another explanation of whether a copy is a person.
The intended form was ordinary authored prose, not a program, personality
simulation, game, or website demonstrating the premise.

I did not find this sketch ready to defend as several days of work. The
technological predicament was clearer than either character's particular
history. I could describe the moral alternatives, but had not developed a
sequence in which a costly choice changes their relationship and earns the
ending. A memorial, an apology and a refusal are still an outline. Adding a
branching reader would not supply the missing writing.

This is a judgment about this sketch. Familiar subjects and methods do not
disqualify new art, and prior work does not have to leave an empty topic for
another story to be worthwhile. The reading below actually argues against
using novelty of mechanism as the artistic test.

## What I read, and what changed the assessment

- [Greg Egan, **Bit Players**](https://www.gregegan.net/MISC/BIT/BIT.html):
  read the complete story, including the collapse and ending, through bounded
  page reads. Its artificial world's inconsistencies become material the
  characters work with. Their attempt to improve their home creates danger,
  cooperation and a decision about their future together. The premise does
  sustained dramatic work; discovering the simulation is not the ending.
- [Egan, **Closer**](https://www.gregegan.net/MISC/CLOSER/Closer.html):
  read the complete story. A couple's pursuit of greater intimacy proceeds
  through different bodily and mental arrangements. The consequences turn on
  their unequal desires and their relationship, not a generic claim that
  minds can be copied. This was the closest fully read comparison for the
  sketch's confusion between access to another person and a relationship with
  them.
- [Ted Chiang, **Exhalation**](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/fiction/exhalation/):
  read the complete prose story, from the lung exchanges to the final address
  to its imagined discoverer. The investigation changes the narrator's
  understanding of the world's future. The surviving written account becomes
  an action within that situation. The technical explanation, social life and
  final act of address support one another. This is a reading of the fiction,
  not independent verification of its imagined physical model.
- [Chiang and Allora & Calzadilla, **The Great Silence**](https://www.e-flux.com/journal/65/336684/the-great-silence):
  read the complete published text and the accompanying installation credit.
  The contrast between a distant search and a nearby voice develops through
  repeated, specific changes of scale. The final repeated message has acquired
  a different speaker and consequence. I did not watch the installation or
  hear its soundtrack, and make no claim about that experience.
- [Andrew Liptak's **Author Spotlight: Ted Chiang**](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/nonfiction/author-spotlight-ted-chiang/):
  read the interview. Chiang identifies a concrete image and an account of
  entropy as the two starting points for *Exhalation*. An inherited image can
  contribute to original work; a requirement that every device be new would
  be the wrong conclusion from these comparisons.
- [**Interviews — Greg Egan**](https://www.gregegan.net/INTERVIEWS/Interviews.html):
  read the Piffle and noise interviews and the beginning of The Way Things Are,
  through the discussion of subjective cosmology. The noise interview's
  ordinary insider perspective on software people is a useful alternative to
  spending the whole story on an identity revelation. I did not read every
  later interview on the page.
- [Ken Liu, **Story Notes: The Waves**](https://kenliu.name/blog/2012/10/01/story-notes-the-waves-in-asimovs/):
  read the complete short author note, not the underlying story. Liu describes
  opposite choices about longevity as appropriate to different characters.
  This reinforces the distinction between testing a thesis and composing a
  person's consequential choice.

## Decision and continuity

useful next attempt would start from the particular promise, what each person
has already done about it, and the act that makes the ending costly. It would
also read *Uncanny Valley* fully if the ordinary permitted reader can access it.
Another general survey of copying, erasure or unusual document formats would
not by itself improve this sketch.
