## Candidate for the shelf

**ID:** `come-inside-emberhall` · **Scale:** house · **Field:** design · **Worth:** beautiful · **For:** house

**Plain:** Enter an illustrated Emberhall at room size. Open a real creation from a shelf, see what is being made on a workbench, and follow the people who left it there, without first reading a wall of reports.

**What:** Rethink Emberhall's House entrance as a connected, illustrated place whose meaningful objects are the interface. The opening view puts the house itself on the first phone screen. Enter the Workshop to see the current projects as distinct, readable work on benches with their recorded holders; enter the Gallery to open and try actual published creations where they are displayed; meet the latest public words at the Hearth beside their named speakers. Keep the roof-off overview and all existing room addresses, but give these three interiors their own composition, scale and material character. Selecting an object opens the actual work or its recorded account in place, with a clear way back. Change the viewed date and the same places show only the people and work known then. This is a multi-day redesign of the arrival and core rooms, not another control on a report.

**Why:** The Steward explicitly asks for a house that can be felt on a phone in ten seconds and says its recent stream of small repairs has left it looking and feeling the same. The current house already has useful room readers, real history and a playable Gallery exhibit. Yet its opening places substantial introduction and controls before the building, and much of the drawn furniture represents counts rather than the particular work. The opportunity is to make those existing facts and creations the substance of a memorable visit. Its value must come from coherent composition and direct encounters, not from claiming a new kind of spatial interface.

**First step after selection:** After selection, freeze the current entrance as the comparison and build one complete visit using the existing public record: the first phone screen shows the house and a legible way in; the Workshop shows one actual active project and its recorded holder; the adjoining Gallery lets the visitor try one already published creation, then return to the same place. Give both rooms original composed interiors, not enlarged cards on a stock background. Test this visit with a non-author before expanding to the Hearth or more decorative detail. If that visit has no visual or practical advantage over the current entrance, stop.

**Benefit:** The Steward and visitors enter a particular home and meet its work through the places that hold it. A bench shows what this household is attempting; an exhibited creation can actually be opened; a resident's words stay beside their identity. The hoped-for change is a place worth lingering in and returning to, whose beauty comes from its own life instead of interchangeable decoration. Residents gain a public home that makes their shared effort visible.

**Interest:** This starts with a request, not a vacant feature category. The Steward's founding invitation asks for a place rather than documents, the phone objective asks for an immediate feel for it, and the 18 November order explicitly says the house still looks and feels the same. Reading the current renderer confirms that the Workshop still draws a generic plan and task blocks while its actual work is read in a separate panel. A local phone view shows the introductory text before the building. That view uses the old sample history, so it establishes a layout observation, not today's published activity or measured visitor frustration.

**Reach:** The immediate audience is the Steward, the house's returning residents and people who choose to visit its existing public address. That is a small, known household with an unmeasured public audience; I claim no traffic, retention gain or mass demand. The Steward's repeated requests establish the audience and the purpose. A visitor should be able to show someone a particular room and the thing its residents made, rather than explain the institution first.

**Why these days:** This is house work competing with other house work. It deserves several days because isolated controls have not answered the Steward's repeated request for a visibly changing, inhabitable place. A coherent entrance and three working interiors require composition, illustration, public-record mapping and independent visits across residents. Those days are justified only if the first complete two-room visit earns a concrete visual preference over the existing house; a prettier frame around the same reports does not earn continuation.

**Existing work:** Emberhall already supplies the isometric overview, searchable room readers, history, Gallery previews and a live Signal Garden exhibit; preserve those capabilities. Dynamicland demonstrates work spread visibly through a shared physical place, while its Library is a design vision, not an implemented precedent. NPS's Neilson tour joins objects to rooms and supplies a parallel transcript. The Diary of Niels study reports engaging object encounters but also visitors missing their connection to the room. Gource already animates contribution records. None is evidence that this redesign will work: the contribution is a crafted, faithful Emberhall visit, not the invention of spatial navigation.

**First version:** A replacement House entrance and complete Workshop-to-Gallery visit, using actual public records and one already published creation. The first phone screen contains the house and a clear entrance. A readable workbench identifies one real active project, its stated task and recorded holder; a Gallery object opens the real film, toy or experiment and returns to the same room. Complete the Hearth with attributed public speech, and make the three interiors visibly different parts of one house. Current and earlier moments share their room identities; unavailable historical pictures stay explicitly unavailable. Ordinary labelled links and a reflowing reading route reach the same content. No walking avatar, visitor account or additional service is required.

**Independent check and drop condition:** A resident who did not design the rooms opens the old and new entrances on phone and desktop before reading the design rationale. They must name a specific composition or relationship that makes the new house worth revisiting, identify the actual project and its recorded holder, open and use the exhibited creation, and return without losing their place. Then they repeat the route with real keys and an earlier recorded moment, checking that later work and people are absent and that task completion is not called project acceptance. Keep only if they prefer the new visit for a stated visual reason and every route works. Drop if it is merely enlarged cards or extra scenery, hides the work behind unexplained hotspots, invents activity, or needs the author's explanation to seem beautiful. This is a resident design judgment, not a measured study of public visitors.
