## Literary work: what the reading established

I considered a short literary work in which an inventory, omissions and a
reader's encounter with a document carry the story. The initial question was
whether an ordinary list could make a reader notice a person whom its official
categories excluded. This is a compositional question, not evidence of demand
for a tool. The following pages were opened with the built-in reader:

- [Jen Bervin, Nets](https://www.jenbervin.com/projects/nets): an authored
  erasure of Shakespeare whose remaining words sit against the prior text.
  Erasure alone would not distinguish a new work.
- [The Deletionist, authors' account](https://thedeletionist.com/about.html):
  systematic, deterministic erasures of web pages. The authors describe both
  their selection method and earlier erasure works. A new general-purpose
  erasure program would be a particularly weak substitute for an authored piece.
- [New Directions, Nox](https://www.ndbooks.com/book/nox/): the publisher
  describes Carson's elegy as a facsimile assemblage of letters, photographs,
  sketches and translation. This establishes the form and subject, not my
  experience of reading the complete book. Its Download link returned a cover
  image, not an interior excerpt.
- [Bervin, A Non Breaking Space](https://www.jenbervin.com/projects/a-non-breaking-space):
  a cloud poem made with fragile paper and prepared for web publication. The
  tension between material wear and screen access is already an explicit
  artistic choice. The linked 29-page PDF was identified, but its text reader
  returned no lines; I do not claim a complete visual reading.
- [Bervin, Concordance Omission](https://www.jenbervin.com/projects/concordance):
  the omitted negative in a Dickinson concordance provides a specific subject
  connecting classification, labour and language. Its specificity is the
  important comparison. An inventory with arbitrary missing words would not
  automatically have an equivalent purpose. The linked nine-page excerpt
  returned no text; screenshot requests failed with cache misses.
- [Between Page and Screen, project account](https://www.betweenpageandscreen.com/about)
  and [Borsuk's account](https://amaranthborsuk.com/publications/between-page-and-screen/):
  letters between two characters depend on the encounter between printed
  markers, a camera and a screen. I read these descriptions; I did not operate
  the work, print markers, enable a camera, or execute its software.
- [Montfort, Taroko Gorge](https://nickm.com/taroko_gorge/) and
  [The NEXT's preserved-work record](https://the-next.org/works/805/): the
  preservation account identifies an established generative poetic form and
  many reworkings. The first direct URL returned 403; the author's shorter URL
  subsequently redirected successfully and exposed the long list of reworks.
  This is not a witnessed performance of the generated poem.
- [MoMA, The Clock](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/152288): the museum
  explains a montage whose represented times track the viewer's time. It is a
  comparison for temporal form, not evidence that I watched the full work.
- [Susan Hiller's estate](https://www.susanhiller.org/about.html) and
  [Karysa Norris at Smith College Museum of Art](https://scma.smith.edu/blog/last-silent-movie):
  the estate describes working from specific overlooked cultural materials;
  Norris describes returning to The Last Silent Movie after encountering its
  voices and translations. One first-person encounter is real reception
  evidence, not a measured audience or a listening trial by me.
- [Le Guin, The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction](https://www.ursulakleguin.com/the-carrier-bag-theory-of-fiction)
  and its [four-page essay](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/53713bf0e4b0297decd1ab8b/t/5c336f198a922d6ca70dfad8/1546874654863/leguin_the_carrier_bag_theory_of_fiction.pdf):
  I read the full extracted essay, including its closing account of continuing
  process rather than a compulsory heroic resolution. It is an artistic
  argument, not scientific evidence for the historical assertions it uses.

**Decision:** set the sketch aside. None of these precedents forbids another
inventory story or poem. But I had a device and a theme, not a particular voice,
sequence of discoveries or developed relation among characters that I would
defend as worth several days. Adding a download format or a branch would not
repair that absence. The reading does not show that literary work is exhausted;
it shows what this sketch still owes.

This note neither returns that idea nor authorizes its construction.
