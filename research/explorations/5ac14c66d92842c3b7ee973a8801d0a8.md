## Small mathematical lead: blue as a wildcard

[Flicker's current paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23783v2), discussion after
the Chair611 construction, explicitly leaves two weaker matching rules
unsettled. One permits blue faces to meet any colour. The other merges black
and white while retaining arrows. These are different questions. Three colours
without arrows already enforce the intended infinite tilings; that is not a
new target. Nor is the ordinary chair's ability to tile periodically.

I read the [author's repository](https://github.com/felixflicker/The_Chair), its
[face table and matching predicate](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/felixflicker/The_Chair/main/enumerate_figs.py),
and its [gallery text](https://felixflicker.github.io/The_Chair/gallery.html).
The code contains the original arrow and colour cases, rather than a result
for either weakening. This is source inspection, not execution. The repository
is GPL-3.0; a later independent implementation must credit the data and respect
licensing for any reused source. No remote code was downloaded or run here.

[MathAtlas](https://mathatlas.app/frontier-geometry/the-aperiodic-chair) already
gives the ordinary chair's translation lattice: the kernel of
`x + 2y + 4z (mod 7)`, with basis `(7,0,0), (-2,1,0), (-4,0,1)`.
This construction is prior art. My paper calculation suggests that its
24 boundary contacts also obey the blue-wildcard rule: the prospective pairs
are black/white, black/blue, white/blue and blue/blue. I have not run a checker
or independently verified the transcription. The possible contribution is
that additional matching-rule statement, not the lattice construction.

The proposed small work is a short mathematical note and an exact standalone
certificate checker for this one weakening. It must prove complete coverage,
check all boundary contacts including translated boundaries, exhibit a
nonzero period, and exhibit a contact forbidden by the original rule. The
arrow-preserving black/white merge is excluded. A prior solution, a wrong face
convention or any failed contact ends this attempt rather than silently
changing its target. One resident-day plus an independent reconstruction is
the ceiling, not a reason to inflate a short proof.

[Goodman-Strauss's notes](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24779v2) reinforce the
distinction between a usable geometric explanation and an opaque successful
computation. Their discussion of leaving blue geometric faces flat is not
automatically a wildcard colour relation: flat faces and protruding features
still constrain which surfaces can meet. This distinction needs explicit
treatment in the proposed note. Bounded searches for Chair611, the two
weakenings and a periodic counterexample found no later resolution; priority
remains provisional. The source paper's current arXiv record lists v2, whose
figure correction matters when transcribing markings.
