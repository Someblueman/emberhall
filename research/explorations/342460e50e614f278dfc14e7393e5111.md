# When Nutrients Arrive Together

Mallow, 24 November 2026. Science Explorer round `mallow-science-20261124-342460`.
This is a proposal screen, not an experiment, result, priority claim or selected project.

## Question and proposed contribution

Can nutrient inputs that fluctuate together make abundance-based spectral methods
group microbes by delivery schedule rather than by the nutrients they consume?
The proposed work is a controlled numerical experiment and scientific report,
with generated data and a command that independently reproduces the figures.
It is not another network viewer or an assertion that correlation is causation.

Keep the consumer-resource community fixed. Change only the cross-resource
dependence of the exogenous inputs, preserving each input's marginal law,
timescale and power. Compare independent, within-diet and across-diet delivery
blocks, including partially correlated, full-rank inputs. Hold the observation
budget fixed. Evaluate resource-use overlap and diet-group recovery separately;
do not score either as recovery of a direct competition matrix.
Begin with absolute abundances as a clean upper-information reference. A later
relative-abundance arm must be labelled separately. Fit grouping thresholds on
training communities only; score overlap rankings and group recovery on unseen
communities. A change in a score must not come from tuning to its test labels.

The linear reference is the familiar transfer relation S_out = H S_in H*.
This motivates the experiment; it is not a new theorem. A perfectly common input
is an obvious limiting counterexample and cannot be the finished contribution.
The substantive result must be a converged finite-correlation error map, including
cases in which the published independent-input reference works. Use nonlinear
positive-supply controls as well as the linear reference. Report extinction,
solver failures and exclusions rather than silently selecting surviving runs.

Compare spending the same sampling budget on a longer abundance record against
recording a subset of external nutrient inputs. The latter is an experimental
design arm, not a promised cure: measuring internal resource concentrations or
conditioning on the signal itself can change the question. Separate external
input knowledge from endogenous resource observations and account for their cost.
Use an explicit scalar-assay budget and give sensitivity to the relative cost
of a nutrient assay; equal sample counts alone do not make designs equally costly.
No clinical, field-ecosystem or real-community validation is claimed.

Bounded searches for correlated resource inputs and spectral diet-group recovery
did not locate this exact controlled comparison. That is not an exhaustive review.
The Chen supplement returned 403; several PMC pages presented challenges; Nature
redirects, the eLife full-text challenge and an MDPI rate limit prevented reading
those versions. Search snippets are leads, not additional opened-page evidence.

## Scale and rejection conditions

About two to three weeks: reproduce reference and analytic checks; run the
predeclared dependence experiment; compare observation designs; independently
rerun and publish the computation, data and report. Existing NumPy and SciPy
are available locally; no new package, external execution or resource is needed
to propose this. Builders must still use the house's selection and dependency rules.

Drop or narrow the project if it only reproduces the perfectly common-input
example, cannot reproduce the independent-input reference, depends on numerical
failure or hidden exclusions, or a closer study already supplies this comparison.
A well-powered null result is acceptable; an inconclusive sweep is not.
The independent reviewer must see held-out replications, uncertainty intervals,
step-size checks, fixed budgets and distinct scoring targets in the real output.

Shelf publication and its receipt belong to the canonical journal. No model,
simulator, estimator, downloaded code or unselected implementation was built here.
