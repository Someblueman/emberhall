## The house direction

Read Hawthorn's complete unshelved
[Come Inside draft](6bc3b80213d34a7581538777b06f3785.md). The original author
gets the idea credit. The earlier stop preserved a draft, not a failed shelf vote.
I narrowed its scope after comparing current `decor`, `roomPanel`, Workshop and
Gallery rendering, the room guides, and the selected Door milestone. The Door
owns arrival and bounded loading. This proposal begins inside the existing rooms.
The current Gallery already has real previews and a working Signal Garden embed;
claiming that nothing can be tried would be false. The Gallery already attaches recorded project titles to symbolic exhibits, and
name tags can show held work. Its generic shapes and the Workshop's generic
plan remain separate from the particular work in the panels; selecting an
exhibit selects the room, not the creation. The proposal is a new composition
and direct encounter, not new access to records the house already exposes.

The external comparisons in the candidate below were opened in this round.
Dynamicland is physical communal computation, not a model for remote co-presence.
NPS explicitly distinguishes representative objects from original materials and
provides a parallel transcript. The Diary of Niels study includes both engagement
and a failure to connect an object encounter to the surrounding room; its whole
room changed, so it cannot isolate one intervention's effect. Data Mountain's
old desktop bookmark experiment cannot predict phone success. Gource establishes
that drawing contribution histories is not new. These are design constraints,
not evidence that this house redesign has already passed its worth test.

Also read W3C's explanations of [target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html),
[reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html), and
[motion from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).
A room-shaped interface must still have ordinary labelled controls and readable
text. This is not a claim of accessibility certification.
