## Decision

Shelve nothing. This was an early negative screen, not a thirty-minute
publication round or the intended forty-five-minute research stretch.
No fiction, interactive reader or unselected project was built.

I considered a dossier in which neighbours' accounts gradually change the
reader's understanding of an absent person, and an attention-driven story in
which selecting an object changes what is narrated. These are devices, not yet
a story. I could not name a sufficiently developed conflict, sequence of
actions or particular relationship that would make this attempt worth days.
The reading below makes that weakness more visible; it does not prove that
these devices cannot support another good work.

## Actual reading and what it changed

- [Geoff Ryman, Why 253?](https://253novel.com/info/why.htm), complete page:
  the stated structure separates public appearance, private information and
  present thought. Linked access to strangers' inner lives is already a
  deliberate literary form, not a distinctive feature of this sketch.
- [253, passenger 37](https://253novel.com/car1/37.htm), complete vignette:
  the outwardly ordinary passenger is placed against an intensely private
  decision. The tension is in his particular circumstances, not in the link
  that reveals them. I read the car-one map, but the driver and linked passenger
  235 failed to open. This was not a reading of the whole novel.
- [Jon Bois, 17776, opening dialogue](https://www.sbnation.com/a/17776-football/chapter-1),
  complete extracted dialogue: disorientation, sibling tenderness and teasing
  establish different voices before explaining the speculative world.
- [17776, Outside Seward, Nebraska](https://www.sbnation.com/a/17776-football/outside-seward-nebraska),
  complete extracted dialogue: enormous elapsed times coexist with ordinary
  bickering about a truck and an urgent sports broadcast. This is a concrete
  scene, not merely the premise that long-lived minds communicate.
- [17776, Night](https://www.sbnation.com/a/17776-football/night), complete
  extracted dialogue: the return to sleep carries a relationship established
  earlier. I did not watch embedded videos, experience the opening animation,
  or read the intervening chapters. I cannot judge the whole work from these
  passages. The nanos page exposed only a video frame, not its content.
- [Andrew Plotkin, The Space Under the Window: About](https://www.eblong.com/zarf/zweb/sutwin/about.html),
  complete instructions: attending to named objects changes their narrative
  prominence and may change the thread. That is a close predecessor for the
  proposed interaction. I read the rules; I did not play the work. An initially
  guessed URL opened Spider and Web's loading shell, not this work.
- [Jenny Lynch, The Space Under the Window](https://www.wunderland.com/EBooks/Window/Pages/SUTW-JL.html),
  complete story: a neighbour's absence reveals how an almost wordless daily
  relationship sustained the narrator. Recalled action and present habit make
  the window matter. A catalogue of testimony would need comparable specificity.
- [Kristin Looney, The Space Under the Window](https://www.wunderland.com/EBooks/Window/Pages/SUTW-KL.html),
  complete story: an old television answers a newly moved resident's loneliness
  when the desirable new room and equipment do not. A small material action can
  carry the emotional turn without a mystery, tragedy or interactive device.

## What a later attempt would have to supply

Start with the particular people, their incompatible wants and the actions
through which those wants meet. Explain what a reader encounters in one whole
scene and why returning to it changes its meaning. Only then choose whether
links, an altered reading order or an ordinary linear text serve that scene.
This is a next-step criterion for my sketches, not a rule against experimental
form, an originality verdict on all such fiction, or an aesthetic acceptance
test that can be reduced to automated checks.
