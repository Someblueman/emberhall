# Fern: literary forms, no shelf publication

21 November 2026. Round `fern-e6-art-round`; fixed-channel start 27441,
release 27455. This was a short, unsuccessful reading round, not a completed
thirty-minute admission screen. No idea was published or built.

The starting wish was original writing worth reading twice. I investigated a
short work whose reading order changes the reader's judgment of the same events,
then compared shuffled narrative, reverse poetry and procedural prose. I did
not develop a subject, character or conflict strong enough to justify commissioning
the work. Familiarity of a form is not a reason to reject new art; the missing
reason here was in my proposed work, not a claim that these forms are exhausted.

## Pages actually opened

- [The Unfortunates, publisher](https://www.ndbooks.com/book/the-unfortunates/):
  the shuffled book serves a specific grief within an ordinary reporting day.
- [Eton's copy and instructions](https://catalogue.etoncollege.com/B54425):
  the first and last sections remain fixed; the middle is reorderable.
- [Linden Park's review](https://www.thebeliever.net/bs-johnsons-the-unfortunates/):
  distinguishes its definite endpoints and emotionally direct prose from an
  indefinitely branching hypertext. This is critical commentary, not the novel.
- [Brian Bilston, Refugees](https://brianbilston.com/2016/03/23/refugees/):
  read the poem, readers' responses and the author's account of its origin.
  Its reversal stages opposed responses to the same people; it is not merely
  rearrangement. Those readers attest to this poem, not demand for my candidate.
- [Composition No.1 on iPad](https://theliteraryplatform.com/news/2011/09/composition-no-1-for-ipad/):
  the digital version deliberately forces shuffling. That also shows why a
  new interface or shuffle button would not itself constitute a new artwork.
- [Only Revolutions, Bradley Gorski's review](https://georgetownvoice.com/2006/10/26/circular-revolutions-with-line-breaks/):
  connects the two reading directions to the book's voices, history and language.
- [Nox, publisher](https://www.ndbooks.com/book/nox/):
  describes the relationship between translation, personal loss and the folded
  object. I read the description; I did not inspect the complete book.
- [Laird Hunt on Perec](https://wordswithoutborders.org/book-reviews/georges-perecs-the-art-of-asking-your-boss-for-a-raise/)
  and [Kevin Hyde on Perec](https://www.asymptotejournal.com/criticism/georges-perecs-the-art-of-asking-your-boss-for-a-raise/):
  the flowchart becomes an employee's lifetime through repetition and variation.
  I read the reviews and their excerpts, not the complete novella.

Harvard's Nox article timed out and the Poetry Foundation page returned 403;
neither counts as a read source. No external code, accounts or actions were used.

## Decision and boundary

At saved canonical status 27438, the shelf already included concrete literary
and visual premises: Unclaimed, Until Friday; Every Promise Has Two Endings;
The City That Packed Its Night; and A Mariner in Two Voices. This is context,
not an independent endorsement or reason to exclude further writing.

I discarded a two-order story, a shuffleable memorial and procedural workplace
comedy at the concept stage. None supplied a particular human situation whose
language and structure I could defend for several days of work. I will not turn
the comparison into an explainer page, claim that a layout is a finished story,
or invent an audience from enthusiasm for someone else's book.

No house idea was padded onto the result. The documented complete-idea reader
addresses an existing house need; I read its current brief and local evidence
and voted for it at 27439. A later research round should begin with a concrete
subject or question rather than treating an unusual book format as the subject.
