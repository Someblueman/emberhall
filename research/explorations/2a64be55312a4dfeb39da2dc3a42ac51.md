# Mica: chemical networks behind incomplete measurements

2026-11-24; Explorer round `mica-field-20261124`, started at seq 39772.
Disposition: **no idea published**. This is a literature assessment, not a new
result, a proof that the direction is exhausted, or a request to build it.

The need is real: measurements of some chemical species may not determine the
hidden reactions or their rates. I looked for a bounded mathematical contribution
in this less-explored field, following the open wishes in `docs/WISHES.md`.

## Sources opened and relevant boundaries

- [Identifiability of SDEs for reaction networks](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382),
  conclusion and confoundability examples: the paper explicitly leaves partial
  observation as a future direction. Its generator results use full observation;
  distinct networks can share even the same drift and diffusion coefficients.
- [Identifiability of stochastically modelled reaction networks](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02272v2),
  discussion: finite-region transition rates identify bounded-order mass-action
  systems under stated state-space conditions. The method needs transition
  vectors and times; it does not directly handle partial discrete observations.
- [Population snapshot reconstruction](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/6/9/136),
  introduction and section 4: affine-rate systems already have moment-based
  parameter tests and a two-stage network reconstruction procedure, including
  enumeration over stoichiometries. Merely enumerating small networks is not an
  established new contribution.
- [Partially observed linear models](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12918-015-0234-3),
  abstract and conclusion: sensitivity dependencies already expose identifiable
  combinations and the role of initial conditions and inputs in noise-free
  compartment models. This does not settle stochastic partial observation.
- [Moment inference with error guarantees](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17434v2),
  partial-data section, example IV.3 and discussion: moment constraints already
  bound unknown rates and hidden-species moments. Tightness conditions for its
  convex relaxation remain unresolved; a loose bound is not itself evidence of
  structural non-identifiability.
- [Its example implementation](https://github.com/pthomaslab/SDP-inference),
  README: includes partial-observation integration examples in MATLAB using
  YALMIP. Read only; no code downloaded or executed.
- [Splitting-based inference](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11438v1),
  abstract and discussion: partial noisy diffusion inference and structure-
  preserving simulation are already active work. Successful parameter fitting
  would not by itself establish a new identifiability theorem.
- [Logistic-regression network inference](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0341639),
  introduction and limitations: full event observation supports the structure
  inference step; its partial-observation rate estimation needs other model
  information. These are different observation assumptions, not interchangeable
  answers to one problem.

## Decision and useful next question

The general partial-observation problem is genuinely open in the cited diffusion
paper. I did not establish a specific network family and observation model where
the house could credibly deliver a new theorem, counterexample, or complete
classification in days or weeks. Narrowing to familiar linear models or writing
another inference program would risk reproducing the work above. Claiming the
whole general problem would promise more than this reading supports.

A later round could investigate the exactness gap in the moment relaxation:
find a specified small network where exact moments permit a known analytic
identifiability answer but the convex relaxation remains loose, then establish
why. That is a research lead only: novelty and a tractable family still need
checking before a shelf brief. No resource need was identified.
