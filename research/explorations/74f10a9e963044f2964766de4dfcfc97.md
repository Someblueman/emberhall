## Candidate: determine the nineteen-square torus

Find the largest equal pair of queen armies on a 19 by 19 board with opposite
edges joined, where opposite colours never attack. Same-colour attacks are allowed.
The intended result is an exact value, with a placement, an exhaustive upper-bound
argument and independently checkable computation. A search program alone is not it.

Sources actually opened:

- [Clinch et al., current v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06974v3), introduction,
  odd-board construction, relaxation and appendix. It supplies a 32-per-colour
  example at order 19, not an optimality proof. Its asymptotic and empirical
  statements must not be mistaken for exact finite answers.
- [Davies, 20 November preprint](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00729v1),
  especially sections 6 and 7. Explicitly leaves the torus at order 19 unknown;
  says its root bound often fails for odd boards. Its ordinary-board answers
  and even-board confirmations do not settle this target. Claims are unrefereed.
- [Davies's repository](https://github.com/jasondavies/peaceable-queens), README:
  existing line-colouring/min-cut method, verification scope and substantial
  compute costs. Opened, not installed or run. A raw source fetch failed.
- [Harries's exact even-torus release](https://github.com/jphme/math-problems/tree/main/peaceable-queens-even-torus),
  README: the even case is already covered, including a formal proof; it also
  reports exact odd values at 15 and 17. Do not propose these as new.
- [Harries's author page](https://jph.me/) corroborates the even-case scope.

Three-day feasibility gate: independently derive the finite model, recover small
known values, freeze the strongest current order-19 bounds and measure a complete
small odd case. Continue only with a new valid bound/reduction and a conservative
estimate that a complete order-19 run fits existing resources. No cloud spending,
new package installation or extrapolated proof of completeness is implied.
One-week stop if the gap has no credible closing route. If continued, allow weeks
for mathematical reductions, search implementation and a separate verifier.
Novelty remains conditional on a fresh literature check at project start.

## Candidate: the missing edge in a three-dimensional grid

For every even side length at least four, decide whether the fewest edges in a
grid subgraph that first acquires a subdivided tetrahedron after *any* missing
grid edge is added equals the number of vertices plus one or plus two. Here a
subdivided tetrahedron means four branch vertices joined by six internally
disjoint paths. The output is a theorem and certificates, not a web simulation.

Sources actually opened:

- [Miller and Yane, published paper](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%20123/saturation-of-k_4-subdivisions-in-multidimensional-grids.pdf),
  sections 1.2, 2, 3 and conclusion. Corollary 3.2 leaves exactly this one-edge
  gap; its construction gives the larger bound. The two-dimensional result
  and higher-dimensional upper bounds do not close it. PDF screenshots failed;
  the accessible text and publisher HTML were read.
- [Publisher HTML](https://combinatorialpress.com/jcmcc-articles/volume-123/saturation-of-k_4-subdivisions-in-multidimensional-grids/)
  supplies the same definitions and scope; it is not a second independent result.
- [Ferrara et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jgt.21625), abstract:
  subdivision saturation in complete hosts; a different problem from grid hosts.
- [Minimum-saturation survey](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/DS19),
  abstract: the field's extremal question and definition. Not evidence of mass demand.
- [Miller's related mesh paper](https://sites.miamioh.edu/millerz/files/2025/03/a-saturaion-problem-on-meshes-revision.pdf),
  introduction and final bounds: stars are the forbidden graphs there, not tetrahedra.
- [Author's publication list](https://sites.miamioh.edu/millerz/publications/):
  checked for related work; absence from a list is not proof of novelty.
- [Demaine et al., induced-tree partitioning](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/YinYang_CCCG2021/paper.pdf),
  introduction: preassigned two-tree partitions of planar grids are hard.
  This neither decides our three-dimensional case nor proves it intractable.

Three-day gate: derive and check the structure forced by the lower-bound edge
count, reconstruct the published upper-bound example and make the smallest even
cube decidable within a fixed local budget. A possible route is classifying
two-cycle cores and attached trees; sufficiency and completeness are unproved.
Continue toward an all-even-size theorem only if the reduction produces either
a scalable construction or a structural obstruction. Stop after one week without
such a route. Finite failures alone never prove the universal result.

Possible structural route, not a proved reduction: a lower-bound candidate has
cycle rank two. Test whether saturation forces a theta core, junctions at grid
corners, and three induced trees attached to its three branches. Every direction
of that proposed equivalence needs proof before it can constrain an exhaustive
search. It is a research lead, not a result of this round.
