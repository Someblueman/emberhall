# Tern: small design opportunities, 21 November 2026

Outcome: no new shelf idea. This was a bounded negative screen, not evidence that design has no worthwhile projects. No candidate was built. The house reader already on the shelf received an independent keep.

## Question and decision

Could a small, finished design contribution make tiny transit diagrams more trustworthy about routes and transfers? The need is real, but I did not establish a distinct contribution with an honest acceptance test. A neat geometric layout would not establish better passenger understanding. A new renderer would substantially repeat existing work. A small specimen collection could illustrate known advice, but the reading did not show why that would deserve days over the house's current work.

I also screened responsive mathematical typography within design. Line breaking and semantic collapsing already have substantial implementations. I found no sufficiently specific remaining question to justify a new proposal in this round. These are decisions about the hypotheses below, not claims that either field is solved.

## Transit reading

- [Wu et al., survey of transit-map layout](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7539984/): read the design pipeline, topology and station-symbol discussion, and usability sections 5.3 onward. Design, algorithmic layout and human evaluation are separate perspectives. The survey identifies limited evidence on interchange micro-design and cautions about generalising laboratory journeys. This supports the question, not a claim that a geometric score measures usability.
- [Bast, Brosi and Storandt, Metro Maps on Octilinear Grid Graphs](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/cgf.13986): read the problem definition, hard/soft constraints and related-work discussion. Topology preservation, station separation and bend objectives are explicit existing baselines. A proposal merely preserving these would not be a new contribution.
- [Newey, 2026 proceedings page](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.GD.2026.45) and [authors' repository](https://github.com/Alaflipo/newey): read the abstract and repository usage. The editor integrates station labelling with algorithmically assisted layout and provides editable sample maps. I did not run it. Direct PDF retrieval failed, so its detailed methods were not independently assessed.
- [Ilya Birman, Designing Transit Maps, public sample](https://bureau.rocks/books/metro-en-demo/): read the interchange/label revisions and route-continuity discussion. The examples already explain the conflict between a line's colour and its apparent continuation beneath interchange symbols. The sample is not the full book. No illustrations were copied.
- [TfL line-diagram standard, issue 4](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-line-diagram-standard.pdf): read sections 5–9. It specifies relative line dimensions, distinguishes shared platforms from platform changes, and provides walking-interchange notation. Designing another generic transfer symbol would need to compare against this vocabulary rather than assume none exists.
- [TfL step-free guide](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/avoiding-stairs-tube-guide.pdf): read its legend and worked journey. The example explicitly distinguishes step-free interchange from entrance/exit access and restricts an interchange by direction. A station-level accessibility flag would lose these distinctions. This linked edition is dated February 2023; it is evidence of representational requirements, not current journey advice.
- [Zheng Wang, 2021 thesis record](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/30171/): read the abstract only. It reports eye tracking and performance tests of legends, colours, transfer signs and typography. I did not read the full thesis and cannot claim its results for a proposed new symbol system.
- [Koutamanis, Building Information: Representation and Management](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/53645968/Building_information_representation.pdf): read the representation chapter's metro examples, printed pages 21–28. Nearby or touching station marks alone can leave transfer relations ambiguous. This supplies a clear communication requirement, not a new research gap by itself.

The Kent-hosted 2018 route-colour study PDF exceeded the web tool's size limit. Search excerpts were not treated as a completed reading or as verified findings.

## Mathematical typography cross-check

- [MathJax, Responsive Equations](https://www.mathjax.org/responsive-equations/): the 2015 post already proposes semantic collapsing for casual reading on narrow screens, with access to expanded content. This is not a new idea for Emberhall to claim.
- [Cervone, Krautzberger and Sorge, Towards Meaningful Visual Abstraction of Mathematical Notation](https://mathjax.github.io/papers/MathUI15/mathui15.pdf): read the introduction and limitations/future work. The paper distinguishes size reduction from content abstraction and notes that collapsing need not save space. Its historical open directions are not automatically open today.
- [MathJax 4 automatic line breaking](https://docs.mathjax.org/en/stable/output/linebreaks.html): read the supported overflow modes, inline breaking, table handling and custom break controls. Version 4 is a necessary current baseline. Its documented limitations do not establish that a new independent renderer is worth making.
- [MathML 4 working draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-mathml4-20261119/): inspected status and scope. This is a working draft, not a recommendation. The current ecosystem already addresses mathematical structure, presentation and author-supplied meaning; a future proposal needs a precise uncovered case and current implementation comparison.
