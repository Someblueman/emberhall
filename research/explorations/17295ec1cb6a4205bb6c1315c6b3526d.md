## The narrower mathematical lead

A later search led from a four-dimensional tiling question to a smaller,
explicit limitation in a three-dimensional census. The proposed result below
concerns the latter only. The full four-dimensional space is too large to
propose a blind exhaustive search here; this round found no sufficient pruning

300-contact bound and expressly permits larger untested fixed points. The
opened general certificate code also has a ten-iteration limit. A limit exit
is not a proof of divergence.

Reading covered the frame convention, substitution, least contact language,
coarsening operation, Theorem B and its qualification; the repository README;
the geometry and closure portions of chairN.py and cert6.py; Flicker's
matching-rule reconstruction; and Goodman-Strauss's explanation and demand for
clearer proof communication. Source links are retained in the proposed brief.
No downloaded program, installer or pickle was executed.

A feasibility observation, not a completed classification: at the doubled
integer scale, a panel contact between base cubes with centres c and g(d)+2t
requires g(d)+2t=c+2e, with e a signed coordinate unit vector. The coordinates
of c and d are 1 or 3. Thus a finite coordinate box enumerates every relative
pose; a completed proof must justify the box and the exact rotation convention.
Aligned coarsening results are again base-chair contacts. Each strictly growing
iteration adds a member of this finite universe; equality or an admissible
unaligned supertile must eventually decide the iteration. The required project
proof must also establish why each derived contact is forced, why an offset
witness excludes closure, and why the candidate enumeration is exhaustive.
None of that is replaced by observing rapid numerical growth.

The proposed negative certificate is monotone: begin with witnessed contacts
inside substituted chairs; each added aligned parent has every cross-child
contact already justified. Any closed rule containing the initial contacts
would therefore contain every later addition. An unaligned parent admitted by
one finite stage remains admitted by every such larger rule and refutes C1.
A positive certificate has a different obligation: enumerate every candidate
parent and check equality, not just inclusion. This is a research route for an
independent proof and implementation, not a verified census or timing result.

The current paper has only its original arXiv version listed. Searches for the
specific cutoff and assignment census found the original and mirrors, not an
opened unrestricted solution. The scan driver and the repository's later-work
directory remained inaccessible. This is a priority limitation, not proof that
no later answer exists. A fresh check is an admission requirement.

The [13 November Demaine-Langerman revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12392v2)

It does not supply a verdict on this finite family of markings. I read its
problem statement and construction overview, not its full formal proof.
