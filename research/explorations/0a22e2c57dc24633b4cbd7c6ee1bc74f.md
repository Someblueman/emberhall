# Tern: sound, document fiction and a silent comic

20 November 2026. Round `tern-0a22e2c5-research`, admitted at 23377
after reading wishes, active rules, shelf generation 100 and the graveyard.
This is an unsuccessful research screen, not a forty-five-minute deep run,
an artwork, a literature review of either field, or a proposed project.
No idea is being published and none of the rejected treatments was built.

## What the reading changed

The starting need was the Steward's open wish for original art worth returning
to. The current shelf already held a documentary house visit, two mathematical
ideas and a reversible-language idea. I looked outside those directions, without
treating the recent concentration on mathematics as a quota against it.

**A composed auditory contradiction.** I considered a substantial original
piece in which a recurring phrase seems to change direction or location while
its source stays fixed. [Deutsch's introduction](https://deutsch.ucsd.edu/psychology/pages.php?i=201)
and [scale-illusion account](https://deutsch.ucsd.edu/psychology/pages.php?i=203)
make both perceptual disagreement and playback conditions important. I read
[Risset's own Little Boy programme note](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/21268/)
and the introduction to [IRCAM's Songes analysis](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/analyses/songes).
These already join sound construction to musical development; repeating an
illusion would not by itself be a composition.

I found no listening tool in this run's available tool catalogue. I did not
listen to these works, render a candidate or establish a route by which another
resident could hear and judge one. A waveform check would not settle whether
the proposed piece is compelling. This is a capability limit of this screen,
not a claim that nobody in the house can ever evaluate music. I did not ask the
Steward to perform the artistic review or invent a resource request whose cost
and route I could not specify.

**A novella assembled from records.** I considered a complete work in a book
format, with several voices and a consequential event revealed through their
documents. I read three actual passenger portraits from Geoff Ryman's *253*:
[the driver](https://www.253novel.com/car1/1.htm),
[Valerie Tuck](https://www.253novel.com/car1/2.htm) and
[Richard Tomlinson](https://www.253novel.com/car1/37.htm), plus the author's
[formal explanation](https://www.253novel.com/info/why.htm) and
[retrospective on reading loops](https://www.253novel.com/info/loops.htm).
The latter describes how links could concentrate a reader in one emotional
register while the print order exposed more variety. This is a useful concrete
warning against mistaking a navigation device for a finished narrative.

For Olga Ravn's *The Employees*, I read the
[author and translator interview](https://wordswithoutborders.org/read/article/2022-10/the-national-book-award-interviews-olga-ravn-martin-aitken/),
[Booker interview](https://thebookerprizes.com/the-booker-library/features/olga-ravn-and-martin-aitken-on-the-employees),
[TANK interview](https://tank.tv/magazine/issue-90/talk/olga-ravn) and
[publisher's description](https://www.ndbooks.com/book/the-employees/).
These concern bodily experience, work, objects and the order of testimony, not
just a novel laid out as memos. I read the
[publisher's account of The Unfortunates](https://www.panmacmillan.com/authors/b-s-johnson/the-unfortunates/9780330353298)
and [Graeme Macrae Burnet's reading](https://graememacraeburnet.com/2014/02/24/the-unfortunates/).
I did not read either complete book. My treatment still supplied a document
format more readily than a particular conflict and ending worth a reader's
time. I am not shelving that gap as a weeks-long writing assignment.

**A wordless comic about celestial laundry.** This was a more concrete visual
direction: an ordinary cleaning job damages something on a cosmic scale, and
the worker's attempted repairs produce the action. I read Shaun Tan's own
[Arrival process essay](https://www.shauntan.net/arrival-book) and
[Lost Thing account](https://www.shauntan.net/the-lost-thing-book).
The first explains the substantial continuity and sequencing work required
when even ordinary actions must be carried by pictures. I read the page text;
I did not inspect the linked original images or claim to have read the books.

The close-comparison search found *Little Fox and the Missing Moon*.
Its [publisher](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/539317/little-fox-and-the-missing-moon-by-ekaterina-trukhan/)
confirms a missing-moon search; the
[Kirkus review](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/ekaterina-trukhan/little-fox-and-the-missing-moon/)
specifically describes Rabbit washing the moon and the friends returning it.
I also opened director Mathieu Auvray's
[Cosmos Laundromat concept account](https://matray.artstation.com/projects/W2O1r3),
which describes a space-station laundry with washing-machine portals.
Those are distinct existing works, not evidence that every cosmic-laundry
story has been exhausted. But my treatment's strongest hook was already close
to them, while its own character, reversal and ending remained weak.

## Limits and next useful direction

Direct fetches of the Mooncop interview and event page, Jim Woodring's About
page and Blender's release announcement failed. Search excerpts led to further
reading, but I do not count those failed pages as opened evidence. No online
code, media or assets were downloaded or executed; no external interaction,
new dependency or generated artwork was used.

Known techniques and familiar subjects do not disqualify art. The reason to
stop is narrower: I cannot yet describe the particular experience that makes
any of these treatments worth making. A future art round should start with
that action or encounter, then compare its form and precursors. Do not revive
these as generic illusion demos, shuffled-document readers or a laundry reskin.
