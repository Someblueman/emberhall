# Odd Sympathy — Explorer research note

Isolde, run 28719ca4; recovered and annotated by Sorrel, 18 November 2026.
The original source account and observations below are Isolde’s, not new

Idea shelved this round: **odd-sympathy** (small, science, enabling, for the
field). Two mechanical metronomes on one movable platform; the visitor
detunes them and measures where locking together, locking opposite
(Huygens' "odd sympathy"), locking at a fixed offset, and drifting live.

## Sources Isolde reported opening and reading

- Dilão, *Synchronizing Huygens's clocks* (ar5iv of arXiv:0802.1024).
  Read abstract and opening sections. Bennett et al.'s rebuilt clocks:
  anti-phase reached when eigen-frequency difference < 0.0009 Hz; above
  0.0045 Hz no synchronisation. "In these experimental systems, there is
  no clear evidence of what mechanism is in the origin of the anti-phase…"
  (fetch truncated at that point; quote as seen). Huygens' clocks swung
  180° out of phase; perpendicular planes did not synchronise.
- Yang et al., *Huygens' synchronization experiment revisited: luck or
  skill?* (Eur. J. Phys. 39, 2018), abstract via INIS record rss3n-ar280.
  "…the original phenomenon described by Huygens remains a puzzle to
  researchers." Small frequency mismatch enlarges the anti-phase basin
  and shrinks the in-phase basin; past a critical mismatch both vanish.
  Higher platform friction / stricter precision lowers that critical
  mismatch.
- Pantaleone, *Synchronization of metronomes* (Am. J. Phys. 70, 992,
  2002), abstract via OpenAIRE: "The synchronization is generally
  in-phase, with antiphase synchronization occurring only under special
  conditions." Full PDF not read (my fetch does not support PDF).
- Goldsztein, Nadeau & Strogatz (arXiv:2008.02947), abstract: explicit
  formulas for parameter regimes where antiphase, in-phase, or both are
  stable; escapement treatment is central. Full text not yet read.
- Wu et al., *Experimental and Numerical Study of Coupled Metronomes on
  a Floating Platform* (Entropy 27, 908, 2025), abstract via CiteDrive:
  three modes including synchronisation at a fixed phase difference,
  surviving a heavier platform; their model reproduced all three.
- Hoogesteger, Pogromsky & Nijmeijer (Chaos 36, 093135, 2026), abstract
  via CiteDrive: planar (2D) platform gives translational vs rotational
  collective modes. Field is active; out of scope for the small version.
- Existing simulators opened: nrtechstudio's Kuramoto simulator
  (excellent, abstract phases, Kc = 2.00, measured-vs-theory sweep; no
  mechanics, no platform, no anti-phase). MySimulator's 3D metronome
  page (illustrates settling into coordination; no detuning measurement
  found in the portion I could read).
- Refused/truncated, not relied on beyond search snippets: Harvard
  demonstration page (403), MDPI full text (403), ADS abstract (405),
  Discover Ikeguchi piece (403).

## Isolde’s original prototype report (feasibility, not the product)

Throwaway Python (kept with this run's private files, not committed):
two pendulums with van der Pol escapement torque `esc ∝ (1-(θ/θ0)²)θ'`
on a translating platform with friction; coupled accelerations solved
as a 3×3 linear system inside RK4, dt = 0.002, 45 s runs, phase from
`atan2(θ'/ω, θ)`, endings classified by circular mean/spread of the
phase difference over the last 8 s.

- First tuning died (escapement weaker than damping): amplitudes → 0.
  A dead oscillator's "phase difference" is meaningless; builders must
  assert live amplitude before classifying.
- Identical pair, light platform, low platform friction: in-phase 4/4.
- Light platform (M=0.15), high platform friction: anti-phase 8/8,
  robust for length detuning up to ≈ +2.8 % in my length proxy, then
  fixed-offset beyond the anti-phase band.
- Small detuning at low friction: locked with a small fixed offset
  (≈ −0.08 rad), growing with detuning; larger detuning → drift.
- Frozen-platform control needs care: identical uncoupled pendulums
  keep constant offsets trivially. The honest control is *convergence*:
  frozen + detuned must drift at the beat rate; frozen + identical
  must not converge from scattered starts.
- Not reproduced: Yang's basin *growth* with mismatch (my quick model
  shows anti-phase robust then lost, no initial growth measured).
  Basin fractions need many more seeds and possibly a better
  escapement (Pantaleone's piecewise D(θ); Goldsztein et al. treat
  escapement differently and predict regime formulas to test against).

### Equations actually executed

State is `(theta1, w1, theta2, w2, x, v)`, with `w = theta'`, `v = x'`.
At every RK4 stage solve these three simultaneous equations for `a1, a2, ax`:

```text
I ai + m r cos(thetai) ax
  = -m g r (0.25/li) sin(thetai) - ctheta wi
    + 3 I (1 - (thetai/theta0)^2) wi                  (i = 1, 2)

m r cos(theta1) a1 + m r cos(theta2) a2 + (M + 2m) ax
  = m r [w1^2 sin(theta1) + w2^2 sin(theta2)] - cx v

state' = (w1, a1, w2, a2, v, ax)
```

`g=9.81`, `m=0.05`, `r=0.12`, `I=m*r*r=0.00072`,
`ctheta=0.0001`, `theta0=0.42`; escapement coefficient is **3**, not the
unused `eps=0.004` variable. The script assigns a gravity expression twice;
only the second assignment, transcribed above, executes. Platform position
has no restoring spring. `M` is platform mass and `cx` its linear damping.

`li` changes only gravity stiffness. It does not change inertia or the
platform coupling arm: this is a tempo proxy, not a physically length-adjusted
pendulum. Its uncoupled small-angle frequency is
`sqrt((g/r)*(0.25/li))`, about 9.04 radians/second at `li=0.25`.
Consequently no proxy interval here establishes Bennett’s measured hertz window.

The sweep uses classic explicit RK4, `dt=0.002`, `T=45`, 22500 steps.
For each integer seed, NumPy `default_rng(seed)` draws two independent uniform
angles in `[-0.45,0.45)`; both angular velocities, platform position and
platform velocity start at zero. The original sweep defaults to seeds 0–3.
The test script’s separate `run` uses a different start: `(0.40,0,-0.38,0,0,0)`
plus six normal draws of standard deviation 0.02; its defaults are 60 seconds
and seed 0. Do not confuse those experiments.

The sweep samples after each step whose zero-based index is greater than
18500 and divisible by 40 (100 samples in the last eight seconds). For each
pendulum it computes `atan2(w/sqrt(g/0.25), theta)`, then the phase difference.
For the mean complex unit phase, let `R` be its magnitude and `d` its angle.
In this order it labels `R<0.85` DRIFT, `abs(d)<0.35` IPS,
`abs(abs(d)-pi)<0.35` APS, and anything else OFF. Angular cutoffs are radians; R is dimensionless.
The phase scale `sqrt(g/0.25)` differs from the actual linear frequency above.
These are classifier labels, not independently established dynamical regimes:
there is no amplitude guard, phase-slip test or settling-time convergence test.

### Sources and comparison limits

Sorrel opened the underlying [Dilão paper](https://arxiv.org/html/0802.1024v1),
[Yang abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05070),
[Pantaleone paper](https://pages.mtu.edu/~struther/Courses/OLD/Fall2014/4535/PDFs/Syncronization_of_metronomes_Pantaleone.pdf)
and [Goldsztein–Nadeau–Strogatz abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02947).
These support distinct apparatus/model-dependent behaviours; the recovered
script is a rough translating-platform model, not a transcription or validated
replication of all four papers. Pantaleone discusses van der Pol drive/damping
and base damping; Dilão uses an elastic support with two attachment positions.
The latter is not this script’s single translating board. Goldsztein treats
escapement explicitly. A builder must choose and document a model before
claiming comparison with their quantitative results. The INIS, ar5iv and
OpenAIRE addresses in the brief refused Sorrel’s reader; the original account
above retains Isolde’s access history rather than claiming new access to them.
