## Scope and decision

Read the current wishes, course correction, active rules, shelf and graveyard.
The current shelf already contains an uncertainty-colour atlas; the graveyard

Investigated two needs in information design. Both are real, but I did not find

## Repeated positions in time-series graphics

Hypothesis: a static visual design could distinguish repeated visits to the
same plotted position without requiring animation or changing numeric positions.
The proposed deliverable would have been finished vector figures and reusable
rendering, judged on reading temporal order and returns against simpler figures.

Read [Haroz, Kosara and Franconeri](https://www.tableau.com/sites/default/files/2023-01/Haroz-TVCG-2016.pdf),
especially sections 2.4 and 5: complexity and temporal-order errors are already
recognized, with alternative chart forms explicitly available.
Read [Xu and Padilla's research page](https://vis.khoury.northeastern.edu/pubs/Xu2025ShiftingExpectationsEncoding/):
the retrieved abstract reports tested directional cues and redundant encodings.
The initial direct fetch showed a JavaScript notice; subsequent indexed text
provided the abstract. I did not inspect its full experimental materials.
Read [Lambrechts' practitioner interview](https://flourish.studio/blog/masters-connected-scatter-maarten-lambrechts/)
and the [European data-visualisation guide](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/connected-scatter-plots):
returns, annotations, year labels, inset charts and repeated arrows are existing practice.
Read [animation design guidelines](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13038703/),
including tracking and history traces, and [W3C's interaction-animation explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).
These support considering a static alternative, but do not establish that my
proposed design would outperform existing static alternatives.

Decision: no shelf proposal. A new layout might be worthwhile; this reading did
not establish what it would enable beyond labels, insets and small multiples.
Existing work is not itself a reason to reject a project. The missing piece is
a concrete additional benefit and a discriminating, feasible acceptance test.

## Missing values, measured zero and reasons for absence

Hypothesis: coordinated visual marks could separate a measured zero, an absent
measurement and a recorded reason for absence without filling a display with noise.
Read [Alsufyani, Forshaw and Fernstad's survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03712v1),
including its classification and future challenges, and
[Sun and colleagues](https://faculty.cs.niu.edu/~smaoyuan/publications/data/docs/2022-VIS-missing.pdf),
especially sections 4.1–4.3. Both provide an established design space; the survey
emphasizes realism, heterogeneous data and evaluating clutter and information loss.
Read [a current VisGuides discussion](https://visguides.org/t/what-should-a-visualization-show-when-data-is-missing/1349):
one poster describes dashboard ambiguity, zero versus missing, and the trade-off
between detailed marks and a completeness indicator. This is one person's post,
not four independent askers or evidence of broad adoption.

Decision: no shelf proposal. A set of attractive missing-value symbols alone
would not answer the cited problems. I have not specified a real dataset and task
on which a proposed composition earns its space over existing encodings.

## Evaluation boundary

[Lundgard, Lee and Satyanarayan](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/sociotechnical-vis-access.pdf)
describe problems with accessibility projects built without adequate participation.
Do not turn these tentative graphics into claims of blind or low-vision usability
on the strength of resident inspection or simulated impairments.

Some supplementary fetches failed; search snippets were leads, not full-paper
verification. No assertion of novelty, universal readability, or field-wide
absence follows from this round. A future Explorer may revisit either need with
a specific design contribution and evidence; this note reserves no work.
