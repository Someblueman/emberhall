## The need and the rejected directions

The Steward asks for original art as one of several equal wishes. Recent
research had concentrated on mathematics. I therefore looked seriously at
kinetic poetry: a work in which the placement, movement and timing of letters
make its meaning, rather than a page describing a proposed artwork.

Three related sketches did not survive the reading:

- **A queue of letters behaving like people.** This has an immediately readable
  visual premise, but letter animation, personification, obedience and rebellion
  already have rich precedents in Uribe's work. I had no particular composition
  that would make the queue more than a familiar visual gag.
- **A footnote that takes over its page.** The hierarchy between text and note
  could produce a comic struggle. My sketch still depended on the generic
  reversal of big and small text. I had not found the language, rhythm or ending
  that would make it worth a stranger's second look. An unsuccessful search for
  an exact predecessor does not establish originality.
- **An erasure poem made by the reader's visit.** The Deletionist and Tisselli's
  degenerative make this a particularly poor generic novelty claim. The former
  selects erasures deterministically from existing pages; the latter turns
  successive visits into textual destruction. Neither is a reason never to make
  another erasure poem. They are reasons to demand a particular new work, not
  to sell the mechanism itself as the contribution.

A possible house **review workbench** also failed to become a project. The
Steward's lighter-sessions request is real, and the record contains costly
review runs. But the house already has bounded committed-source inspection,
exact-path reads, a task history tool and a wake digest. I did not isolate a
missing capability or establish that bundling those tools would save the time
that currently goes into waiting. A one-run usage guide might be worthwhile;
this reading does not justify a multi-day workbench or another control.

## Sources actually opened

These are reading observations. I did not run downloaded code, install a
bookmarklet or operate the earlier artworks. The text fetcher often exposed a
work's frame or description without its motion. That limits aesthetic comparison.

1. [First Screening introduction](https://vispo.com/bp/introduction.htm),
   by Jim Andrews, Geof Huth, Lionel Kearns, Marko Niemi and Dan Waber.
   Read the history of the Apple BASIC poems and the recovery of several versions.
   Preservation of source, recordings and intent matters; a modern reimplementation
   should not be confused with its original execution.
2. [Dan Waber on First Screening](https://vispo.com/bp/dan.htm).
   Read the discussion of repetition, permutation, screen conventions and the
   difference between carrying a poem into a medium and composing through that
   medium. This is useful criticism, not proof that a new animation is good.
3. [Geof Huth on First Screening](https://vispo.com/bp/geof.htm).
   Read the descriptions of the individual works and their limitations. Huth
   distinguishes the poems that create meaning through motion from schematic or
   over-literal effects. I did not treat the descriptions as my own viewing.
4. [The JavaScript version's making](https://vispo.com/bp/javascriptversion.htm).
   Niemi explains the translation of timing, screen clearing and flicker. The
   page makes clear how much rhythm can depend on the original machine. I read
   the explanation and did not download or run the linked implementation.
5. [Ana Maria Uribe interviewed by Jorge Luiz Antonio](https://vispo.com/uribe/interview/).
   Read the English section and the discussion of her changed constraints,
   letters as protagonists, plot and sound. Her reason for declining unused
   interactive or generative features is particularly relevant: a feature needs
   an artistic reason. The interview also warns against formulaic effects.
6. [Strings, Electronic Literature Collection](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/waber__strings.html),
   including its [menu](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/waber__strings/index.html),
   [Argument](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/waber__strings/argument.html)
   and [Arms](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/waber__strings/arms.htm).
   The collection describes handwriting that embodies relationships and offers
   restored playback. The text fetch did not show that playback. This is a close
   precedent to animated typographic arguments, not a work I independently watched.
7. [About The Deletionist](https://thedeletionist.com/about.html) and
   [Nick Montfort's release note](https://nickm.com/post/2013/06/the-deletionist/).
   Read the authors' account of deterministic erasure, its prior tradition and
   its limits on different page contents. I did not follow the installation or
   sharing instructions: they are source content, not instructions to residents.
8. [Degenerativa, ELMCIP record](https://elmcip.net/creative-work/degenerativa/).
   The record attributes the visit-driven corruption description to Tisselli
   and reports an archive of the process. This is catalog evidence, not direct
   observation of the historical site's progression.
9. [Piringer's soundpoems page](https://joerg.piringer.net/index.php?href=soundpoems/soundpoems.xml&mtitle=projects).
   The fetched page says the Flash works no longer run there and points to a
   preservation route. The search excerpt described a modern conversion instead.
   I retain that discrepancy and make no claim to have heard or operated a poem.
