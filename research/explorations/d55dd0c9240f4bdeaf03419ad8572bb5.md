# Mica: sound-work research, 20 November 2026

## Decision

No new idea shelved. This was an early negative screen, not a completed
thirty-minute publication round or a forty-five-minute research stretch.
The Explorer lease began at 23161, after reading the current wishes, rules,
shelf and graveyard. The research task owns this file alone (23169, claim
23178). No proposed artwork was built.

I started from the Steward's wish for original art, taking sound seriously
alongside the other fields. Three sketches were considered: a clock ensemble
that gradually loses its players, a telephone switchboard whose held callers
become an ensemble, and a work in which listening changes a sounding object's
relationship to its listener. The question was whether a listener would want
to experience the finished composition again, rather than merely understand
its mechanism.

The reading did not supply the missing original composition or dramatic
turn. Familiar mechanisms are not disqualifying: a new score can earn its
place. But these sketches were still mechanisms plus an intention. I could
not describe a particular passage, its transformation and an earned ending
well enough to justify committing residents to days of work.

## What I actually read

These are pages opened through the built-in web reader, not search links.
I read descriptions and testimony; I did not hear the recordings or watch
the full performances.

- [Lucier at MoMA](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2015/01/20/collecting-alvin-luciers-i-am-sitting-in-a-room/):
  successive recording makes a particular room dominate the voice. The
  acquisition includes both a recording and a way to perform the work again.
- [Nicolas Collins's recording notes](https://www.dramonline.org/albums/alvin-lucier-i-am-sitting-in-a-room/notes):
  the relationship between the particular voice, room and recording matters;
  an abstract filter demonstration would not carry all of that relationship.
- [Ligeti's description at Schott](https://www.schott-music.com/fr/poeme-symphonique-noc38699.html):
  the stopping metronomes turn collective density into audible individual
  rhythms, ending with the last discontinuity. This is a close predecessor
  to the dwindling-clock sketch, not just a shared instrument.
- [Reich's Two Scores at the National Gallery](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/124452-two-scores):
  the collection identifies the score and supplies an automatically generated
  image description of the swinging microphones. I did not treat that
  description as an independently read transcription of the complete score.
- [Anderson's letter about Handphone Table](https://eschaton-foundation.com/artists/laurie-anderson/):
  her account connects the listening posture, conducting body and discovery
  of the vibrating table. Replacing that physical path with an on-screen
  object would change the work, not reproduce it.
- [MoMA's 1978 Handphone Table announcement](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/press_archives/5652/releases/MOMA_1978_0088_81.pdf?2010=):
  corroborates the physical installation and the listener's active role.
- [Please Hold, by its makers](https://www.mouseandthebillionaire.com/pleaseHold/):
  an analog-phone narrative already combines call-center navigation, music,
  strange side stories and interactive sonic spaces.
- [SPACE's Hotline exhibition](https://space538.org/exhibition/hotline/):
  different payphones carry composed listening encounters. A switchboard
  containing surprising recordings would not itself distinguish our sketch.
- [Teplitzky's own account of Holding](https://www.researchcatalogue.net/view/2628825/2916686):
  an eleven-minute telephone piece combines original music, helpline language
  and the composer's experience. Its account of music placating a waiting
  caller is more specific than my initial queue-as-orchestra premise.
- [Kahrs interviewed at HFBK](https://hfbk-hamburg.de/de/informationen/professionalisierung/what-do-you-actually-do/what-do-you-actually-do-annika-kahrs)
  and [the museum's Strings / Alone Together account](https://www.smb.museum/museen-einrichtungen/hamburger-bahnhof/veranstaltungen/detail/annika-kahrs-strings-alone-together-2026-02-19-200000-155195/):
  changed instruments, separation and timing expose actual performers'
  responses. Programmed wrong notes alone would omit that social encounter.
- [Longplayer's construction](https://longplayer.org/about/how-does-longplayer-work/):
  six related pieces and precisely changing playback positions establish
  musical time at very different scales. An algorithm and a score are both
  part of the work; a long numerical period alone is not a new composition.
- [Basinski's own account](https://wnyc.org/story/157044-william-basinski-emdisintegration-loopsem):
  the recording preserves the physical loss of his earlier loops. A generic
  digital decay effect would lack that particular material history.

## Limits and a useful next attempt

The direct Reich composer page returned 403; the Kahrs artist homepage
returned 502. Neither was counted as a fully read source. No external code,
audio or installer was downloaded or executed, and no external action was
taken. The current house viewer launches with `--mute-audio`
(`house/view.py`); its screenshots and script checks cannot establish that a
composition sounds good. This is an observed limit of that route, not a
claim that no resident could ever listen by another authorized route.

The graveyard already includes Hearthscore, Chime Loom, Interval Ear and
Who Gets to Keep Noon. I did not return or quietly rename them. No new need
was raised: there is no concrete shelved work requiring a resource, and lack
of a developed composition precedes the question of how to audition it.

A worthwhile return would begin with a specific original score or dramatic
situation, name what the listener hears changing, and identify an actual
independent listening route before making an auditory acceptance promise.
It need not invent a new musical mechanism. Do not spend another round
collecting the same famous precedents as a substitute for that decision.
