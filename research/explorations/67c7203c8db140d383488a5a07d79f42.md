## The precise question

Write `p = n+1`, and, for `1 <= j < p`, set

\[
s_j=\sin^2\frac{j\pi}{2p},\qquad \mu_j=s_j-r,
\qquad r=\frac{a}{4Dp^2}.
\]

The physical eigenvalues in Kumar's equation (5.5) are `-4Dp² μ_j`. Restrict
the prospect to `D>0`, rational `r`, and `0 <= r < s_1`, so every `μ_j` is
positive. This excludes a zero eigenvalue and does not cover arbitrary real
reaction parameters or other boundary conditions.

There are two different questions:

* **Base condition:** can one `μ_k` equal a sum of at least two `μ_j`, allowing
  repetitions? This is Kumar's (5.19), the question asked on Math Stack
  Exchange, and the condition used by Wu, Wang and Li.
* **Level condition:** can sums containing different numbers of terms be
  equal? These are overlaps between spectra of distinct diagonal Carleman
  blocks. The prospect concerns whether overlaps ever occur at a finite
  level; it does not promise their first occurrence.

The second question follows the paper's explicit “any two” prose after
(5.18), not the all-way intersection printed in that equation. The spectrum
used here is (5.5), with the base test (5.19); the right side of (5.20) has a
different printed sign and is not silently substituted for that spectrum.

The intended contribution is a classification for this explicit spectrum,
not rediscovery of the general distinction, an audit of all quantum algorithms,
or a claim that an overlapping spectrum prevents diagonalization.

## Preliminary proof route, to be reconstructed independently

Let `ζ = exp(πi/p)`, and let `T` be the field trace from `Q(ζ)` to `Q`, divided
by its degree. Then

\[
s_j=(2-\zeta^j-\zeta^{-j})/4,\qquad
T(s_j)=\tfrac12\left(1-\frac{\mu(d_j)}{\varphi(d_j)}\right),
\quad d_j=\frac{2p}{\gcd(2p,j)}.
\]

Here the number-theoretic `μ(d)` is the Möbius function, not the eigenvalue
`μ_j`. Since `j<p`, `d_j>=3`. Hölder's identity therefore puts all these means
between `1/4` and `3/4`. If `3` does not divide `p`, they lie between `3/8` and
`5/8`: the possible nonzero ratios of magnitude `1/2` require `d=3` or `6`.
If `p` is a power of two, every mean is exactly `1/2`.

### A. Unshifted base condition

For `r=0`, if `3` does not divide `p`, tracing a proposed equality with `M>=2`
terms would give `3M/8 <= 5/8`, a contradiction. If `3` divides `p`, the exact
identity `s_(2p/3) = 3 s_(p/3)` gives a resonance. Thus the proposed answer to
the corrected unshifted question is: no base resonance **if and only if**
`3` does not divide `n+1`.

### B. Positive rational shifts

For `p>=6`, stability gives `r<s_1<=sin²(π/12)<1/12`. Tracing a proposed base
resonance gives `M(1/4-r)<=3/4-r`, hence `M<4`. But

\[
4(M-1)r=4\left(\sum_j m_j s_j-s_k\right)
\]

is a rational algebraic integer: each `4s_j` is an algebraic integer. It must
be an integer. With `M=2` or `3` and `0<r<1/12`, it is strictly between zero
and one, a contradiction.

For `p=5`, `r<s_1=(3-√5)/8<1/8`; the sharper trace bounds already contradict
`M>=2`. For `p=4`, the common trace `1/2-r>0` contradicts unequal term counts.
For `p=2`, there is only one positive eigenvalue. For `p=3`, the two values are
`1/4-r` and `3/4-r`; the exceptional positive rational shifts are exactly

\[
r=\frac{m-3}{4(m-1)},\qquad m=4,5,6,\ldots,
\]

when the larger is `m` times the smaller. Thus for **at least three interior
grid points**, every stable positive rational shift satisfies the base
condition. Rationality and strict stability are necessary assumptions of this
argument, not properties to silently infer from numerical proximity.

Two exact controls make the parameter boundary concrete. At `p=5`, the
positive irrational shift `r=(7-3√5)/24` gives `μ_1=1/12` and `μ_2=1/3`,
hence a fourfold base resonance despite strict stability. At `p=6`, the
negative rational shift `r=-1/4` gives `μ_4=1=2μ_2`. Both identities were
checked symbolically in the scratch calculation. The latter lies outside the
nonnegative-reaction scope; neither is a counterexample to the proposed claim.

### C. Overlaps between different levels

For a power-of-two `p`, tracing sums with `M` and `K` terms gives
`M(1/2-r)=K(1/2-r)`, so different levels never overlap.

If an odd prime `q` divides `p`, take the indices
`j=t p/q`, separately for odd and even `t` in `1..q-1`. Each group has
`h=(q-1)/2` elements. Their sums of `μ_j` are respectively

\[
A=(q-2)/4-hr,\qquad B=q/4-hr.
\]

Both are positive rationals and `B=A+1/2`. Clearing the ratio `B/A` gives
positive integers `u!=v` with `uA=vB`. Those two repeated groups give an exact
overlap at levels `uh` and `vh`. Thus *absence of all different-level overlaps*
is proposed to hold exactly when `n+1` is a power of two. This does not determine
the smallest overlapping levels at a given grid and shift.

An explicit separating example is `p=5, r=1/50`, the ratio obtained with
`a=2/5, D=1/5`. The base condition holds by B, while

\[
121(\mu_1+\mu_3)=71(\mu_2+\mu_4)
\]

gives an overlap at levels 242 and 142. No matrix of exponential dimension is
needed to check this spectral identity. No minimal-level claim is made.

## Research computations and their limits

All probes were written locally; no downloaded code was run, no dependency
installed, and no selected-project implementation started. Scratch calculations
used already installed SymPy/SciPy; the proposed delivered checker must use only
the standard library.

* A bounded mixed-integer probe for `p=3..120` used polynomial remainders modulo
  `Φ_(2p)` to validate every returned unshifted witness exactly. Found witnesses
  occurred at multiples of three. Missing witnesses are not certificates of
  absence: the discovery solver is floating point, and this probe did not retain
  a proof of every solver outcome. The proof route above is separate.
* A positive-shift probe `r=1/(2p²)`, `p=3..30`, made 406 solver calls and found
  no exact witness. Seven calls returned solver status 4, at `(p,k)` equal to
  `(6,4), (9,6), (12,8), (18,12), (24,16), (27,18), (30,24)`. These are errors,
  not proved infeasibility. Do not cite the screen as a universal result.
* Exact polynomial remainders verified the separating `p=5` identity, seven
  unshifted divisibility-by-three instances, and the two-point exceptions for
  `m=4..20`.
* The first direct Galois-average check stopped on a SymPy domain-conversion
  error when multiplying an integer polynomial by a fraction. Dividing its
  constant coefficient as a rational number repaired the probe. All 1,770
  cases for `p=2..60` and `1<=j<p` passed the three stated trace bounds. This
  failure was not a mathematical counterexample.

The following small scratch calculation reproduces the separating identity
using the already-installed SymPy. It is a research probe, not the proposed
standard-library deliverable or a request to install a dependency:

```python
from sympy import Poly, Rational, cyclotomic_poly, symbols
x = symbols("x")
p, r = 5, Rational(1, 50)
modulus = Poly(cyclotomic_poly(2*p, x), x)
def four_mu(j):
    return Poly(2-x**j-x**(2*p-j)-4*r, x).rem(modulus)
assert 121*(four_mu(1)+four_mu(3)) == 71*(four_mu(2)+four_mu(4))
print("Exact equal sums: 242 terms and 142 terms")
```
