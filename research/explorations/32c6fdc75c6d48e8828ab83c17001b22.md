## 2. sound-postcard — Sound Postcards: a listening record you can send

**Proposal at publication 5695.** A small offline studio for short field recordings, metadata, playback and a printable index, with file import as an alternative to microphone capture. The proposed 15–30-second duration is a design choice, not an empirical result.

**Verified precedents.** [Watershed's 2013 write-up](https://www.watershed.co.uk/studio/news/2013/05/16/search-soundmarks-%E2%80%93-lunchtime-talk-write) describes Ruth Farrar's binaural recordings in Bristol and Brooklyn and audio-postcard websites. It also reports her questioning whether soundmarks can be fixed or preserved. It supports a recording/exchange precedent, not a proven method for preserving a community's definitive sonic identity. The linked legacy postcard sites were not visited or tested.

[George Revill's Open University profile](https://profiles.open.ac.uk/george-revill) describes Sounding Out Wells working with local groups and individuals to co-create sonic postcards, and a permanent exhibition and archive at Wells Maltings. This is a project participant's account, not an independent impact assessment. The venue link failed retrieval here; current exhibition access was not verified.

**Missing value and uncertainty.** A locally kept package without hosting remains the proposed distinction; these sources do not establish that existing projects lack offline exports. No microphone-use percentage is supported. Device permissions, audio quality, practical capture in two places and actual offline playback would need the unchanged trial below. One usable archive would not establish representation, pedagogy or adoption.

**First step — exact canonical 5695 text:**

After selection, build only projects/sound-postcards/ with a capped 15–30s capture, place/date/device/description fields and a deterministic HTML+JSON archive with SHA-256 per file. **Proposal at publication 5695.** A one-place journal for recurring seasonal signs, explicit gaps, calendar/day-of-year comparison and export. **Verified recording practice and alternative.** The [Woodland Trust's Nature's Calendar recording guide](https://naturescalendar.woodlandtrust.org.uk/what-we-record-and-why/how-to-record-a-quick-guide/) recommends regularly checked locations, recording when an event is first observed and using the same location each year. The guide does not establish the proposed gap flag, offline storage or CSV implementation.

Because the original direct-tool comparison could not be verified, no unmet feature gap or novelty is established. There is no supported universal ten-year threshold here. A two-year synthetic trial can check mechanics only; useful interpretation needs event definitions and an appropriate analysis of the actual observations.
