## Candidate: five-mark packing

Question: for positive coprime integers a and b, how densely can translates of
the five-point pattern S = {0, a, 2a, 2a+b, 2a+2b} pack the integer line?
Density here counts **starting positions**, not the fraction of positions
covered; the latter is five times the former. Translations may cross any
displayed interval boundary. This is an infinite-line question.

Natalchenko and Sagdeev's [2025 paper, version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01101v2)
gives a lower bound for patterns with k gaps of size a followed by m of size b,
conjectures equality, and proves it when k or m is one. Its concluding remarks
still name tightness as the main open problem. The proposed target is k=m=2,
excluding the already-settled residue-zero case. A search finding no later
answer is not a proof that none exists.

Write a-b=5d+r with 0<=r<5. Specializing that bound gives:

- r=0: 1/5 (already known, not a new result);
- r=1 or 2: (b+2d)/(2a+3b);
- r=3 or 4: (a-2(d+1))/(3a+2b).

The new work would prove this specialization, refute it with an exactly
checkable periodic packing, or establish a clearly named nontrivial infinite
subfamily left open by the literature. A calculator, a finite matching table,
or a paraphrase of the existing theorem would not finish the project.

## Closest work actually read

- [Natalchenko–Sagdeev, full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01101v2):
  introduction, lower-bound construction, upper-bound reduction and conclusion.
  The r=0 case and one-gap cases must be credited. For k=m=2, the sufficient
  strict inequality in Corollary 1 is an equality at r=2 and r=3; copying that
  argument without a new idea will not prove the target.
- [Publisher's article page](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jgt.23269):
  confirms the journal publication and stated limitation. This is the same
  work, not independent mathematical corroboration; the full URL redirected
  to its abstract. It does not establish the present state of every follow-up.
- [Frankl–Kupavskii–Sagdeev](https://oro.open.ac.uk/88442/1/2203.03873v1.pdf):
  the earlier three-point problem is solved. Read definitions, theorem and
  related results. It supplies a baseline and warns against selling an
  old three-point formula as new work.
- [Carraher–Galvin–Hartke–Radcliffe–Stolee, published paper](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~dgalvin1/pdf/journal/infinitecayley_J.pdf):
  read periodic-extremizer proof, state construction and computational method.
  Existing finite-state and discharging methods already provide the approach;
  a fresh implementation of them is not mathematical novelty. Its algorithm
  compares periodic lower bounds with finite-interval upper bounds.
- [Li–Offner–Ye](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01571v1):
  read the four-element minimum-density statement and difference-set argument.
  It concerns the minimum over all four-element sets, not the proposed exact
  five-element family. A greedy construction alone need not be optimal.
- [Liu–Zhu's erratum](https://www.calstatela.edu/sites/default/files/erratumfinal.pdf):
  read both pages. Their old counting map could leave its declared target set;
  the replacement follows a chain until a valid target is reached. A new
  counting proof must establish range and non-overlap, not just its count.
  The [original paper's abstract](https://www.calstatela.edu/sites/default/files/largecliquedensityjgt.pdf)
  concerns distance graphs with clique size at least the number of forbidden
  distances. This is not permission to assume every five-mark case is new.

## Feasibility and failure boundaries

The positive difference set of S is
M = {ia+jb : 0<=i,j<=2, i+j>0}. The known distance-graph representation gives
a bounded experiment without a new dependency or downloaded program. A builder
could enumerate legal binary windows of length max(M), use legal shifts as
edges, and compare a periodic cycle with a rational upper-bound certificate.
All windows and edges must be covered; sampling cycles establishes only a
lower bound. An independent verifier should rebuild the geometry, rather than
trust the searcher's graph or claimed optimum. An inequality of the form
q*w(edge)-p <= potential(next)-potential(current), checked on every edge,
telescopes to a cycle-mean upper bound p/q. This certificate proposal is my
research plan, not an executed experiment or a new theorem.

For the r=2 obstruction, assume a>b as in the reduction, and put
x=|T|-|I| in the paper's notation. If its
inequality (10) held but neither alternative (7) did, integer arithmetic would
force x=2d+1 and |U|=4d+2. This is a direct specialization to inspect, not a
proved exclusion or a counterexample. The broader inequality (10) is itself
unproved here. Refuting that sufficient route would not refute the conjecture.

A concrete possible first target is a=b+2 with positive odd b. Then d=0,
r=2, the proposed density is b/(5b+4), and each of the two intervals defining
T is a singleton. That makes a bounded local proof investigation plausible;
it does not establish its novelty or truth. By comparison, a=b+1 makes both
T intervals empty, so merely presenting that immediate consequence of the
published reduction would be too weak a milestone. The novelty screen must
compare mathematical consequences, not just search for the same title.

Exponential state growth is real. A proposed first bounded domain is a+b<=12,
with a written state/time ceiling and explicit unresolved cases. Larger cases
do not silently become smaller ones, and timeouts are not proofs. Reproduce
known three-point and one-gap cases before trusting new five-point data. Check
reflection, common-factor normalization, negative d, interval boundaries and
period wraparound. No search program was run during this research round; the specializations above were checked by hand.

The first milestone should be a two-day admission screen: refresh the
literature, reproduce known exact values, and determine whether the small
domain yields a defensible proof direction. Stop if the claim is already
proved or the only outcome is a table. Subsequent days belong to a new lemma
or counterexample and an independent attempt to break it. Publishing a shelf
brief does not authorize starting those calculations.

  claims a complete characterization of attainable winning probabilities for
  balanced n-tuples. Do not propose that existence/upper-limit question as open.
- Ordinary planar Heesch census: [Kaplan's dataset](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~csk/heesch/)
  already covers enormous families. Read its inclusion thresholds; an omitted
  shape is not an unclassified shape. Read the [paper's conclusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.09438v1):
  simply extending enumeration has a major computation cost.
- Broad three-dimensional Heesch unboundedness: [Bašić, October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24281v1)
  supplies an explicit construction. Read its one-dimensional lemmas, lifting
  construction and final bound. It proves finite height at least h, not an
  exact height. The bumps that lock orientations matter; an undecorated
  polycube is not automatically the same theorem.
