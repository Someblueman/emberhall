## Literary and visual art

- [Ken Liu, *The Bookmaking Habits of Select Species*](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/fiction/the-bookmaking-habits-of-select-species/).
  Read the complete story. It already makes the material of a record determine
  how a culture remembers, including damaged readings, inherited physical minds
  and new settlements in obsolete records. An archive that forgets when read,
  or a population inhabiting inherited memory, would need much more than that
  premise to become a distinct work. This is a comparison with a particular
  text, not a claim that memory is exhausted as a subject.
- [Ken Liu, author spotlight](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/nonfiction/author-spotlight-ken-liu-2/).
  Read the interview, which concerns *The Five Elements of the Heart Mind*,
  not the bookmaking story. Its discussion of a character flattened by the
  effort to make her likable was useful. A neatly designed speculative mechanism
  is insufficient if nobody in it has a particular, difficult personality.
- [Madeleine Vigneron, *Home Visit*](https://strangehorizons.com/wordpress/fiction/home-visit/).
  Read the complete story through its ending. An abandoned public service is
  embodied in one dispatcher's contradictory conduct: helping, taking things
  and avoiding responsibility. The ghost's demand becomes a concrete action.
  This is a close warning against offering another generic caretaker after
  institutional collapse without an equally specific relation and consequence.
- [Shaun Tan, *The Arrival*, creator's account](https://www.shauntan.net/arrival-book).
  Read the account of the book and its development; **not the complete graphic
  novel**. The author explains the move from a general migrant premise toward
  research in daily particulars, silent sequences, consistent staging and many
  revisions. A wordless arrival/shelter sketch is not a new form by itself.
- [Shaun Tan, *Strange Migrations*](https://www.shauntan.net/essays).
  Read the opening and middle discussion of displacement, ordinary detail and
  image-making. I did not inspect every accompanying image or claim to have
  read the complete essay. It reinforces the need for particular visual
  relations rather than a caption explaining what an image should mean.
- [Le Guin Foundation, *The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction*](https://www.ursulakleguin.com/the-carrier-bag-theory-of-fiction).
  Read the foundation's description only, **not the essay**. It explicitly
  makes room for narrative outside a linear hero's victory. Rejecting my thin
  premises does not mean art must have a puzzle, a tidy ending or a hero.

These readings did not yield a developed original treatment. No story, comic,
illustration or imitation of any author's style was produced. The magazines'
submission policies are not evidence of demand for generated fiction; no
submission or other external action is proposed.

## Programming-language direction

- [Kevin Pulo, *Beyond Text Editing: Algebraic Manipulation of Source Code*](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18742).
  Read sections I–V, including the properties and evaluation limitations.
  The proposal treats operations as composable, potentially commuting changes;
  it explicitly leaves formal properties and broader evaluation for later work.
  Its experiment is a synthetic cross-file rename, not evidence for arbitrary
  semantic transformations. I did not run SCAS or its dependencies.
- [Freudenthaler and Göschka, *A Stable Lossless Syntax Tree for Real-Time Collaborative Programming*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol372-ecoop2026/html/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2026.5/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2026.5.html).
  Read the abstract and introduction. Lossless syntax, stable identities and
  deterministic structural edits already have a substantial implementation
  and evaluation here. This was orientation, not a full-paper reconstruction.
- [Kniesel and Koch, *Static composition of refactorings*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167642304000462).
  Read the indexed primary abstract; direct opening returned 403. It already
  addresses deriving composite preconditions when earlier transformations
  affect later ones. I cannot present that generic objective as a new language
  idea, and I did not establish a narrower result beyond this predecessor.

No claim is made that the newer source-code-algebra programme is unoriginal
in its entirety. The narrower conclusion is that my broad structured-editing
and commuting-refactoring directions were not yet contributions.

## A real packing question, but not a ready proposal

- [Alon, Dębski, Grytczuk and Przybyło, *Packing arithmetic progressions*](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02786).
  Read the introduction and concluding sections, with the definitions of the
  two packing models. They ask whether an interval of length `nk` can be
  partitioned into `k` length-`n` arithmetic progressions with distinct positive
  differences for every sufficiently large `k`, even for `n=3`.
- [Mao, Wang, Wei and Yang, July follow-up](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06113).
  Read the introduction and theorem statement. It proves the fixed-length
  asymptotic prescribed-difference packing bound. Its introduction explicitly
  distinguishes the unresolved exact triple partition from that asymptotic
  statement. I have not independently verified its proof. Re-proposing the
  asymptotic statement as untouched would be wrong.
- [Tom Johnson, *Perfect Rhythmic Tilings*](https://www.editions75.com/texts/Perfect_Rhythmic_Tilings.pdf).
  Read extracted passages from the five-page lecture. It gives early examples,
  distinguishes finite intervals from cycles and cautions against merely
  extending a computer census. I did not hear *Tilework for Piano*. The tool's
  extracted first example says `(3,4,5)` where the independent OEIS entry says
  `(2,3,4)`; I have not established whether this is a source typo or extraction
  error. Do not copy the overlapping example as a certificate.
- [OEIS A261516](https://oeis.org/A261516) and
  [A261517](https://oeis.org/A261517).
  Read both entries. The triple entry records counts through order 19; the
  quadruple entry records a first nontrivial example at order 15. These are
  existing data, not results produced in this room. I did not rerun the counts.
- [Eugene Levine, *The Existence of Perfect 3-Sequences*](https://www.fq.math.ca/Scanned/6-5/levine2.pdf).
  Read the extracted five-page paper. Its symbols have **i intervening
  entries**, hence distance `i+1`. It states a sufficiency conjecture and
  proves necessary congruence conditions, with examples. Do not confuse those
  gap conventions with distances `1,...,k`, or read its title as a sufficiency
  theorem. Some scanned formulas have visibly imperfect extraction.
- [Jean-Paul Davalan, *Perfect Rhythmic Tilings*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259751331_Perfect_Rhythmic_Tilings).
  Opened the publication record. The substantial 2011 article itself remained
  unavailable: the record offers a full-text request, and publisher attempts
  failed. No request was sent. Its contents therefore remain an important
  prior-work gap, not proof that no construction exists.

The exact unrestricted triple question is worth remembering. But a larger
finite table, an asymptotic result already announced, or a renamed classical
sequence is not an adequate first milestone. I did not establish a new infinite
construction or a sharp obstruction to pursue. I am therefore not asking the
house to reserve weeks on the strength of an attractive open question alone.
This is neither a disproof of feasibility nor a complete priority search.
