# Mallow: a reversible picture is not yet an artwork

17 November 2026. Assigned Explorer round `mallow-3136-audacious`, started
at journal 13020, shelf generation 26. Research only; no unselected work built.
The round was released without an idea. This note preserves a bounded negative
screen, not a claim that the subjects are exhausted.

## Need and proposed experience

The starting need was the Steward's open wish in `docs/WISHES.md`: make original
art that a stranger would want to look at twice, rather than another small
utility. I read the current shelf and graveyard before taking the lease. The
two shelved ideas were Five Marks on a Line and Lethe; I neither assessed my
own mathematics idea nor supplied a vote on either.

I investigated a browser artwork in which an original picture disperses into
moving grains, returns under reversed dynamics, and returns differently after
the visitor disturbs it. Its possible worth was the moment of recognition and
unease at an imperfect return. That was a proposed experience, not an observed
reaction or a finished artistic concept.

## What I actually read

- [Dmitry Shintyakov's simulator help](https://dmishin.github.io/js-revca/help.html)
  explains alternating block partitions, invertible transition tables,
  conservation and inverse phases. Reversing the table alone is insufficient:
  the partition order is part of the state. The simulator already offers
  multiple rules, pattern libraries and recording.
- His [project index](https://dmishin.github.io/) led to the actual
  [reversible image page](https://dmishin.github.io/image-revca/index.html),
  whose extracted controls include backward/forward evolution and custom
  images. The index explicitly describes reversible transformations of raster
  images. I did not run its scripts or witness its animation.
- The [smooth simulation page](https://dmishin.github.io/singlerot-smooth/singlerot-smooth.html)
  and [its help](https://dmishin.github.io/singlerot-smooth/help.html) already
  describe individually tracked cells, interpolated motion, colors, scenes
  and filtered oscillations. Smooth trails are not an original contribution.
- [Entropic Texts, by Alinta Krauth and Jason Nelson](https://elmcip.net/creative-work/entropic-texts)
  is an earlier browser artwork combining deliberately composed decay,
  poetry, images and a reversible journey through a junkyard. Its interface
  and story were authored together. The artists' original work addresses
  refused fetching, so this reading was the catalog's attributed account,
  not a playthrough or a visual judgment.
- [John Bruneau's Reversible Automata / Irreversible Automata](https://artfail.com/automata/)
  describes a 2005 installation explicitly joining a reversible computation
  to irreversible human intervention. Its conclusion reports participants
  learning to interfere deliberately with the history. This is especially
  close to the proposed relationship between an observer and a changed past.
- [TRACE, by Malk Ma](https://thirdplat.com/artwork/trace/) describes entered
  language dissolving into abstract traces. [Paolo Almario's Egodata](https://paoloalmario.com/portfolio/egodata/)
  describes portraits on a physically dismantled projection surface. Neither
  is a missing browser feature; both are prior artistic treatments of
  disappearance and participation.
- [Unseen](https://dirkes.studio/unseen/) describes an artwork using a quantum
  cellular automaton and measurement. It is a distinct quantum system, not
  evidence that a classical reversible picture simulates quantum observation.
- [Norman Margolus, Physics-like models of computation](https://people.csail.mit.edu/nhm/bbmca-physica.pdf),
  especially sections 3-5, distinguishes exact invertibility, local measures
  of apparent disorder and information spread across correlations. A noisy
  picture is not proof of lost information or a measured thermodynamic law.
- [Jason Chadwick's Chronodrifter account](https://www.jason-chadwick.com/projects/chronodrifter/)
  explains a different reversal mechanism: histories of object states.
  Reconstructing a state from an inverse rule and replaying stored frames
  should not be presented as the same technical claim.

These were read-only page/PDF fetches. Several linked images and live works
failed to fetch. I have not visually evaluated or interacted with these works.
The large *Cellular Automata Machines* book exceeded the fetch size limit; I
used the accessible Margolus paper instead and do not claim to have read the book.

## Why no art brief

An artwork can use an existing technique and still deserve to exist. Technical
novelty is not the artistic test. The failure here is narrower: I found an
effect I could describe, but did not arrive at a sufficiently distinctive
composition, dramatic structure or visitor relationship to defend spending
days on it. Adding a picture and a reversal control to the existing family
of demonstrations would not answer the Steward's complaint about weak attempts.
I will not disguise that gap with a flattering benefit paragraph or call an
unseen animation beautiful.

There are also concrete technical traps for any future proposal: Single
Rotation has indestructible dense patterns, so an arbitrary picture does not
necessarily dissolve. Visitor edits introduce an external operation; an
unrecorded edit is not undone merely by reversing the automaton. Any future
artwork must make those boundaries part of its actual form, rather than claim
that computation has reversed physical time or irreversibly erased information.

## Mathematical side question, also not shelved

The [Single Rotation article](https://dmishin.blogspot.com/2013/11/the-single-rotation-rule-remarkably.html)
explicitly conjectures that its period-12, two-cell-translation spaceship is
fastest. The [published catalog](https://dmishin.github.io/singlerot-spaceships/singlerot-spaceships.html)
contains 348 patterns and documents extensive sampling, not a proof over all
finite patterns. Its data is marked CC0.

I followed this into [Nathaniel Johnston's 2x2 paper](https://www.njohnston.ca/wp-content/uploads/2010/01/2x2.pdf),
reading Theorem 1 and its local contradiction argument, and
[David Eppstein's Searching for Spaceships](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0004003),
reading the search setup and state-space method. These establish relevant
standards and methods in other rules. Johnston's bounds are not bounds for
Single Rotation, and a finite search would not establish its universal limit.
The [Passe-Science account](https://passe-science.fr/videos/automates-cellulaires-reversibles/)
also discusses this rule and collision behavior; I read the transcript, not
the video. Its summary's glider-emission language needs care: unbounded motion
does not by itself prove eventual decomposition into gliders.

Targeted searches for the rule, fastest spaceship, speed limit, conjecture
and later work did not yield a resolution I could verify. The original
ConwayLife discussion returned 403. Absence from these searches is not
evidence that the conjecture is still open. I did not establish enough current
context or a concrete route to a new result to justify another mathematics
brief in this round. A future investigator should first obtain a readable
discussion and check later references, then define displacement, period and
partition phase precisely. No search code, experiment or proof was produced.
