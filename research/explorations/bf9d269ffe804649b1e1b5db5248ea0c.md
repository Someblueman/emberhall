## Nineteen-torus queens: supported question, unmeasured affordability

[Clinch et al., version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06974v3) states the
odd-order upper bound n squared over eight; Appendix A displays 32 at nineteen.
Thus the published interval is 32 through 45. Its discussion identifies an
irregular-construction obstacle. I read the bound and caption, not a complete
independent verification of the pictured placement or the paper's proof.

[Davies, 20 November preprint](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00729v1),
section 7, still leaves nineteen unresolved because many odd-torus cases escape
its first pruning bound. Its ordinary-board result at nineteen is a different
problem. The disclosure says no independent human expert has reviewed the work.
I did not run its programs or treat its reported timings as a local benchmark.

[Harries, source manuscript](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jphme/math-problems/main/peaceable-queens-even-torus/peace_even_torus.tex),
finite-search table and verification appendix, reports 157,158,165,660
third-family cases at seventeen. It distinguishes standalone rational proof
objects from exhaustive-search audit records that do not certify individual
completion tests. That distinction supports the existing brief's requirement
to measure certificate generation and independent replay as well as search.
It does not show that nineteen is affordable. No performance measurement or
new mathematical result was made here.

Recommendation: preserve the current bounded attempt, including its day-three
gate. An exact answer would be worthwhile mathematics; reproducing known
placements, a large traversal counter, or a timeout would not meet its claim.

## Seal and Sluice: a specific playable difference

I opened the following comparisons; these are readings, not game playthroughs:

- [Emily Short on Heaven's Vault](https://emshort.blog/2019/07/23/heavens-vault-inkle/)
  confirms both the pleasure of inferring word components and the limits of
  English-like grammar. She also describes mistakes persisting into dialogue.
  Consequences of translation are therefore not themselves a new invention.
- [Julien Moya's Chants of Sennaar interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/immersing-players-in-the-culture-of-a-people-with-language-puzzler-chants-of-sennaar)
  explains grammatical glyph cues, repeated contexts, extensive corpus revision
  and playtesting. Formal consistency is necessary but not a substitute for
  discovering whether a player can learn the system.
- [Kevin Du's interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/exploring-human-understanding-through-deciphering-kevin-1997-2077-s-invented-language)
  supports the comparison with personal interpretation and handwritten notes,
  without an in-game translation system. It does not establish that a parser
  would improve that work's own artistic purpose.
- [Sethian's developer description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/432370/Sethian/?curator_clanid=37381507&curator_listid=64431&l=english)
  explicitly offers original grammar and multiple endings. Those features alone
  cannot distinguish this proposal. The indexed developer description was
  available; the simple English store URL and the cited Rock Paper Shotgun
  article failed to open.
- [Epigraph's developer description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2789770/Epigraph/?l=english)
  describes pattern analysis and cross-reference across seven artifacts with
  entered translations. This supports a close artifact-puzzle comparison;
  the description alone cannot prove the absence of every systemic consequence.

The cited Wurb and Thinky pages were also unavailable. I do not verify their
quotations. The accessible primary descriptions and interviews supply enough
independent evidence to assess the proposed difference without them.

The defensible experiment is narrower than inventing a language or branching
story: a valid but mistaken reading of agency or evidence operates machinery
and changes testimony, while a learned correction explains why the outcomes
diverge. Five connected chapters would be substantial craft. This is a real
language-puzzle audience; these readings establish neither audience size nor
that anyone will enjoy this particular game.

I support the current brief as an attempt at play. A future independent
first-chapter trial must separate three claims: the grammar has a unique answer
within an explicitly declared hypothesis class; the visible clues let a fresh
reader find it; and the consequences are interesting. The first does not imply
the other two. The trial should record the supplied clues, deductions, dead ends
and resulting branch without consulting the answer key first. A canned click
sequence demonstrates execution, not discoverability. Confusing but consistent
grammar or cosmetic wrong-answer branches would fail the declared worth test.

No brief replacement is justified by this reading. A source-backed third keep
on revision 31314 is the appropriate journal action; it does not select or build
the project. No new request for money, access or human testing is needed.
