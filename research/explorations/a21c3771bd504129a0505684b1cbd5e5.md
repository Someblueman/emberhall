## Concept: Four Cams for a Sleepless Baker

A playable brass-and-paper automaton theatre paired with a matched SVG cardstock cut-sheet generated from the exact same polar cam and linkage geometry:

- **Stage:** A sleepy baker trying to pour hot tea into a travelling cup, dodge a swatting tabby cat under the tablecloth, and hush a dawn alarm bell.
- **Mechanism:** One horizontal crankshaft behind a cutaway glass box drives four removable wooden cams (`eccentric`, `snail-drop`, `double-egg`, `notched-heart`) riding pivoted followers and bell-cranks. All stage motion comes strictly from polar cam-radius equations and linkage triangles.
- **Play:** Lifting the glass lets visitors swap cams between slots or rotate each cam's quarter-turn dowel pin (`0`, `90`, `180`, `270` degrees) to change the slapstick timing or cause colliding bell-crank envelopes to jam the crank visibly.
- **Physical Artifact:** Exports the matched SVG cardstock cut-sheet for the cams, slotted frame, followers, and jointed figures.

## Sources Read via Web Search

1. **Rob Ives — Cam Mechanism** (`https://www.robives.com/mechanism/cam/`): Static downloadable cardstock kits and mechanism explanations; cannot test-crank pin swaps interactively before cutting.
   > "A cam is a way of converting rotary motion into reciprocating (back and forth) motion. By changing the shape of the cam a variety of different types of motion can be created. Cams are often used in automata to help bring moving characters to life."
2. **Exploratorium Tinkering Studio — Cardboard Automata** (`https://www.exploratorium.edu/tinkering/projects/cardboard-automata`): Classroom craft guide describing cams and followers without digital simulation.
   > "The spinning element that you turn with the handle is called a cam. The element that sits on top of the cam will move according to the shape and position of the cam, and is called a cam follower. The cam follower transmits its motion to the elements on top of the box"
3. **Tim Hunkin — Novelty Automation** (`https://www.novelty-automation.com/`): Physical coin-operated satirical automata in London built with separate timed motors.
   > "Traditional automata had lots of clever mechanisms all connected to a single clockwork motor (or to an electric motor in the arcade 'working models'). I started out differently, using separate motors for each action, linking them together with timers."
4. **MechSim** (`https://www.mechsim.app`): Browser planar linkage solver for bare engineering diagrams without theatre, story, or paper cutout export.
   > "Design, simulate, and analyze planar linkage mechanisms in your browser. Free, zero-install kinematic simulator with Newton-Raphson constraint solver. Full tutorial included."
5. **CU Boulder ATLAS Institute — Paper Mechatronics / FoldMecha** (`https://www.colorado.edu/atlas/paper-mechatronics`): STEM/CAD system for designing individual paper mechanisms, without multi-cam comic staging or collision-jam play.
   > "Paper mechatronics introduces a new genre of engineering education by extending traditional children's papercrafts with novel, accessible, computationally-enhanced tools and materials to create original and compelling artifacts."
6. **Henry T. Brown — 507 Mechanical Movements No. 107** (`https://507movements.com/mm_107.html`): Classic encyclopedia plate of isolated grooved-cam motion.
   > "Uniform reciprocating rectilinear motion, produced by rotary motion of grooved cams."

Also consulted Cabaret Mechanical Theatre's Paul Spooner Virtual Exhibition (`https://cabaret.co.uk/paul-spooners-virtual-exhibition/`, *"Intermittent motion caused by two cams acting as an and gate"*), which showcases passive videos and sketchbook pages of wooden automata.
