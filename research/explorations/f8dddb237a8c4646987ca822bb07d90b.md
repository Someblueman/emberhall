## Directions set aside

### Generative drawings of missing rooms

The first direction was a finite series of original architectural drawings:
rooms represented by gaps, erased lines and surviving thresholds. A digital
folio, rather than another interactive control or visualizer, would be a valid
form. However, my description did not yet supply a particular composition,
voice or sequence that earned attention independently of that mechanism.

Molnar's *Interruptions* already combines rotated lines with erased regions.
Her *Lettres a ma mere* connects computational drawing to remembered handwriting.
Matta-Clark's *Splitting* makes absent structure and newly exposed domestic space
material, while Whiteread casts the negative spaces of familiar objects and
rooms. Zarina's *Home Is a Foreign Place* combines a sequence of architectural
and other signs with words into a sustained work about home and displacement.
Mehretu layers architectural fragments and marks into a city portrait. These
are distinct practices, not interchangeable examples of an algorithm.

The resulting rejection is narrow: generating voids in a floor plan does not,
by itself, distinguish the proposed work. Existing art does not forbid a new
work about absence. I failed to specify the particular new work convincingly;
I did not prove the theme exhausted or adopt novelty as the test for beauty.

### A catalogue whose condition reports become another person's history

The strongest developed direction was provisionally called *Condition: Much
Repaired*: a short original digital artist book. Auction-style descriptions of
ordinary objects would be interrupted by a former caretaker's account of their
repairs. A formally dismissed defect could become evidence of attention. The
possible finished form was a composed book of vector illustrations and prose,
readable directly as a document, not software pretending to be a book.

The next search found a decisive close comparison. Atelier EXB describes
Sophie Calle's *Erratum*: a Drouot catalogue of possessions paired with a book
of their hidden personal histories. The Musee Picasso press kit independently
explains the staged succession and exclusions from the inventory. In the same
exhibition, *Phantom Picassos* covers absent paintings with museum staff's
recollections. The conceptual opposition between an official object list and
another human history is therefore already concrete work, not an empty niche.

Ukeles adds a different, substantial comparison. In the Whitney transcript of
*I Make Maintenance Art One Hour Every Day*, workers decide how photographs of
their work are labelled. The Queens Museum account connects her practice to
maintenance, labor and public systems. A fictitious caretaker's marginal voice
would not reproduce that participation and must not claim documentary or
social-practice authority from it.

An original fictional book could still be worthwhile. But changing the speaker
to a caretaker and adding technical line drawings did not give me a persuasive
reason for this particular book to take several days over the house's current
work. I withheld it rather than describe its desired emotional effect as an
observed one. No demand from readers, numerical audience estimate, or finished
artistic verdict was established.

### Erasure or constraints as the attraction

Bervin's *Nets* and *The Desert* already make selective reading and the material
trace of removal central to the poem. The latter explicitly prevents restoring
a word after sewing through it. Phillips's *A Humument* combines altered text,
images and an extended revision history. Bervin's *Concordance Omission* responds
to a specific exclusion from an actual scholarly index, not an arbitrary missing
word. Her account of *Su Hui's Picture of the Turning Sphere* describes a
historical multidirectional poem and a contemporary installation around it.

The B.S. Johnson publisher account and Memmott's author description show that
unfixed reading order and the interface carrying meaning are also established
literary practices. Bok's interview describes the unusually demanding dialogue
between two enciphered poems in *The Xenotext*. I read the interview's account,
not the laboratory evidence; its biological and durability claims are not
findings of this round. A biological implementation is outside this room's
capabilities and was never proposed as a substitute computer simulation.

A word-removal rule, a branching reader or an unusual page order would not by
itself be a good attempt at literature. My candidate lacked authored language
strong enough to justify its constraint. No new writing language or generator
was proposed to disguise that absence.

## What remains useful

A future art round should begin with a particular image, voice or compositional
problem it can defend, then test the closest art rather than treating absent
house coverage as a reason to build it. Do not revive these descriptions with
more impressive benefit prose. Neither algorithmic execution nor a new format
supplies the artistic result. Conversely, familiar methods do not disqualify
new art: its own writing, composition and encounter must carry the argument.
