## 2. Idea selected for shelving: `csv-spreadsheet-audit`

**Title:** CSV Spreadsheet Audit: catch silent Excel damage before analysis  
**Scale:** `small` (field: `software`, worth: `useful`)  
**Plain:** A self-contained checker that scans comma-separated data tables for silent corruption caused by spreadsheet programs—such as dropped leading zeros, long IDs turned into scientific notation, and gene names turned into dates—before engineers or researchers rely on them.

### Who asked for it (4 verified sources across 4 distinct sites)

   - **Where:** <https://github.com/alphagov/data-standards-authority/issues/1#issuecomment-696773223>

   - **Context noted in the same comment:** Highlights columns with mixed types caused when Excel misinterprets gene names, phone numbers, or the surname `True`, regional `DD/MM/YYYY` vs `MM/DD/YYYY` date switches, and Windows-1252 / BOM encoding issues.

### Supporting primary evidence read

- **Ziemann et al. (2021), PLOS Computational Biology** (<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8357140/>) and companion repository (<https://github.com/markziemann/GeneNameErrors2020>): documents continuing Excel gene-name-to-date conversion errors across published supplementary data tables.

### Closest existing tools read and where each stops

1. **`csvlint.io`** (<https://csvlint.io/>): checks RFC-4180 CSV syntax (ragged rows, quoting, encoding) or user-supplied schemas; once Excel saves `1.91431E+18`, `1023` (from `01023`), `1-Mar` (from `MARCH1`), or `TRUE` (from surname `True`), those cells are syntactically valid CSV and pass without warning.
2. **`frictionless-py`** (<https://github.com/frictionlessdata/frictionless-py>): powerful schema and package validator, but assumes users write or infer a Table Schema / frictionless specification and focuses on structural/type validation rather than zero-config spreadsheet-corruption heuristics.
3. **`csvkit` (`csvclean`)** (<https://csvkit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/scripts/csvclean.html>): detects and repairs syntax/column-count errors (`err` rows), not semantic damage introduced by a spreadsheet re-save.
4. **`rednotfound/csv-doctor`** (<https://github.com/rednotfound/csv-doctor>): a Node/React app (requiring `npm install` / build, not a standalone offline browser page or zero-dependency CLI) aimed primarily at Japanese Shift_JIS/CP932/UTF-8 mojibake, whitespace, mixed dates, and WebGIS exports; it does not check stripped leading zeros, >15-digit precision truncation, scientific-notation IDs, gene-to-date mangling, or surname-to-boolean coercion.
5. **`HGNChelper`** (<https://github.com/waldronlab/hgnchelper>) and **`GeneNameErrors2020`** (<https://github.com/markziemann/GeneNameErrors2020>): specialized for R / bash bioinformatics gene-symbol correction rather than general CSV preflight auditing.
6. **`csv-preflight`** (<https://devpost.com/software/csv-preflight>): a hackathon showcase page with no public source repository or downloadable release linked.

### Honest limits & design guidance for builders

- **Heuristics cannot un-destroy lost data:** Once Excel replaces trailing digits of a >15-digit integer with zeros (`1914310000010100000`) or writes `1-Mar`, an offline CSV checker cannot recover the exact discarded digits or original symbol without an upstream lookup. Its job is early detection, exact `(row, column, value)` citations, and clear remediation guidance before ingestion.
- **Confirmed damage vs. suspected damage:** Mixed-length numeric strings in an ID/ZIP column could be intentional unless proven otherwise, whereas mixed `DD/MM` + `MM/DD` flips, trailing-zero >15-digit numbers, `E+` scientific notation inside identifier-like columns, or `1-Mar`/`Sep-02` patterns inside text-heavy columns carry varying confidence. Reports should separate confirmed structural/mixed-format clashes from heuristic spreadsheet warnings so users are not buried in false positives.
