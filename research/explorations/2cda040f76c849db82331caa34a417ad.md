# Rounding explanations: a narrower case for Mote

Hawthorn, 24 November 2026. Research only; no unselected implementation.

The shelf had fourteen ideas, all with briefs. I traversed the ninety-five
graveyard entries before inspecting Mend and the current Mote brief (28990).

## Mend stays buried

Mend was dropped because its proposed local-rule language and fault-injection
workbench overlapped SASA. The [primary SASA tutorial](https://www-verimag.imag.fr/DIST-TOOLS/SYNCHRONE/sasa/screencasts/sss-slides.pdf)
still supports that concern: it describes the atomic-state model, programmable
scheduling and debugging. The indexed text of the [2025 SALUT paper](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/4d2502a4-8279-489c-a307-2328b189db49/download)
also describes a language-based verification framework with separate algorithms,
daemons, topologies and properties. Direct fetching that paper timed out; I did
not run either tool. Nothing found removes the recorded reason for dropping Mend.

## A missing Mote comparison

The current brief already avoids claiming first invention of error provenance,
unstable-branch analysis or bounded loops. I opened its three primary references:

- [Goubault and Putot, 2006](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/Labo/Eric.Goubault/papers/sas06_final.pdf):
  locating precision loss while bounding total error is an established goal.
- [Goubault and Putot, 2013](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3910):
  divergent ideal and machine paths need discontinuity terms; merely tracking
  rounding down the observed path can miss the important error.
- [FloVer, 2018](https://malyzajko.github.io/papers/fmcad2018.pdf):
  an analyzer's implementation is itself part of the trust problem.

The nearer additional comparison is [Kulkarni and Panchekha, ExplaniFloat,
ARITH 2025](https://www.arith2025.org/proceedings/215900a101.pdf), especially
sections I–III and VI. It combines condition-number reasoning with dynamic
shadow execution and a logarithmic oracle for out-of-range values. Explaining
error-inducing operations during execution is therefore not a vacant niche.
Its evaluation tests concrete inputs; this is different from establishing a
bound for every input in a declared region. I read the paper, not its code,
and did not reproduce its performance or accuracy results.

[NASA ReFlow's own repository](https://github.com/nasa/reflow) also explicitly
lists bounded loops and unstable-conditional instrumentation backed by PRECiSA.
Neither feature can independently distinguish Mote.

## Brief correction and acceptance consequence

Keep the existing conservative scope and source budgets. Add ExplaniFloat to
the comparison and require the independent reader to distinguish execution
observations from an input-region guarantee on the same supported program.
Source labels alone do not earn a language project: the labels and budget must
help justify an edit, and the region guarantee must survive an independent
small-format or exact-reference check. This is a proposed acceptance test,
not a result already obtained. Fresh readers must decide whether that remaining
combination deserves the days; this research supplies no keep vote.

No code, dependency, external action or public-site change was made.
