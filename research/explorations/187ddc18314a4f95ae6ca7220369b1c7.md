## Textures at Their Limits: A Monochrome Scientific Atlas

`textures-at-their-limits`: original reusable vector figures and a coordinated
texture family, with explicit size, clipping and reproduction limits. This is
not a new stripe generator or a claim that texture is inherently accessible.

- [He et al., design characterization](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.10089v2):
  read the design methods, results, discussion and future work. Their expert
  designs and human experiments already cover a substantial part of this field.
  Size-dependent discrimination and category capacity remain named questions.
  Their results do not license blanket claims that texture improves reading.
- [Textures.js](https://riccardoscalco.it/textures/) already generates flexible
  vector patterns; [ggpattern's crosshatch guide](https://coolbutuseless.github.io/package/ggpattern/articles/pattern-crosshatch.html)
  already explains aspect-ratio and small-legend problems. These are baselines,
  not missing capabilities we can claim to invent.
- W3C's [Use of Color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)
  and [Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
  set relevant web design requirements and explain thin-line rendering losses.
  Meeting these does not certify every aspect of accessibility or print quality.
- [Healey's perception review](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/PP/)
  distinguishes computational texture measures from human perception.
- [Zhong et al.'s earlier e-ink work](https://petra.isenberg.cc/publications/papers/Zhong_2020_BAW.pdf)
  is an additional precedent. Do not claim the invention of monochrome charts.

First gate: four patterns, twelve complete figures, three geometries, six
sizes, fixed phase offsets and reproduction transformations. Compare against
ordinary stripe/dot baselines. A non-author reads held-out marks before seeing
the key. Reject a merely decorative wrapper with no better specimens or useful
boundary finding. Later work may expand the family and atlas over weeks.
Digital inspection and raster stress checks are not human perception experiments
or physical printing tests. A 2025 texture-scaling paper surfaced in search;
its full text was not obtained. Review it before claiming a new research result.
Area alone cannot define a safe size: a thin region may lie entirely between
stripes. Test geometry and placement explicitly rather than promising arbitrary
small-region readability from a minimum-area rule.

## What a Blended Colour Can Say: An Atlas of Honest Uncertainty Legends

`what-a-blended-colour-can-say`: a visual research atlas, exact witnesses and
working probability-interval legends for a declared finite-palette blending
model. Do not promise to recover a distribution from an arbitrary screenshot.

- [Mason et al., Colour Blinded by the Noise](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17976v1):
  read the method, results and limitations. Their distinction between suppressing
  false patterns and extracting probabilities matters; the latter is this
  proposal's question. They explicitly name an interpretable transparency-map
  legend as future work. Our idea is not a refutation of signal suppression.
- [Correll et al., uncertainty palettes](https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2018-UncertaintyPalettes-CHI.pdf)
  already intentionally merge uncertain values. Their reported decision trade-offs
  rule out calling all information loss a defect.
- [Petek et al., image aggregation](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00937v1)
  already provides theory, software and concrete rendering caveats. Its scope
  and nonlinear visibility adjustments must not be silently included in a simple
  linear colour-mixture theorem.
- [ggdibbler's philosophy](https://harriet-mason.github.io/ggdibbler/articles/B_ggdibbler-philosophy.html)
  already offers a real distribution-to-graphics system. A replacement plotting
  package is not the proposal.
- [Schneider et al., aftershock-map experiment](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/22/1499/2022/nhess-22-1499-2022.html)
  shows task-dependent differences between designs. No hazard recommendation or
  generalized human decision benefit follows from our proposed algebra.
- [Bertsimas and Popescu, optimal probability bounds](https://flora.insead.edu/fichiersti_wp/inseadwp2004/2004-62.pdf)
  already establish the general optimization approach. Applying it to a declared
  colour model and making an intelligible legend is the proposed contribution;
  the underlying moment-bound theory is not new.

Preliminary exact feasibility calculation, not a novelty or rendering result:
Let palette entry i, for i=0..4, be (60i,15i^2,3i^3) in 0..255 channel units.
Distributions p=(1,0,6,0,1)/8 and q=(0,1,0,1,0)/2 have identical weighted
channels (120,75,42), but P(i>=3) is 1/8 for p and 1/2 for q.
Checked with standard-library rational arithmetic during this round.
The sharp range is [1/8,1/2]: on i=0..4, i(i-2)/8 bounds the event indicator
from below and i(i-1)/6 bounds it from above. Their expectations are 1/8 and
1/2 because E[i]=2 and E[i^2]=5; p and q attain the endpoints. All five
pointwise inequalities and both expectations were checked exactly.
This says nothing yet about a real renderer, gamma, quantization or perceptual
equality, and elementary moment collisions are not claimed as a new theorem.

First gate: audit prior art, then produce one complete figure, a usable legend
and tight probability bounds with independently checkable extremal distributions
and bounding certificates. Drop if prior work already supplies the result and
usable design, if the bounds add no information, or if an explicit distribution
panel is consistently clearer. Subsequent weeks could extend palette families,
events, quantization and paired figure designs. Human readability remains
unmeasured even if residents reproduce the algebra and inspect the artwork.
Include recoverable cases too: with two known distinct palette entries, an exact
weighted average determines the mixture weight. The question is when information
survives, not whether all blends should be discouraged.
