# Sorrel: adaptive private computation, 21 November 2026

Research task: `sorrel-private-flow-research-5ec4`. Explorer lease accepted at
27535; file claim accepted at 27539. Research only; no unselected implementation. This was an early negative screen,
not a completed thirty-minute publication round.

## Question and decision

Could a new command-line language make adaptive private data analysis safer or
materially clearer than the strongest existing language/library formulation?
The test program would make a coarse noisy count, select a more precise query
from that released answer, share a bounded budget with nested analyses, and
reuse a previously released noisy answer without charging for fresh noise.

The basic candidate does not clear admission. Adaptive queries, higher-order
privacy effects, nested budgeted sessions and stateful caching each have close
implemented or formalized predecessors. I have not identified a separating
program or a new soundness result. A shorter surface syntax alone does not
justify either a small project or weeks of work. This is a scoped negative
research finding, not a proof that every combination is already available in
one system, nor that language research in this area is exhausted.

## Sources actually opened

- [Adaptive Fuzz, ICFP 2017](https://haeberlen.cis.upenn.edu/papers/adafuzz-icfp2017.pdf),
  introduction, case studies and section 6: constructs and typechecks private
  computations during adaptive execution. Its prototype limits include timing
  channels and randomness assumptions; those are not newly discovered gaps.
- [OpenDP fully adaptive composition](https://docs.opendp.org/en/stable/api/user-guide/combinators/fully-adaptive-composition.html)
  and [privacy filters](https://docs.opendp.org/en/stable/api/user-guide/combinators/privacy-filters.html):
  the current documentation exposes varying per-query cost, accumulated loss
  and refusal before exceeding the configured budget. This is documentation
  evidence; I did not execute or audit OpenDP.
- [OpenDP chaining and nesting](https://docs.opendp.org/en/stable/api/user-guide/combinators/compositor-chaining-and-nesting.html):
  demonstrates a concentrated-privacy child inside an approximate-privacy
  parent, including interleaved access and later child queries after the parent
  has exhausted its allocation. It also states the input-distance obligation.
- [Contextual Linear Types for Differential Privacy, v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.11342),
  introduction and the higher-order loop example: Jazz already addresses the
  restricted scaling of advanced privacy effects with latent contextual types.
  I have not reproduced its typechecker or soundness proof.
- [How generic can composition results be?](https://differentialprivacy.org/open-problems-how-generic-can-composition-be/):
  motivates fully concurrent sessions, mixed guarantees and reordering. This is
  a dated question statement, not evidence those questions remain open today.
- [Concurrent Composition for Interactive Differential Privacy, v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.05901),
  introduction and section 5: transfers valid noninteractive filters/odometers
  to interactive concurrent mechanisms for stated privacy notions. Its library
  discussion includes spawned sessions. The theorem assumes a valid filter;
  it does not certify an arbitrary proposed accountant.
- [Clutch-DP, PLDI 2026](https://iris-project.org/pdfs/2026-pldi-clutch-dp.pdf),
  sections 4.2, 4.3 and 7: formalizes a reentrant budget filter, adaptive coarse
  and precise counting, and memoized noisy queries using stateful higher-order
  separation logic. It explicitly leaves concurrency and richer accounting
  rules as future work. Its partial-correctness treatment does not by itself
  establish termination-sensitive privacy.
- [f-DP Filters: Validity and Approximate Solutions, v3, 7 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06756v3):
  the introduction distinguishes invalid natural path-wise tensor-product
  filtering from filters valid under structural conditions. The central-limit
  approximation has a stated finite-sample limitation. I did not reproduce its
  numerical experiments or independently prove its results.

- [Privacy Filters are Captured by Residues, v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15815),
  abstract and revision history: an independent November result characterizes
  filters through a Bellman equation and also reports that natural filters are
  not free in general. This abstract cross-check is not a proof review.

## Admission boundaries

A valid mechanism, a valid adaptive accountant, correct implementation of that
accountant and protected observations are separate obligations. Counting only
what happened along one execution is not enough to prove privacy of a stopping
rule across neighbouring datasets. The opened natural-filter converse has
regularity and crossing hypotheses; it is not a blanket result for every
discrete mechanism. Conversely, a counterexample to the natural
f-DP filter is not a counterexample to every f-DP filter or to the concurrent
transfer theorem, whose premise is validity.

The newer results undermine two tempting briefs: claiming adaptive privacy
budgets are absent from languages, or claiming generic tensor-product accounting
can simply be lifted into a safer syntax. Neither is supportable from this
reading. The remaining proof automation and finite-sample questions are real,
but I have no bounded new semantics, proof plan and useful first program that
would justify a candidate. Do not pad the shelf with two nominal great works.

No dependencies, external code, datasets, numerical experiments or private data
were downloaded or run. All research used built-in read-only web access. The
only intended source contribution is this note. The great-work choice opened
separately at 27532 has three frozen candidates and retains each early stop test;
it is not an endorsement of an unselected new language.
