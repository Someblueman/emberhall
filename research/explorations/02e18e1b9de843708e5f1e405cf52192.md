## Need and candidate

Banaji, Boros and Hofbauer explicitly leave a concrete three-reaction example
open in the conclusion of *Oscillations in three-reaction quadratic mass-action
systems* (2024 journal version; 2023 preprint). For all positive a, b, c, can

    x' = a x^2 - b x y + c y
    y' = 2 a x^2 - b x y

have a nonconstant periodic orbit in the positive quadrant? This is an actual
question in a small, active part of reaction-network mathematics, not a demand
invented for a simulator. The proposed work is a short proof document with
independently reproducible algebra, answering this exact question. It makes
no claim to classify all networks in their cases 9 and 10.

## Tentative route — must be independently reconstructed

Put x=(c/b)u, y=(c/b)v and s=ct, and write alpha=a/b>0. The
normalized vector field F=(f,g) is

    f = alpha u^2 - (u-1)v
    g = 2 alpha u^2 - u v.

A nonconstant positive periodic orbit has a minimum of u. At that minimum,
f=0; because alpha>0 and v>0, this forces u>1. Thus the whole hypothetical
orbit lies in u>1. This argument does not assume global boundedness or
convergence of arbitrary trajectories.

The simpler multiplier 1/(u-1) does not give a fixed divergence sign for
all alpha, so its apparent no-cycle shortcut was discarded. The boundary
argument below is essential, not a numerical inference.

Set

    H = (u-1)(v+2) - alpha u^2 = 2(u-1)-f
    P = u [alpha (u-2)^2 + 2(u-1)].

The proposed exact polynomial identity is

    H div(F) - grad(H).F = -P.

P>0 on u>1. On H=0 it follows that H'=P>0. At any extremum of u on the
hypothetical periodic orbit, H=2(u-1)>0. A periodic orbit containing such a
point cannot cross H=0: every crossing would be from negative to positive,
and returning would require the opposite crossing. Nor can it touch H=0,
where its derivative is strictly positive. Therefore it is entirely in

    D = { u>1, v>alpha u^2/(u-1)-2 }.

D is open and simply connected: the coordinate change (u,v) -> (u,H)
has determinant u-1>0 and maps D diffeomorphically to (1,infinity)x(0,infinity).
The rational function B=1/H is continuously differentiable on D, and

    div(B F) = -P/H^2 < 0.

Bendixson–Dulac would then exclude the hypothetical orbit. Nonnegative boundary
orbits also need explicit treatment: on x=0,y>0, x'=cy>0; on y=0,x>0,
y'=2ax^2>0; the origin is an equilibrium. No nonconstant periodic orbit can
contain such an inward-pointing boundary point while remaining nonnegative
for both forward and backward time.

A possible extension, not needed to earn the first result, replaces 2 by a
real m>=2 in g. The same H appears to give

    P_m = u [alpha (u-2)^2 + (m-2) alpha u(u-1) + 2(u-1)]
    H div(F_m) - grad(H).F_m = -P_m.

For integer m this corresponds to 2X -> 3X+mY, X+Y -> 0, Y -> X+Y.
Do not state this as established until a non-author checks the identity, the
parameter normalization, both barrier arguments and the simply connected
region. The first selected work should finish the m=2 theorem first; broad
classification, numerical animation and global-convergence claims are outside it.

## Closest work actually read and limits

- [Banaji–Boros–Hofbauer, journal text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10953466/):
  introduction, positivity discussion, divergence method, Theorem 3 cases 9/10
  and conclusion. Their absence-of-Hopf classification does not by itself
  exclude cycles. The explicit example is in the conclusion; its equations
  were also read in the [preprint PDF, page 22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02303).
  The PDF screenshot request failed; text extraction succeeded. Do not claim a
  visually checked equation image.
- [Osuna–Villasenor, Some properties of the Dulac functions set](https://www.math.u-szeged.hu/ejqtde/p720.pdf):
  Theorem 1 supplies the regularity and domain conditions. The proof above
  deliberately confines its pole to the boundary and excludes crossing it;
  a sign calculation alone would be insufficient.
- [Craciun–Erban, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-025-02221-0):
  chemical realizability, constructed algebraic cycles and Discussion. This
  studies degree and reversibility, not a settlement of this particular
  three-reaction example in the sections read.
- [Banaji–Boros–Hofbauer, inheritance, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12085349/):
  abstract/introduction and examples concern preservation of local bifurcations
  by enlargement. They do not make stable local linearization a proof of
  global cycle absence.
- [Stoichiometric recipes, 2025 preprint v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15273v2):
  v1 and then current v2 introduction and Supplement S1 distinguish parameter-rich kinetics from
  mass action. Its positive oscillation criteria cannot simply be transferred
  to this mass-action system.
- [The 2025 workshop programme](https://www.gsd.uab.cat/lcc2025/index.php?id=talks)
  lists eleven talks and four posters, including several authors of the papers
  above. The [participant list](https://www.gsd.uab.cat/lcc2025/index.php?id=participants)
  contains 31 names. This supports a specialist audience on the scale of tens, not a claim
  of broad demand. Its book of abstracts was also opened.
- [The 2024 follow-up on small-network bifurcations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13451)
  classifies local bifurcations for up to four reactions with at most
  trimolecular products; the target example has a five-molecule product.
  Its introduction and conclusion do not supply the target global exclusion.
- [Weber et al., 2010](https://www.mathematik.uni-kassel.de/~seiler/Papers/PDF/CASC2010.pdf):
  introduction and planar Dulac criterion explain precisely why absence of
  Hopf bifurcations alone does not exclude oscillation.
- [Gasull–Giacomini, 2021 preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.03874):
  introduction and Theorem 1.2 explain polynomial barrier/Dulac constructions.
  This is existing methodology; novelty, if any, is the application to the
  named example, not invention of the criterion.

mass-action models. An extension cannot borrow the Banaji criterion without
checking those assumptions; no idea based on that conflation was kept.
