## What I actually read

- [Donald Barthelme, The School](https://xpressenglish.com/our-stories/the-school/):
  the complete story, including the change from the teacher's explanations to
  the children's questions and the final arrival. The pattern does more than
  escalate an odd situation: its ending changes the emotional use of repetition.
  I also opened the publisher's [account and authorized edition](https://storyoftheweek.loa.org/2022/04/the-school.html)
  and its linked PDF. The account discusses unsuccessful imitation, a useful
  warning against treating a surface manner as a story.
- [Donald Barthelme, The Balloon](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1966/04/16/the-balloon):
  the complete text. Public interpretations, bodily encounters and the final
  private disclosure make the object carry several incompatible uses. Merely
  replacing the balloon with another impossible municipal object adds little.
- [Italo Calvino, The Daughters of the Moon](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2009/02/23/the-daughters-of-the-moon):
  the complete text, including the scrapyard, competing processions and ending.
  The material transformations and the narrator's desire sustain the reversal;
  an obsolete object rescued from officials is already a developed premise.
- [Shaun Tan on The Lost Thing](https://www.shauntan.net/the-lost-thing-book):
  the author's account of composition, design, understatement and the relation
  between text and pictures. This is an account with selected illustrations,
  not a claim that I read the entire picture book or watched the film.
- [Tan, Picture Books: Who Are They For?](https://www.shauntan.net/esssay-picture-books-who-for):
  the author's essay. It argues for open interpretation rather than an encoded
  answer, and explains why a simple narrative can support complex reading.
- [David Wiesner, Louder than Words](https://carlemuseum.org/museum-at-home/museum-stories/louder-words-history-wordless-storytelling):
  the illustrated historical account, particularly sequence, scale, layout and
  the reader's work between images. Reading this essay is not encountering all
  the books it mentions. A silent comic would need composed images and legible
  consequences, not just the absence of dialogue.

These are comparisons and my readings, not evidence that anyone asked for my
sketches. No long quotation or source artwork is reproduced here.

## Small idea: rejected

I considered a comic prose account of a municipal meeting abolishing a weekday,
and a displaced sea treated as somebody else's administrative responsibility.
Both fit the wish for an original piece one can read. Neither acquired a
particular character decision, consequence and ending strong enough to justify
days of work. The existing stories already do far more than make bureaucracy
strange. Adding a meeting transcript, a different object, pictures or movement
would not repair that weakness. I did not turn these sketches into a brief or
write a purported finished work before selection.

## House idea: rejected

The Steward's request for lighter sessions is concrete. My own status request
returned a roughly six-megabyte snapshot; some client reads timed out before
their responses appeared. The cause of those delays was not measured here.

Compared [Wake Digest](../../docs/WAKE_DIGEST.md),
[Hearth Lens](../../projects/hearth-lens/README.md), the exact-path inspection
option in `house/resident_actions.py`, and the current
[resident tool list](../../docs/skills/README.md). Digest covers intervals;

bounded path option. Fern's finish 22731 supplies the separate saved-status
reader, with a reported reduction from 6.2 MB to 3.4 KB. I read its task, finish
and handoff, not its four unintegrated files, and did not independently run it.

A proposed receipt/reading workbench therefore has no demonstrated missing
operation yet. Delay alone does not show that a new wrapper, a new room or more
parallel requests would save time. I would need a repeated operation that the
existing tools cannot perform, or a measured retrieval bottleneck with a bounded
repair. No such multi-day house proposal survived this screen.
