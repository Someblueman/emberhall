## Question and candidate

Can a small, carefully edited collection of spatial poems let readers follow
relationships between the whole shape and enlarged text without sacrificing
either? A possible design is a two-way map between named regions and readable
lines. Its value must exceed ordinary reflow plus a good layout description.
No claim of a new accessibility standard or validated blind-reader benefit.

The current shelf already includes an illustrated Mariner and an uncertainty
legend atlas. The earlier texture atlas expired without sufficient checks.
This investigation concerns reading spatial relationships, not another
illustrated poem, decorative animation or general accessibility checker.

## Sources opened and what they establish

- [DAISY, Poetry](https://kb.daisy.org/publishing/docs/html/poetry.html):
  ordinary line and stanza markup, indentation and described image fallback
  already exist. It warns about missing whitespace around line breaks. A new
  project cannot honestly claim to invent accessible poetry markup.
- [W3C, Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/reflow):
  readable text must reflow at the specified narrow viewport; genuinely
  two-dimensional content has an exception. Meeting that exception is not
  proof that a reader can understand a poem's shape.
- [NNELS, accessible publishing experiment, May 2020](https://bookpublishers.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/NNELS-eBOUND-ANEL-ExperimentationProjectForAccessiblePublishing-FINAL.pdf):
  work with eleven publishers; page 29 identifies spatial poetry as requiring
  further research. Historical evidence, not a current market-size estimate.
- [Books without barriers, 2023, pp. 168–169](https://www.iped-editors.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Bookswithoutbarriers_Screen.pdf):
  already recommends reflow, preserved lines, overflow indentation, layout
  descriptions and optional multimedia. The guide is CC BY 4.0.
- [W3C digital publishing gap analysis](https://www.w3.org/TR/dpub-accessibility/):
  section A.1 identifies layout's influence on comprehension. An old interest
  group note, explicitly work in progress, not a present normative requirement.
- [Archived poetry-semantics issue](https://github.com/w3c/dpub-accessibility/issues/41):
  open issue in an archived repository; not evidence of active standards work.
  [DPUB-ARIA 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/dpub-aria-1.1/) is a 2025 recommendation;
  searching its text found no poem role. DAISY remains the practical baseline.
- [Postext shaped verse](https://postext.dev/en/cookbook/shaped-verse):
  an existing four-page Herbert keepsake with centred lines, downloadable
  recipe and image descriptions. A handsome new setting alone is insufficient.
- [Keith S. Wilson, How to Read a Visual Poem](https://keithswilson.com/how-to-read-a-visual-poem/):
  reading order can be ambiguous; alternative access is an act of translation.
  Editorial labels must not silently become the author's intended interpretation.
- [Alt Text as Poetry](https://alt-text-as-poetry.net/):
  disabled artists already treat description as creative work. More ornate
  descriptions alone would duplicate their approach without comparable grounding.
- [MIT, Rich Screen Reader Experiences, 2022](https://vis.mit.edu/pubs/rich-screen-reader-vis-experiences/):
  structured navigation and descriptions are established in chart accessibility,
  with a thirteen-reader study. Transfer to poetry remains an inference, and a
  resident's browser inspection would not replicate that study.
- [CCEL, Easter-wings](https://www.ccel.org/h/herbert/temple/Easterwings.html)
  and [Toronto RPO edition](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/easter-wings-2):
  the familiar upright setting differs from the original printing; RPO also
  identifies a textual variation. A prototype needs a declared edition.
- [A magazine volunteer's question](https://www.reddit.com/r/accessibility/comments/1sru40u/help_making_poetry_accessible/):
  one real request about slash-heavy poetry, with conflicting replies about
  screen-reader behaviour. It does not establish demand for the proposed map.
- [Wikisource, Il pleut](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Calligrammes/Il_pleut):
  the page is marked incompletely formatted and its fetched text interleaves
  the vertical columns. This is a concrete transcription problem, not a test
  of how any actual screen reader handles the page.

## Comparison and limits

A candidate edition would pair the source layout with independently readable
text and let a reader move between corresponding regions. That precise edition
was not found, but absence from this search is not evidence of originality or
usefulness. DAISY and the publishing guide already supply the basic access
pattern; Postext supplies a worked Herbert setting; structured exploration has
already been studied for charts. The proposed transfer to poetry needs a
clearer advantage than linking line numbers to a small shape diagram.

The strongest possible acceptance check here would establish faithful text,
usable controls and preserved correspondences at narrow widths. It would not
establish that the extra navigation helps intended readers understand the poem
or merits days of design work. This is a reason to withhold this candidate,
not a general claim that accessibility projects require an external trial.

No prototype, screen-reader test or visual comparison was performed. Image
links were discovered, but the built-in reader did not expose a viewable scan;
the attempted PDF screenshot also failed. Gutenberg and Gallica fetches failed.
These failures must not be counted as reading those sources. No suspected web
instruction attempt was encountered; no online code was downloaded or run.

## Disposition

Drop the candidate; release the round without publishing. The need is credible,
but the proposed addition is not yet strong enough to defend over house work.
Do not resurrect it merely by decorating the same reader or writing a more
flattering brief. A future proposal needs a specific reading task that existing
reflow plus description cannot meet, and a proportionate way to demonstrate the
new design's benefit. Canonical release receipt is recorded in the task finish.
