I also opened the six-page published Pal-Reuveni paper in a public mirror

publisher abstract. Pal and Reuveni's older proof concerns genuinely renewed attempts. Averaging a
fixed hidden target after taking each conditional mean is a different problem
from redrawing the target after each reset. Their sharp-restart theorem must
therefore be checked for its assumptions, not described as contradicted.

paper already provides a general reweighting framework and released scripts;
a new simulation wrapper would add too little.

## Concrete research route, not an accepted theorem

Let D>0 and let mu be a probability law on positive finite restart times.
Write f_mu(x)=E_mu[erfc(x/sqrt(4Dt))], and
A_mu(x)=E_mu[integral_0^t erf(x/sqrt(4Ds)) ds].
The candidate route is to establish A_mu(x)=D^-1 integral_0^infinity
min(x,y) f_mu(y) dy, first for finite support and then with nonnegative
integration. For a baseline mu0 with finite three-quarter moment, set
Z=integral f_mu0(x)^2 dx and P=f_mu0^2/Z. Symmetrization of
integral P(x) A_mu(x)/f_mu(x) dx should give a nonnegative squared-ratio
remainder against the baseline. This would compare entire laws rather than
just small variations at one law.

The positive-distance density in this plan integrates to one on x>0. The
source mixes a symmetric-line convention with positive-distance formulas;
reconciling that factor of two is a required check, not evidence against the
optimality claim. The competitor uses one unchanged law at every attempt,
drawn independently of the search; learning policies are outside scope.

A finished proof must settle finiteness, interchange of integrals, equality,
and infinite competitor costs. It must not smuggle in finite mean restart
time: a finite three-quarter moment allows some infinite-mean laws. Atoms at
zero and infinity, finite reset costs, adaptive resets, drift and higher
spatial dimensions are outside the proposed contract. I ran no numerical
search or checker and claim no established theorem from this preliminary
algebra. A failed derivation or discovered earlier proof ends the proposal.
