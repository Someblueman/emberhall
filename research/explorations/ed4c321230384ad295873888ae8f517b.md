## 3. Technical Reference Notes for Builders on `locked-polyomino-tilings-bounds`

### 3.1 Definitions and Metagraph Context
- Let $G(m, n)$ be the $m \times n$ rectangular grid graph. A **$t$-omino tiling** partitions $G(m, n)$ into connected induced subgraphs (tiles) of size $t$.
- In the paper's **ReCom metagraph** $\mathcal{M}(G(m,n),t)$, the second argument is the tile size $t$, not the number of districts $k=mn/t$. Vertices are tilings, and an edge joins two distinct tilings differing on two tiles: a 2-way recombination replaces a pair by another pair with the same union.
- A tiling is **locked** precisely when it is an isolated vertex **and there is at least one other tiling** in $\mathcal{M}(G(m,n),t)$ ([paper, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.15996)). Equivalently, no pair of tiles has a different two-tile repartition, and another global tiling exists. Checking adjacent pairs suffices on a rectangular grid; disjoint nonadjacent tiles cannot be repartitioned into different connected tiles of the same size. The second condition is essential: the unique domino tiling of a $1\times4$ grid is isolated but is not locked under the paper's definition.
- Under **3-way recombination**, use the same nondegeneracy condition: a 3-way locked tiling has another global tiling but no different repartition of any connected union of three tiles. The paper's Open Problem 3 concerns this stronger move on finite grids for $t\ge4$.

### 3.2 Established Results in `arXiv:2307.15996` (Table 1, Table 2, Table 3, Theorems 2.1–6.1)
- **$t = 2$ (Dominoes):** Theorem 2.1 — $\mathcal{M}(G(m, n), 2)$ is connected for all $m, n$; no locked domino tilings exist on rectangular grids.
- **$t = 3$ (Trominoes):** Theorem 3.1 — connected when $\min(m, n) \le 3$ (and Emre, Jerison, Veomett `arXiv:2606.10152` prove connectivity for $3 \times n$ partitioned into 3 districts of size $n$). Theorem 3.2 — locked 3-omino tilings exist for all $m \ge 6$ divisible by 3 and $n \ge 100$ (and all sufficiently large rectangular grids divisible by 3). Under 3-way recombination moves, corner enumeration shows no 3-way locked 3-omino tilings exist on finite grids.
- **$t = 4$ (Tetrominoes):**
  - Exhaustive search up to $20 \times 20$ (Table 2): one locked 4-omino tiling up to isomorphism on $10 \times 10$ (Figure 8; uses only 4 of the 5 free tetrominoes, omitting the L-tetromino; any locked 4-omino tiling with $\min(m,n) \ge 14$ must contain all 5 shapes).
  - Theorem 4.1: For every integer $n \ge 1$, the square grid of side length $26 + 16n$ (starting at $42 \times 42$ for $n=1$, Figure 12, and $90 \times 90$ for $n=4$, Figure 13) admits a locked 4-omino tiling constructed by repeating border segments and 8 diagonal/orthogonal "spokes" around a central core (each period adds 8 to each side, increasing side length by 16).
- **$t = 5$ (Pentominoes):**
  - Exhaustive search up to $20 \times 20$ (Table 2): one smallest locked 5-omino tiling up to isomorphism on $20 \times 20$ (Figure 9).
  - 4-fold rotationally symmetric search on $5n \times 5n$ square grids up to $40 \times 40$ (Table 3 / OEIS A365862):
    - $20 \times 20$ ($n=4$): 1 up to isomorphism (2 fixed/mirror orientations in A365862)
    - $25 \times 25$ ($n=5$): 0
    - $30 \times 30$ ($n=6$): 10 (20 in A365862)
    - $35 \times 35$ ($n=7$): 27 (54 in A365862)
    - $40 \times 40$ ($n=8$): 3,232 (6,464 in A365862)
    - Total up to $40 \times 40$: $1 + 0 + 10 + 27 + 3232 = 3,270$ tilings.
  - **Open Problem 1:** Do any of these generalize to an infinite family (e.g., via periodic border + spoke repetition), or what occurs on $45 \times 45$ ($n=9$) and rectangular $5a \times 5b$ grids?
- **$t = 6$ (Hexominoes):**
  - 0 tilings up to $20 \times 20$, and 0 tilings with 4-fold rotational symmetry up to $40 \times 40$ (Table 2).
  - Section 4.4: A single locked 6-omino tiling was discovered on a $48 \times 48$ grid (Figure 14). The paper does not establish its minimality or uniqueness.
  - **Open Problem 1 & Section 4.4:** Does a locked 6-omino tiling exist on a smaller grid (e.g. $24 \times 24, 30 \times 30, 36 \times 36, 42 \times 42$ without 4-fold rotational symmetry, or rectangular $6a \times 6b$ with area $< 2304$), and can an infinite family be constructed?
- **$t = 7$ (Heptominoes) and $t \ge 8$ (Octominoes):**
  - Theorem 4.2: No $m \times n$ rectangular grid has a locked 7-omino tiling (proved by finite corner-neighborhood case analysis around a degree-2 corner vertex).
  - **Open Problem 2:** Are there any locked $t$-omino grid tilings on finite rectangular grids for any $t > 7$ (starting at $t = 8$), or does the corner/boundary obstruction terminate at finite depth for $t = 8$ as well?
