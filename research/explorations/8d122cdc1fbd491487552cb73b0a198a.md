## Reversible Steps Through Local Janus Programs

The proposed work is a paper and command-line interpreter for a specified
Janus fragment with local integers, stacks and nested parameterless calls.
Its proposed contribution is a proved reversible transition system for these
constructs, with a relation to their whole-program meaning. Ordinary backward
execution is already established and is not a contribution.

Primary reading:

- [Lanese and Vidal, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16913v2): sections 2,
  4 and 5 distinguish reversible statements from reversible semantic steps;
  local variables and stacks are explicitly left for extension. The paper's
  Loop Lemma concerns reachable configurations. Expressions are atomic here.
- [Jana's tutorial](https://github.com/kirkedal/Jana-JanusInterp/blob/master/Tutorial.md):
  local/delocal, stack operations and call/uncall already exist. Its output
  operations are expressly not reversible. This was reading, not execution.
- [Mogensen's L4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31201v1): sections 8, 11 and 12
  already give local environments, bidirectional steps and a different
  treatment of uncall. A Standard ML interpreter is described as available
  on request. I did not request it or run it.
- [Makino and Yokoyama's R-CORE paper](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-30839-9_12):
  the primary abstract sets a competing locally injective step semantics.
  Full text was unavailable; no claim of full comparison is made.
- [Gluck, Kaarsgaard and Yokoyama](https://rkaarsgaard.github.io/assets/pdf/papers/rpla2019.pdf):
  a reversible metalanguage already handles iteration, recursion and dynamic
  data. Reversible semantic composition itself is not new.
- [Paolini, Piccolo and Roversi](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol069-types2015/LIPIcs.TYPES.2015.7/LIPIcs.TYPES.2015.7.pdf):
  the certified treatment is a big-step model with global variables. It
  explicitly discusses extending its syntax with reversible local allocation.
- [Kuroda and Yuen's Concurrent Janus](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/34/0/34_444/_article/-char/en):
  the primary abstract describes translation to CRIL and dependency tracking.
  Its PDF fetch timed out. Neither concurrency nor a first reversible Janus
  runtime is claimed here.

The proposed decisive check is to restart from one complete current machine
state inside a nested local scope and reverse without consulting a trace of
past stores. Control frames and live language data are part of that state;
they must be specified, not concealed as an implementation detail. That
experiment supplements a universal inverse-step and correspondence proof;
it cannot replace one. No parser, interpreter or proof was built this round.
Priority and the exact comparison with the newer languages remain open.

## Fair Swaps After an Uneven Final Round

The first, broader candidate was rejected during reading. The
[2024 paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-024-01271-y)
asks about allowing a small amount of temporary envy during rearrangement.
[Frank and Suksompong, September 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15358v1)
already prove a negative answer for every fixed relaxation, even with two
agents. Their Theorem 3.1 defeats the tempting inference from small examples.

The narrower proposed target is their explicitly open question after
Proposition 3.4: when the endpoints come from recursively balanced picking
sequences, with the same size vector, must an EF1 exchange path exist even
when the last round is incomplete? The paper proves an EF2 path, and an EF1
path when all rounds are complete. Its four-good example rules out staying
within picking outcomes, but has an EF1 bridge outside them. Any search must
include those outside states.

Other primary reading:

- [The authors' research poster](https://shermanyuen.github.io/reachability-poster.pdf)
  states the exchange-only model and intermediate-fairness requirement.
- [Reforming an Unfair Allocation, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-026-01401-8)
  concerns reaching any fair endpoint, a different contract from reaching a
  specified destination while keeping every intermediate state fair.
- [Comparing Picking Sequences](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17604v1),
  introduction and conclusion: different orders have different maximin-share
  guarantees. Those welfare comparisons do not answer connectivity.

### Disposable feasibility screens

These were local standard-library Python calculations, not downloaded code.
No screen is a universal result or evidence of priority. Inputs, seeds and
scope were fixed before each screen; no extension was used to turn a failure
into a pass.

1. Seed 75620261119; 180 profiles each for 6, 8 and 10 goods; two equal-sized
   bundles; values sampled from {0,1,2,4,8,16,32}. For 11,560 sampled pairs of
   EF1 endpoints, a dynamic program exhausted target-directed shortest swap
   paths and minimized their largest required EFk value: 1,843 needed k=0,
   9,715 k=1 and 2 k=2. This sampled result was superseded as a candidate by
   the published arbitrary-size obstruction above. That obstruction uses unequal
   bundle sizes; it does not refute the finite equal-size observations. The
   proposed arbitrary-size guarantee was abandoned, not relabeled as a pass.
2. Seed 7562026111902; utilities sampled from {0,1,2,3,5,10,20}. Enumerated
   the complete fixed-size allocation graph, retaining every EF1 state, and
   checked components containing all endpoints generated by each complete
   round permutation and a fixed final picking set. Ties always favored the
   smallest good index. Tested (agents,goods,profiles) = (2,7,200),
   (3,7,250), (3,8,150), (3,10,35). No separated picking endpoints occurred
   in these 635 profiles. This does not cover all utilities, all tie orders,
   or arbitrary sizes; it supports only attempting the narrower question.

Acceptance must be a universal theorem with a path construction or an explicit
counterexample with a complete graph or separating-invariant certificate. A finite census alone fails.
The first proposed stage fixes the two-agent odd-length case for arbitrary
valuations, or finds a counterexample to the full target. There is no claim
that either has been achieved.

## Reproduce the bounded second screen

This is the exact exploratory script that produced the four rows above. It is
not the proposed project checker or a proof. Run the code below with Python 3.

```python
from itertools import combinations, permutations, product
from random import Random
from collections import deque
import json
rng=Random(7562026111902)

def all_states(n,m,sizes):
    def build(agent, remaining, owners):
        if agent == n-1:
            x=list(owners)
            for g in remaining:x[g]=agent
            yield tuple(x);return
        for choice in combinations(remaining,sizes[agent]):
            chosen=set(choice); x=list(owners)
            for g in choice:x[g]=agent
            yield from build(agent+1,tuple(g for g in remaining if g not in chosen),x)
    yield from build(0,tuple(range(m)),[-1]*m)

def ef1(a,u,n):
    for i in range(n):
        sums=[0]*n; largest=[0]*n
        for g,owner in enumerate(a):
            sums[owner]+=u[i][g];largest[owner]=max(largest[owner],u[i][g])
        if any(sums[i]<sums[j]-largest[j] for j in range(n)):return False
    return True

def components(states,m):
    todo=set(states); ids={}; c=0
    while todo:
        a=todo.pop(); ids[a]=c; q=deque([a])
        while q:
            a=q.popleft()
            for x in range(m):
                for y in range(x):
                    if a[x]==a[y]:continue
                    b=list(a);b[x],b[y]=b[y],b[x];b=tuple(b)
                    if b in todo: todo.remove(b);ids[b]=c;q.append(b)
        c+=1
    return ids

cases=0
for n,m,trials in [(2,7,200),(3,7,250),(3,8,150),(3,10,35)]:
    q,r=divmod(m,n);sizes=[q+(i<r) for i in range(n)]
    states=list(all_states(n,m,sizes))
    sequences=[sum(parts,())+tail for parts in product(list(permutations(range(n))),repeat=q)
               for tail in permutations(range(r))]
    for trial in range(trials):
        u=[[rng.choice([0,1,2,3,5,10,20]) for _ in range(m)] for _ in range(n)]
        allocations={}
        for seq in sequences:
            remain=set(range(m));a=[-1]*m
            for agent in seq:
                g=max(remain,key=lambda g:(u[agent][g],-g));remain.remove(g);a[g]=agent
            allocations[tuple(a)]=seq
        ids=components([a for a in states if ef1(a,u,n)],m)
        assert all(a in ids for a in allocations)
        parts={ids[a] for a in allocations}
        cases+=1
        if len(parts)>1:
            representatives=[];seen=set()
            for a,seq in allocations.items():
                if ids[a] not in seen:representatives.append({'owners':a,'sequence':seq,'component':ids[a]});seen.add(ids[a])
            print(json.dumps({'counterexample':True,'seed':7562026111902,'n':n,'m':m,'utilities':u,'witnesses':representatives,'fair_state_count':len(ids),'cases':cases}));raise SystemExit
    print(json.dumps({'n':n,'m':m,'trials':trials,'counterexample':False,'allocation_states':len(states),'pick_sequences':len(sequences)}),flush=True)
print(json.dumps({'total_profiles':cases,'counterexample':False,'limitation':'random finite screen with fixed ascending tie breaking; not a theorem'}))
```
