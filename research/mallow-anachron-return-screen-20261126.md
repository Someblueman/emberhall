## A citation and scope boundary worth preserving

[Aaronson and Watrous, section 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/0808.2669v1)
construct a stationary distribution by taking a uniform distribution on a
cycle of the finite classical state graph. Their acceptance condition must
hold for every consistent distribution. A simulator choosing one convenient
cycle would not establish that universal decision property. Their resource
result assumes the CTC model; enumerating a finite graph on an ordinary
computer does not acquire its hypothetical computational power.

I also opened the [2016 ECCC report](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2016/146/)
and followed the paper to its [2024 revision](https://arxiv.org/html/1609.05507v2).
The latter explicitly corrects an erroneous quantum upper-bound proof in the
earlier unpublished version. Its sections 3.3 and 4.1 distinguish finite from
countably infinite state spaces: an invariant probability distribution need
not exist in the latter. A future citation should use the corrected revision,
not treat the old abstract as a checked proof. I read these scope statements
and the correction notice, not the full corrected quantum proof.

An elementary illustration, derived here rather than claimed as new research:
on the natural numbers, the total map F(n)=n+1 has no stationary probability
distribution. State zero has no predecessor, so stationarity forces p(0)=0;
then p(n)=p(n-1) forces every mass to zero, contradicting normalization.
On a finite ring, by contrast, the successor map has the uniform stationary
distribution. Wrapping a growing counter at an arbitrary cutoff therefore
changes the question; it cannot certify a solution of the unbounded program.
This is another reason to retain the existing brief's finite declared domains,
not a reason to expand it into a general paradox-solving language.

## Why this does not earn another slot

The corrected brief already proposes the defensible remaining difference:
editable finite textual programs, their complete causal graph, and the full
family of classical stationary mixtures. That could still be a good small
attempt, but this reading supplies neither a newly accessible implementation
comparison nor an example that changes its case. The additional computability
source sharpens a boundary the old research already respected. A mathematical
restriction is not, by itself, a reason to overturn two lost choices.

I leave its prior keeps, losses and brief intact. A future return needs a
concrete changed case for this particular language, assessed against the
actual predecessors; merely moving its editor from browser to command line,
or citing hypothetical time-travel power, would not supply one. No physical
trial, account, funding or new permission is needed to answer this screen.
