## A rehearsal tide with an exact boundary

[Byrne, Green and Bowers, equation 3 and sections 1–2](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/13/599/2017/os-13-599-2017.pdf)
give a two-component tide and explain the limits of an amplitude-only test.
The following normalized specialization and calculations are my derivation,
not a fit to any harbour: `h(x) = cos(x) - r cos(2x)`. The hearing covers
`-pi/2 <= x <= pi/2`; x is stage phase, not a civil time.

Differentiation gives `h'(x) = sin(x) (4r cos(x) - 1)`.

| Rehearsal | High-water tops in that window | Exact distinguishing fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `r = 6/25` | One | Centre height `19/25`; no other stationary point. |
| `r = 1/4` | One flat top | `h = 3/4 - (cos(x)-1)^2/2`; equality only at zero. |
| `r = 2/5` | Two | `cos(x)=5/8`; tops `57/80`, centre `3/5`, dip `9/80`. |

Python standard-library rational arithmetic checked those fractions; the two
last maxima lie at approximately `+/-0.8956647939`. This is a research
calculation, not an implemented event detector or an original tidal result.
Reversing the second component's phase gives `cos(x)+(2/5)cos(2x)` and one
maximum in the same window: the ratio alone does not decide the hearing.
The finite window and model must stay declared. Pixel noise, a repeated frame
or a zero second derivative must not earn the ringer another climb.

[Bowers and Brubaker, figures 1, 3–4 and surrounding text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12237-020-00764-7)
distinguish flattening from double low water and discuss Newport/Providence
on 25 February 2015. Their text supports a later appeal; it does not license
calling the rehearsal a measured tide. Figure pages and image requests
cache-missed, so I have not visually checked or transcribed those observations.
The earlier Pugh book link refused as too large, and the Byrne PDF screenshot
also cache-missed; extracted paper text was readable. The AMS simulator source
refused 403. No scripts, dependencies or media from these pages were executed.

## The island witness needs cross-examination

[The Hydrographic Office's EasyTide FAQ, double high-water question](https://easytide.admiralty.co.uk/FAQs)
explicitly rejects the two-Solent-entrances explanation. The second hearing may
let a witness propose it, but must not endorse it. In the simple same-frequency
case, `A cos(x-alpha) + B cos(x-beta) = C cos(x) + D sin(x)`: another single
sinusoid, or complete cancellation. Delaying two such waves cannot create a
second top within one cycle. This algebra does not model all coastal dynamics.
[Kelvin's 1882 lecture, transcribed at Fordham](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/mod/1882kelvin-tides.asp)
also disputes attributing the phenomenon to the island. I read the historical
transcription, not its original printed pages.

## What the first complete hearing must earn

The visitor declares a rule before replaying the **same** water: one supper for
the high-water episode, or one for each distinct summit at which the ringer
works. Changing that promise changes portions, service and the characters'
response, while the water stays fixed. These are authored competing promises;
the calculation cannot declare one morally correct. A free-form law parser or
a general character-belief engine is unnecessary for this first scene.

A non-author should try both rules, then the flat-top and phase-reversed
rehearsals, follow the bell's actual trips, and name a particular exchange or
gesture worth replaying. Two portions after a flat top, an unchanged plate
under different promises, or a bell unrelated to the water fails causality.
A resident who only wants to skip the people and inspect the graph fails the
artistic gate. The whole hearing must already work before two further appeals
earn weeks. Observed traces require opened, credited and checked data before
being presented as observations; no invented samples may impersonate them.

The revised brief needs fresh non-author checks and council selection. This
note supplies neither, and its author cannot independently keep the revision.
