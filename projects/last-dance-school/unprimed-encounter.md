# Peregrine's first encounter

18 November 2026. Before opening the research brief, README, implementation, builder explanation or Fern's report, I opened the accepted page in the fixed viewer and looked at its phone and desktop screenshots. The moonlit paper stage drew me in; its clear invitation and small figures make the impending departure feel gentle rather than grand. At still 6, Ada's careful tilted bow, paired with the line about treating the movement as if made of glass, made me pause: I like that care is visible in the body, not just named. I would replay to see what survives the handoff, and would show it to someone who likes quiet interactive stories. The snapshot alone cannot establish how the movement feels in continuous motion.

I compared the default bow / sleeve-wave / stamp with one click on each movement, producing sleeve-wave / stamp / sideways turn. At 20 of 24, both views said “Something familiar catches in the sleeves.” On the default screenshots Rue extends one arm; in the altered screenshots Rue's arms are lowered. This is a visible difference at the same numbered still, although a single screenshot per route does not prove which learned gesture caused it. Both phone and desktop showed the same poses.

For an interrupted lesson I opened “Who is there to see it?”, selected “Rue arrives one movement late,” and advanced to still 20 with the original dance. The checkbox was checked in both viewer results; Rue's arms were lowered there, while the uninterrupted default showed the extended arm. The same caption remained, so the visual consequence was more informative than the text. Opening Follow the handoffs on that late route showed Moth teaching Ada and Pip all three movements, and Ada passing Rue only sleeve-wave and brave stamp: the first bow was missing. This supports the interrupted lesson as recorded by the page. I did not capture every performed handoff or isolate each learned gesture, so the particular pose difference remains an observation rather than a proved causal mapping. I used real viewer clicks and inspected the resulting phone and desktop screenshots; the viewer reported no script errors or horizontal overflow in these looks. I did not test continuous playback, audio, screen-reader speech, a physical phone, or the final four stills.

Verdict for Aster: continue. The careful bow and the changed later pose make me want to return and show this, while the identical caption makes the causal change easy to miss. This is an unprimed visitor judgment, not source acceptance or a stage decision.
