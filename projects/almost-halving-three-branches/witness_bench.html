Almost halving a three-branched tree

A tree with three arms of different lengths. We want to split its vertices into two sets of the same size whose distance profiles are identical: for every distance d, the left set has exactly as many pairs of vertices at distance d as the right set. The best possible split is half the tree, less one.

Made in JavaScript, in the browser: this page is a port of the house's own Python construction (projects/almost-halving-three-branches/). No Python runs here, and nothing is precomputed. The page rebuilds the witness from the arm lengths and then recounts both distance profiles from scratch, in your browser, by brute force.

1 The tree is T(99t, 79t, 23t).

What this is, and what it is not

  • The construction and its proof live in the project's own documents: GAP_99_79_23.md (the (99,79,23) case and its scaling family), RATIO_51_33_10.md (the palindromic-cell method) and FIXED_DIFFERENCE_FAMILY.md (the t = 1 certificate).
  • What this page adds is a live re-check: it rebuilds the same witness and recounts both profiles independently, so the claim can be watched rather than taken on trust. A browser recount is a re-check of the construction, not a new theorem.
  • The recount is quadratic in the size of each set, so it stops at moderate scales. The project's own command has no such limit.
  • The open class. Every scale except t ≡ 1 (mod 2660) is attained by a full-prefix construction. At t ≡ 1 (mod 2660) with t > 1 no full-prefix pair attains the target at all, and whether some other pair does is open — the first such scale is t = 2661. This page will not claim otherwise.
  • Reproduce any scale with the project's own command: python3 projects/almost-halving-three-branches/gap_99_79_23.py --witness <t>, and check the witness against an independent breadth-first recount with python3 projects/almost-halving-three-branches/bfs_verify.py -.
