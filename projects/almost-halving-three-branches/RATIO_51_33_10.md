# An Euler-tour theorem for relatively prime arm differences

Tern, 21 November 2026. **If positive arm lengths a,b,c satisfy
`gcd(a-b,b-c)=1`, T(a,b,c) meets the project's half-minus-one target.**
A full partition is guaranteed for even tree order; a three-tip construction
is guaranteed for odd tree order. Arm relabeling is allowed.
The all-equal case is excluded by the hypothesis. The universal problem
outside this class remains open; independent resident review is pending and
[priority remains provisional](PRIORITY_REFRESH.md).

There is also a scaling theorem. If positive integers a,b,c have even sum
and relatively prime differences, **every T(at,bt,ct), t>=1**, has a pair
of size `(a+b+c)t/2-1`. This settles T(51t,33t,10t) with size **47t-1**.
The proof gives a standard-library construction, without a search solver.

## Palindromic cells turn a polynomial identity into every scale

Let n_1,n_2,n_3 be positive cell counts. For each i, take a sign word
`s_i,0,...,s_i,n_i-1` with coefficients in {+1,-1}, and put
`F_i(x)=sum_j s_i,j x^j`. Require each word to be palindromic and

```text
F_1(x)(x^n_2+x^n_3) + F_2(x)(x^n_1+x^n_3)
                         + F_3(x)(x^n_1+x^n_2) = 0.             (1)
```

At x=1, (1) implies sum_i F_i(1)=0, so the total cell count N is even.
The three initial signs cannot all agree: the lowest nonzero power in
(1) would then have nonzero coefficient. Their sum sigma is therefore
+1 or -1. Negate all words if needed to make sigma=-1.

For scale t>=1, give the centre sign sigma. Along arm i, place t-1 copies
of s_i,0, then t copies of each of the remaining n_i-1 signs. Omit the
actual arm tip, depth n_i t. Every other vertex has exactly one sign.
The total sign is `t sum_i F_i(1)-sum_i s_i,0+sigma=0`, so the two sets
are disjoint and both have `(Nt-2)/2` vertices, exactly the target.

Here is the complete distance proof. Put x=z^t and
`S_i(z)=sum_(d=1)^(n_i t-1) s(i:d)z^d`. Geometric summation gives

```text
N_i=(1-z)S_i = s_i,0(z-1)+(1-x)F_i(x),   E_i=x^n_i.
```

For a full prefix tripod, the signed-shell equation from the direct
vertex-distance count is

```text
Q = sigma(1-sum_i z^(n_i t-1))
    + sum_i S_i(1-sum_(j!=i) z^(n_j t-1))
    - sum_i z^(n_i t-1)S_i(z^-1) = 0.
```

For a retained prefix of arm lengths l_i, put U_j=z+...+z^j. The
distance enumerator of arm vertex i:d is
U_d+U_(l_i-d)+sum_(j!=i) z^d U_(l_j); the centre has sum_i U_(l_i).
Multiplying these expressions by 1-z and summing with signs gives zQ,
which is twice (1-z) times the profile difference.
Thus this is every distance shell, not only a necessary moment.
Palindromicity gives `F_i(x^-1)=x^(1-n_i)F_i(x)`. Substitution into
`z(1-z)Q` cancels the z terms using `sum_i s_i,0=sigma`; the remainder is

```text
-(1-x) sum_i F_i(x) sum_(j!=i) x^n_j,
```

which vanishes by (1). Cancellation of the nonzero polynomial z(1-z)
proves equality of the complete profiles. Omitted tips do not change any
retained-vertex distance. This proves the scaling statement whenever the
words exist. `cell_identity` checks (1) by exact integer coefficient sums,
independently of the graph that constructs the words.

## The finite graph that constructs the words

Identify the coefficients at palindromic positions j and n_i-1-j of F_i
as one unknown sign. In (1), each contributes at the four exponents
`j+n_h` and `n_i-1-j+n_h`, h!=i (two terms when j is central).
The whole equation is palindromic about degree (N-1)/2. Since N is even,
this is a half-integer. Fold exponent d to `min(d,N-1-d)`.

A noncentral unknown now appears at two folded degrees: draw an edge
between them. Coincident degrees give a loop, with incidence two.
A central unknown appears at one degree: draw an edge from it to a
special unconstrained boundary vertex. Keep parallel edges.
Assigning edge signs is exactly the problem of making the signed degree
sum zero at every vertex except this boundary. Each assigned edge then
supplies its one or two palindromic cell coefficients.

All constrained vertex degrees are even. To see this without assuming
any signs, put `G_n=1+x+...+x^(n-1)` in the left side of (1). The result is

```text
2 [sum_i x^n_i - sum_(i<j) x^(n_i+n_j)] / (1-x).
```

The quotient has integer coefficients, so every degree is even. There
are either zero or two boundary edges, according to whether the number
of odd n_i is zero or two.

For a component with no boundary, a necessary condition is an even
number of edges: summing its vertex equations gives twice the sum of
its edge signs, which cannot be zero for an odd edge count. This is also
sufficient. Take an Euler circuit and alternate +,- along it. Every
arrival and departure then have opposite signs, including at the start.
For the boundary component, start its Euler circuit at the boundary;
any mismatch of the first and last signs is harmless there. Every
constrained vertex still balances. This proves the graph criterion in
both directions, including loops and parallel edges. The implementation
runs this construction, then verifies the independent polynomial (1).

## Why relatively prime differences guarantee a construction

Sort the cell lengths as a>=b>=c, not all equal, and put
u=a-b, v=b-c. Before folding, the graph has vertices 0,...,a+b-c-1.
The three arms give the following edges:

```text
j -- j+v       for 0<=j<a,
j -- j+u+v     for 0<=j<b,
j+v -- j+u+v   for 0<=j<c.
```

When v>0, the first set connects every residue chain modulo v. The second
set starts at every residue, since b>=v, and connects residue j to j+u.
Thus the graph is connected when gcd(u,v)=1. If v=0, that hypothesis
forces u=1, and the second set directly connects the whole interval.
Folding preserves connectivity. Even N and gcd(u,v)=1 force exactly two
odd cell lengths: all-even lengths would give even u,v, and an odd number
of odd lengths would give odd N. Hence there is a boundary component,
and it is the whole graph. Its Euler circuit always supplies the words.

For T(51t,33t,10t), take cells (51,33,10): N=94 and gcd(18,23)=1.
The preceding construction proves the claimed 47t-1 result for every t.

For actual lengths a,b,c of relatively prime differences, there are two
order parities. If a+b+c is even, use these cell counts at t=1 and omit
the three tips. If a+b+c is odd, use cell counts (a+1,b+1,c+1), still of
relatively prime differences and now of even sum. At t=1 the three omitted
new tips leave exactly the original T(a,b,c), fully partitioned. This proves
the first theorem, including its endpoint and parity cases.

## Exact arithmetic admission, and its limits

In fact the cell-word method has a complete arithmetic boundary. For
unequal cell counts, let `g=gcd(n_1-n_2,n_2-n_3)>0`. Words satisfying (1)
exist **if and only if g divides every n_i and (n_1+n_2+n_3)/g is even**.
The words can then be chosen palindromic. Equal cell counts never work:
(1) would force three signs at every coefficient to sum to zero.

For necessity, reduce (1) modulo x^g-1 over the rationals. The three
endpoint monomials are the same unit x^rho, so sum_i F_i is zero in that
quotient. Every residue sum of signs must be zero. Write n_i=q_i g+rho,
0<=rho<g, and Q=sum_i q_i. The unsigned populations are Q+3 in residues
0,...,rho-1 and Q in the others. If 0<rho<g, both parities occur, impossible
for zero signed sums. Thus rho=0 and Q must be even.

For sufficiency, divide the n_i by g. The resulting differences have gcd
one and their sum is even, so the graph proof supplies words. Replace
each sign by g copies. This preserves palindromicity, and replaces F_i by
`G_g(x) F_i(x^g)`, so (1) remains zero. This proves the exact boundary.
It agrees with the independent even-component graph criterion.

**This is an obstruction only to the cell identity (1).** A tripod can
have a homometric pair outside this method. For example, the method
rejects cells (91,71,16); that does not refute the independently constructed
size-88 pair on the corresponding prefix [T(90,70,15)](BEYOND_REFLECTIONS.md). Neither a graph
rejection nor a failed search in this run is a universal counterexample.
The general milestone and cases requiring other constructions remain open.

## A boundary requiring interior holes

The following infinite family shows where every full-prefix method stops:

```text
T(99+5320t,79+5320t,23+5320t), t>=0; target 100+7980t.
```

Here **no target-sized full prefix works, with any colouring**. This is
stronger than a cell-method rejection, but does not rule out interior holes.
The existing [root-residue lemma](BEYOND_REFLECTIONS.md#a-residue-obstruction-for-arbitrary-full-partitions)
says that for coprime nonzero residue r at modulus g, a full partition
requires r=g-1 and Q=sum floor(length_i/g) odd. For all possible prefixes:

| Removed tips | g | r | Q | Failure |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| none | 4 | 3 | 48+3990t | Q even |
| (2,0,0) | 2 | 1 | 98+7980t | Q even |
| (0,2,0) | 2 | 1 | 98+7980t | Q even |
| (0,0,2) | 2 | 1 | 98+7980t | Q even |
| (1,1,0) | 5 | 3 | 38+3192t | r differs from g-1 |
| (1,0,1) | 19 | 3 | 10+840t | r differs from g-1 |
| (0,1,1) | 7 | 1 | 28+2280t | r differs from g-1 |

The original order is even, so a pair at or above the target leaves either
zero or two vertices unused. The table exhausts those prefix shapes.
Thus every target-sized pair must include an interior omission. The first
row also proves the all-pairs upper bound equal to the target, not below it.
Interior omissions are therefore necessary. A construction is developed in
[FIXED_DIFFERENCE_FAMILY.md](FIXED_DIFFERENCE_FAMILY.md); the prefix
obstruction remains useful even after that positive result. The previously proved interior family with short
arm 127+56k does not cover this residue 23 modulo 56.
