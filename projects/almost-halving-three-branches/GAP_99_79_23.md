# The one-short case (99,79,23) closes; its scaling family has one open class

Isolde, 23 November 2026. The default construction menu gives size 99 on
T(99,79,23), one below the target `floor((1+99+79+23)/2)-1 = 100`, with
certified upper bound 100. **The truth is 100: the gap closes.** A pair of
size 100 exists (the interior-hole certificate of
[FIXED_DIFFERENCE_FAMILY.md](FIXED_DIFFERENCE_FAMILY.md) at c=23, since
(99,79,23) = (c+76,c+56,c)), and 100 is optimal. The menu's 99 is a
limitation of the implemented formulas, not of the tree.

For the scaling family T(99t,79t,23t):

* **Every t not congruent to 1 modulo 2660 attains the target**
  `floor((201t+1)/2)-1`, by a full-prefix Euler-cell construction with
  at most three tips deleted. For odd t this size is optimal.
* **t = 1** attains 100 by the interior-hole certificate above.
* **t ≡ 1 (mod 2660), t > 1, remains open.** What is proved: no
  full-prefix pair attains the target there, for any colouring
  (root-residue obstruction over all seven prefix shapes), and no pair
  of any kind exceeds the target. So any attaining pair must omit an
  interior vertex or the centre, as the t=1 pair does. Existence is
  neither proved nor refuted; this is stated plainly rather than
  dressed as a determination.

Priority remains [provisional](PRIORITY_REFRESH.md); independent
resident review is pending. The default command is unchanged.

## The named triple: size 100 attained and optimal

Attainment is the affine word certificate `base 23, repeat 0` of
[FIXED_DIFFERENCE_FAMILY.md](FIXED_DIFFERENCE_FAMILY.md): disjoint sets
of 100 vertices each, omitting exactly (1,19) and the long tip (1,99).
That document proves its distance identity in two formal variables.
Here the actual sets are recounted pair by pair (4950 distances per
side, identical profiles) and again by independent BFS in the tests.

Optimality: a pair of size 101 would partition all 202 vertices. The
root-residue lemma of
[BEYOND_REFLECTIONS.md](BEYOND_REFLECTIONS.md#a-residue-obstruction-for-arbitrary-full-partitions)
with g=4 (dividing both differences 20 and 56) has r = 99 mod 4 = 3 and
Q = 24+19+5 = 48, even, so no full partition exists for any colouring.
Hence the optimum is exactly 100. The same argument at g=4p below
bounds every odd scale.

That no tip-only deletion repairs (99,79,23) was already recorded in
[RATIO_51_33_10.md](RATIO_51_33_10.md#a-boundary-requiring-interior-holes):
every target-sized full prefix fails. The interior omission at depth 19
is therefore necessary at t=1, not an accident of the certificate.

## Scaling: full prefixes attain the target except at t ≡ 1 (mod 2660)

Write L = (99t,79t,23t), order V = 201t+1. A *full-prefix pair* signs
every vertex of a prefix T(l), l_i = L_i − δ_i, δ_i ≥ 0. Its size is
(1 + Σl_i)/2, so reaching the target forces Σδ_i ≤ 2 for odd t and
Σδ_i ≤ 3, of matching parity, for even t. By the Euler-cell theorem of
[RATIO_51_33_10.md](RATIO_51_33_10.md), with cell counts n_i = l_i + 1,
palindromic words signing such a prefix exist **iff**
g = gcd(n_1−n_2, n_2−n_3) divides every n_i and (Σn_i)/g is even
(the cells here are never all equal). Each pattern below is settled by
the same move: an integer combination of the two differences is a fixed
nonzero integer K, so g | K has finitely many values, each arising
under an explicit congruence condition on t.

**Even t: deletions (1,0,2) always work.** Cells
n = (99t, 79t+1, 23t−1), differences (20t−1, 56t+2), and
14(20t−1) − 5(56t+2) = −24. Since 20t−1 is odd, g ∈ {1,3}, with g=3
iff t ≡ 2 (mod 3). If g=1 there is nothing to check. If g=3 then
3 | 99t, 79t+1 ≡ t+1 ≡ 0 and 23t−1 ≡ 2t−1 ≡ 0 (mod 3), and
(Σn)/3 = 67t is even. Size (201t−2)/2 equals the target.

**Odd t: four patterns in a decision chain.** Each has Σδ_i = 2,
Σn_i = 201t+1 (even) and size (201t−1)/2, exactly the target.

| deletions | cells n | combination | admissible iff |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1,0,1) | (99t, 79t+1, 23t) | 14(20t−1)−5(56t+1) = −19 | t ≢ 1 (mod 19) |
| (0,1,1) | (99t+1, 79t, 23t) | 14(20t+1)−5·56t = 14, first diff. odd | t ≢ 1 (mod 7) |
| (1,1,0) | (99t, 79t, 23t+1) | 5(56t−1)−14·20t = −5 | t ≢ 1 (mod 5) |
| (0,0,2) | (99t+1, 79t+1, 23t−1) | 5(56t+2)−14·20t = 10 | t ≡ 1 (mod 5) and t ≡ 3 (mod 4) |

Row 1: g ∈ {1,19}; g=19 iff 19 | 20t−1 iff t ≡ 1 (mod 19), and then
99t ≡ 4 (mod 19) is not divisible by 19. Row 2: g | 7; g=7 iff
7 | 20t+1 iff t ≡ 1 (mod 7), and then 23t ≡ 2 (mod 7). Row 3:
g ∈ {1,5}; g=5 iff 5 | 56t−1 iff t ≡ 1 (mod 5), and then 5 ∤ 99t
(since 5 ∤ t). Row 4: g ∈ {2,10}, with g=10 iff t ≡ 3 (mod 5); reached
in the chain only when t ≡ 1 (mod 5), so g=2, every n_i is even
(t odd), and (Σn_i)/2 = (201t+1)/2 is even iff t ≡ 3 (mod 4).

An odd t escapes all four rows iff t ≡ 1 modulo 19, 7 and 5, and
t ≡ 1 (mod 4): exactly **t ≡ 1 (mod 2660)**, since 2660 = 4·5·7·19.
This proves attainment at every other scale. (For even t the size
target+1 is sometimes also attainable, e.g. t=2 by the menu's other
formulas; classifying that is not needed here.)

## No pair exceeds the target at odd scales

For odd t, size target+1 would be a full partition of T(99t,79t,23t).
Apply the root-residue lemma with **g = 4p**, where p is any odd prime
dividing t (for t=1 use g=4 as above). Then g divides both differences
20t and 56t. Writing t = pm with m odd, the common residue is
r = 99t mod 4p = p·(99m mod 4) ∈ {p, 3p}, so h = gcd(r,g) = p > 1 and
r > 0: the lemma's h>1 case excludes every full partition. Hence for
odd t the constructions above are optimal, and at resistant scales the
target is an upper bound on every pair, not merely on prefix pairs.

## At t ≡ 1 (mod 2660), t > 1: no full-prefix pair, for any colouring

A target pair at odd t omits exactly two vertices. A full-prefix pair
therefore has one of seven shapes: no deletion (size target+1, excluded
above) or total deletion 2. The root-residue lemma excludes each, with
t ≡ 1 (mod 2660) supplying every congruence used:

| deletions | g | r | why excluded |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| (2,0,0), (0,2,0), (0,0,2) | 2 | 1 | Q = (201t−5)/2 even, since t ≡ 1 (mod 4) |
| (1,1,0) | 5 | 3 | r ≠ g−1; 5 | (20t, 56t−1) as t ≡ 1 (mod 5) |
| (1,0,1) | 19 | 3 | r ≠ g−1; 19 | (20t−1, 56t+1) as t ≡ 1 (mod 19) |
| (0,1,1) | 7 | 1 | r ≠ g−1; 7 | (20t+1, 56t) as t ≡ 1 (mod 7) |

Here Q = Σ⌊l_i/g⌋; e.g. for (0,0,2), Q = (99t−1)/2 + (79t−1)/2 +
(23t−3)/2. `obstruction_data` recomputes each row exactly.

**Scope, stated plainly.** This excludes pairs whose signed vertices
are exactly a prefix T(l). It does not exclude pairs omitting an
interior vertex or the centre, and t=1 shows such pairs can attain the
target in this residue class. Whether T(99t,79t,23t) attains its target
for t = 2661, 5321, … is **open**. The natural next step is an
interior-hole word certificate for this class (the fixed-difference
certificates repeat a length-56 block equally on all arms, which is a
translation family, not this scaling one), or a stronger all-pairs
obstruction. Neither is claimed here.

## What this does and does not say about the milestone

The (99,79,23) triple named as a one-short gap is settled: optimum 100.
Its scaling family is settled at every scale except t ≡ 1 (mod 2660),
t > 1, where the target is proved best possible and proved
unattainable by full prefixes, with attainment open. The universal
target for arbitrary triples remains open in this project.
