# Fresh larger triples: where exact parity completion helps, and where it runs out

Tansyfern, November 2026. Task `tansyfern-fresh-triples-sweep-20261124`.
A scaling check on [exact_completion.py](EXACT_COMPLETION_TOOL.md),
not a census and not a settledness verdict for any row.

Eighteen off-grid triples with arm lengths from 60 to 100 (plus five
odd-length triples between 61 and 97) were run in this session with

```sh
timeout 15 python3 exact_completion.py A B C
```

(default support order; tool BFS-verifies any witness). The menu
column is `construct((a,b,c), "best")` recounted by `check_pair`.
"No witness on tried supports" means every tip/pool support returned
exactly obstructed for that support or inconclusive at the free-bit
caps; it excludes nothing about other supports, and several such rows
are settled by the menu anyway. Times out are inconclusive.

Reading the table: the tool found BFS-verified witnesses on the tip
support for 5 of 18, including two genuine menu shortfalls —
`(61,43,29)` (menu 65, target 66) and `(73,41,19)` (menu 65, target
66) — which those two commands therefore settle finitely. One timed
out. The other twelve found no witness on the tip/pool supports
within the caps; five of them are menu shortfalls by one
(`(70,60,40)`, `(100,80,50)`, `(71,57,39)`, `(97,73,53)`) or four
(`(80,70,50)`) and remain open on this evidence. So beyond the coarse
grid's scale the tip-support trick is a sometimes-cheap first exact
step, not a general method: its successes stay exact, its failures
prove nothing, and the next structural step — broader supports,
smarter exact completion, or the general construction — is still
needed. Witness payloads are regenerated by the commands above with
`--out`; none are stored for this sweep.
