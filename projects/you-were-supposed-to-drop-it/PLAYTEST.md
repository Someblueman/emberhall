# Two takes with the same cast

Linnet, 18 November 2026. I own the project. Rook wrote its brief and Yarrow
built the act. I tried the stage from accepted source `344b903`.

On Stage I, Pip, Bess and Gus started on marks 1, 2 and 3. I put Dot on mark 4.
I sent these two sets of directions to the house viewer with the curtain closed.

| Take | Direction | What I saw on both devices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Miss | Pip to Gus, hold a beat, Gus to Dot | Dot catches on Stage I. On Stage II the bowler lands on the drummer. |
| Clean | Pip to Bess, hold, Bess to Pip, Pip to Dot, Dot to Bess | Bess finishes holding the bowler on both stages. |

I replayed the missed catch, stepped back one still and advanced to the drop
again. Behind the curtain, the pair table showed five matching wires. Only
Gus and Dot had a wire on one stage and a gap on the other.

The rival placed Pip, Bess, Gus and Dot on marks 1, 2, 6 and 13. Stage I marks
3 and 4 share a row. On Stage II the move from 6 to 13 has displacement
(2, 3) modulo four, outside its six allowed shifts. Choosing Gus as the sender
and Dot as the receiver picked the missing wire.

I independently counted wires among every four-mark subset from the coordinate
rules, using each unordered pair once. Of 1,820 sets per rig, Stage I had
eight with six wires and zero with five. Stage II had zero with six and 48
with five.

**Keep the act.** I'd replay the pause before Gus throws to Dot. Both
drummers have their sticks raised. One still has them up a beat later, while
the other's eyes have disappeared under the bowler. Getting Bess through the whole
second take without putting either drummer out of work made the first one
feel like a joke I'd directed.

On the 1,280-pixel desktop the two pits sit side by side. On the 390-pixel
phone the rival drummer appears below Dot's clean catch. Both takes fit the
screen width and completed without script errors. This is the two-stage act
its title promises.
