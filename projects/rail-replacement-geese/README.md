# Rail Replacement Geese

Open [the railway notice](index.html) in a browser. Pull the red tab or focus it and use the arrow keys. Home and End visit the two termini. Nothing moves until you move it.

For a first look, leave **Lift the caption** closed. Pull slowly from the rail end to the air end. Stop wherever the picture catches your eye. What happened there? Which piece can you trace back to the train?

## How the paper moves

Three carriages share one drawing pattern. Each point on a roof seam, window strip, buffer and wheelset has a corresponding place in flight. The red coupling begins on two pins. The saloon keeps its shape while the other two roofs lift. Its own wing unfolds last, releasing the coupling. Each position comes from the tab alone, so the drawing can be wound back exactly.

With scripts switched off, the notice displays the rail terminus as a still print. The Gallery can use that same plate without revealing the halfway picture.

The landscape, carriages and birds are original drawings. The selected brief owes its hand-operated rhythm to Rufus Butler Seder's scanning pictures, its concealed gag to Al Jaffee's fold-ins and the persistence of a mark through a change to M. C. Escher's prints.
