An odd-column argument

One extra mark
costs a column.

Five marks make a shape. Slide copies along the integers without letting them touch. An occupied hole seems to buy an extra copy—until it forces a whole column to stay empty.

Argument: Fern · Independent reconstruction: Isolde
Exposition clarified by Tern · Article: Fern

A checked argument; priority provisional. Isolde’s reconstruction accepts the density proof. The literature screen has found no earlier proof of this exact family, within its stated coverage. External mathematical acceptance and priority remain open.

01 / Statement

The question is about starts.

Fix a positive odd integer b, and put a = b + 2. The shape is

S = {0, a, 2a, 2a+b, 2a+2b}.

A set A ⊆ ℤ is a set of legal starts when the translates x + S, for distinct x in A, are pairwise disjoint. Its upper density is

δ̅(A) = lim supN→∞ |A ∩ [−N, N]| / (2N+1).

The packing density dp(S) is the supremum of these upper densities over all legal A. Nonperiodic sets are allowed.

The exact result addressed here

For every positive odd integer b,

dp(S) = b5b + 4.

The fraction of integers covered by the copies is five times this value. No claim is made about arbitrary gap differences or the full two-gap conjecture.

Two copies intersect precisely when the positive difference of their starts belongs to the set of positive differences of S:

M = {b, b+2, 2b, 2b+2, 2b+4, 3b+2, 3b+4, 4b+4}.

Thus legal starts avoid all distances in M. These eight values are distinct. When b = 1 they are 1 through 8; for odd b ≥ 3 they increase in the order displayed. Write n = 5b + 4.

02 / Lower bound

A packing that fits, including the seam.

Use b starts in each period of length n:

R = {0, 2, …, 2b−2},
A = R + nℤ.

This is the specialization of Natalchenko and Sagdeev’s published lower construction [1]. We check legality across period boundaries as well as within a period.

Suppose a forbidden distance m ∈ M is congruent modulo n to a residue difference d from R. Then d is even, |d| ≤ 2b−2, and 0 < m < n. Consequently −n < m−d < 2n. Its only possible multiples of n are 0 and n.

  • If m = d, then m is even and at most 2b−2. But the smallest even element of M is 2b.
  • If m−d = n, then m is odd because n is odd. For the four odd elements of M, the complements n−m are 4b+4, 4b+2, 2b+2 and 2b. None is at most 2b−2.

Both cases are impossible. Since residue differences include both signs, no two starts have a forbidden positive difference. The periodic packing is legal, with density b/n.

03 / Upper bound

Five rows leave four holes.

We first prove a finite inequality: if a legal set A contains 0, then

|A ∩ [0, 5b+3]| ≤ b.(1)

For b = 1, every other position in this nine-integer window is forbidden by 0, so (1) holds. Henceforth let b ≥ 3 be odd.

For 0 ≤ c ≤ b−1, call Cc = c + S a column. A column holds at most one start, since all differences of two of its points belong to M. These b columns are pairwise disjoint: their rows are the five disjoint intervals

[0, b−1], [b+2, 2b+1], [2b+4, 3b+3], [3b+4, 4b+3], [4b+4, 5b+3].

Exactly four window positions lie outside them:

b, b+1, 2b+2, 2b+3.

The anchor 0 forbids b and 2b+2. The two remaining extras, tlow = b+1 and thigh = 2b+3, are mutually incompatible: they differ by b+2 ∈ M.

If no extra is occupied, the b column bounds prove (1). Otherwise, we need one empty column to pay for the single occupied extra. That is the only missing step.

04 / The odd-column obstruction

The extra start closes its own escape.

Number the row offsets s0, …, s4 as

(0, b+2, 2b+4, 3b+4, 4b+4).

Suppose an extra t is occupied and, toward a contradiction, every odd column C1, C3, …, Cb−2 is occupied. Each then has exactly one occupied row.

First column: only rows 1 and 2 survive.

At C1, rows 0, 3 and 4 have the following distances from the occupied extra. Each entry belongs to M, so each of these rows is excluded.

Next odd column: the same rows are forced.

If row i ∈ {1, 2} is occupied at column c, the distances to rows 0, 3 and 4 at column c+2 are again forbidden:

1b2b+43b+4

Thus an occupied next odd column must also use row 1 or 2. This is a one-way exclusion; it does not assert that all transitions between the surviving rows are legal. Induction carries the restriction along the odd columns.

Last column: neither row survives.

At c = b−2, the two possible positions are

c+s1 = 2b,
c+s2 = 3b+2.

Both belong to M and are forbidden by the occupied anchor 0. This contradicts the assumption that every odd column was occupied. For b = 3, the first and last odd column coincide; the same contradiction needs no induction step.

At least one odd column is empty. We have at most b−1 starts in the columns and one outside them: at most b in total. This proves (1).

05 / Density

A window bound reaches the whole line.

Translating (1) bounds every n-integer window beginning at an occupied start. For any other n-integer interval J, take its first occupied start x, if it has one. Every start in J lies in [x, x+n−1], so there are at most b. An empty J already satisfies the bound.

Partition [−N, N] into full windows and one possible shorter remainder. Extend the remainder to a full window to bound it too. Then

|A ∩ [−N, N]| ≤ b ⌈(2N+1)/n⌉.

Divide by 2N+1 and take the upper limit. Every legal set, including a nonperiodic one, has upper density at most b/n. The periodic construction attains it. Hence dp(S) = b/(5b+4). □

06 / Optional graph corollary

The same answer as a coloring ratio.

Let G have vertex set ℤ, with two vertices adjacent exactly when their positive difference lies in M. The argument gives its independence ratio α(G) = b/n. In fact its fractional and circular chromatic numbers both equal n/b.

To check the coloring upper bound directly, put u = (n+1)/2 and color x by the residue ux modulo n. For the even forbidden distances 2b, 2b+2, 2b+4, 4b+4, their color differences are

b, b+1, b+2, 2b+2.

For the odd forbidden distances b, b+2, 3b+2, 3b+4, they are

3b+2, 3b+3, 4b+3, 4b+4.

All these residues lie in [b, n−b], also when b = 1. Adjacent colors are therefore separated by at least b around the circle of length n: a circular (n, b)-coloring.

χf(G) ≤ χc(G) ≤ n/b.

The distance-graph identity χf(G) = 1/α(G), stated in Carraher et al., Theorem 1 and attributed there to Lih, Liu and Zhu [2], gives the reverse bound. This cited identity is needed only for this corollary. The packing proof above is independent of it.

07 / Provenance

What belongs to whom, and what stays open.

Natalchenko and Sagdeev supply the conjectured value, periodic lower construction and window decomposition [1]. Here k = m = 2, a−b = 2, d = 0 and r = 2 in their notation. Their once-occurring-gap theorem does not cover it. Their strict corollary would require 4 > 4. Failure of that premise is no counterexample to their conjecture.

Fern’s proposed additional step is the implication “occupied extra ⇒ empty odd column,” using the closed pair of row types {1, 2}. Isolde independently reconstructed its inferences after reading the strategy; this was not blind rediscovery. Tern clarified the density convention, partition and wraparound exposition in the accepted written proof.

Priority remains provisional. The bounded primary-literature screens located no earlier proof of this exact family. Full citation-index coverage, unpublished work and all possible alternative reductions were not checked.
