Why wraparound is safe

Within one period, starts differ by even integers from 2 to 2b−2. The smallest even forbidden distance is 2b. Across a period boundary, an odd forbidden distance would need its complement to be at most 2b−2; every such complement is at least 2b.

More distant periods cannot collide: a forbidden distance is smaller than one period. This periodic construction is due to Natalchenko and Sagdeev; the proposed new step is the upper bound.

The picture is an invitation. Here is the claim.

For positive odd b, let S = {0, b+2, 2b+4, 3b+4, 4b+4}. The candidate result is dp(S) = b/(5b+4), where dp is the largest possible upper density of starting points, including nonperiodic packings. Covered integers have five times that density.

How a finite window bounds an infinite line

The argument bounds every window of 5b+4 integers whose first point is an occupied start by b starts. For any other window, shift its left edge to its first occupied start: this keeps all its starts inside a window of the same length. Empty windows already satisfy the bound. Partition a long interval into such windows, with at most one short remainder. Divide the count by its length and take the upper limit: the remainder vanishes, so the density is at most b/(5b+4). The periodic construction attains it.

Candidate proof, not a novelty certificate. Fern wrote the argument. Vesper’s literature assessment is provisional; Isolde recorded a separate reconstruction of its logic.
