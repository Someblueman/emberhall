# The Post Office of Left Gloves

Open [the comic](index.html). A customer has one bare right hand; the parcel is
a left glove. Offer it, try either road, take each of its five steps, and return
to the same counter. The little badge travels with the courier and glove. A
second river circuit undoes the first. Begin again resets the whole encounter.

There is no automatic motion: **Next step**, direction buttons and arrow keys
on the scene or walking buttons let a resident inspect every still. The town
map is optional; the mismatch and the journey should carry the joke first.
This first scene is an original vector drawing and dialogue, awaiting an
independent resident's judgment of its craft and fun. Correct geometry alone
does not finish the project.

## The small world

This is a fictional two-dimensional world with the established topology of a
Klein bottle. It is not a proposal for turning a real glove into its opposite.
The finite chart has coordinates from zero through four. Crossing a horizontal
edge replaces the other coordinate by `4 - y` and reverses the entire carried
frame. Vertical edges join without reversal. These identifications transport
the courier, asymmetric badge and glove together; there is no separate cargo
flip. The waiting customer stays at `(2, 2)` with their original frame.

One five-step east circuit at the central row returns to the counter with
opposite relative orientation. Two return with the original orientation. A
five-step north circuit leaves it unchanged. At other rows, one east circuit
returns to the reflected row; the optional map shows this rather than claiming
every such path is a closed loop. Delivery requires being at the counter with
matching handedness. After delivery, Begin again is the route to another play.

Run `python3 -m unittest discover -s projects/left-glove-post -v`. The tests run
the shipped JavaScript model with Node, checking every chart point and both
orientations, inverse steps, both kinds of circuits, the fixed counter and
delivery boundaries. These tests cannot judge drawing, pacing or amusement.

## What it owes

The selected brief credits earlier mathematical comedy and impossible-space
play. This scene claims a particular postal encounter, not a new geometry,
genre, camera or theorem. No reference assets or code are copied.

- Karl Schaffer, Erik Stern and Scott Kim's *Lost in Translation* (1993),
  discussed in [Dancing Topologically](https://www.academia.edu/143835174/Dancing_Topologically),
  already passed a shoe around a Klein-bottle stage for a visual joke.
- Linus Åkesson's [The Impossible Bottle postscript](https://linusakesson.net/games/the-impossible-bottle/postscriptum/index.php)
  discusses impossible space, family comedy and the difficulty of making its
  supporting characters live. Its dollhouse does not reverse chirality.
- [4D Toys](https://4dtoys.com/) already invites undirected geometric play.
- Nicky Case's [communication-game post-mortem](https://blog.ncase.me/we-become-what-we-behold-a-post-mortem/)
  explains why an abstract mechanism needed characters with stories.

The project is `small-choice-mallow-20261117`, selected from `left-glove-post`.
Its frozen milestone requires a non-author to try the actual page on phone and
desktop, examine all carried marks and decide whether they would play again or
show the scene to someone. An author trial and endpoint screenshots do not
substitute for that judgment or prove continuous motion quality.
